# Step 4 — Critique Pass 3: MMO Fit, Exploits, Salvage Verdicts

Third and final critical iteration over all 500 ideas, building on passes 1 (TEACH) and 2 (FUN). Systems/live-ops lens: does the multiplayer work at 10,000 AND 400 players; the nastiest realistic exploit and whether it kills or IS the lesson; verdict KEEP (viable whole concept) / MINE (steal the named mechanic) / KILL; TOTAL = TEACH+FUN+MMO out of 30.

### 1. Reply-All Ghosts — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 16/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: The email sim is single-player at any population — the lead-list gray market is the only real multiplayer surface, so 10,000 players is just 10,000 parallel voids with a bazaar bolted on.
Exploit: Sellers seed lists with alt accounts that open everything, laundering stale inventory into "fresh" ratings — a genuine lemon-market lesson, except bot-scale mass sends also flood every real recipient inbox until nobody reads anything, killing the receiving side outright.
Steal: player-to-player lemon market in intel goods whose freshness is unverifiable at time of purchase.

### 2. The Dashboard Lies — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 13/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Multiplayer is one shared benchmark PDF — pure decoration on a solo statistics exercise that plays identically at 400 and 10,000 because no other player ever touches your company.
Exploit: Discord-coordinated benchmark-inflation cartels — which accidentally IS the lesson about trusting self-reported industry data, but since the benchmark is the game's only social organ, poisoning it leaves no game behind.
Steal: player-submitted industry benchmarks that are systematically inflated — trust-calibration against self-reported peer metrics.

### 3. Ghost Round — MMO: 7/10 | TOTAL: 23/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: A classic two-population balance problem — tunable at 10,000 but at 400 the VC side thins to a clique and pitching becomes theater for an empty room, so the everyone-runs-both-a-founder-and-a-checkbook fix is load-bearing; with it, the social layer genuinely is the engine.
Exploit: Alt-pair wash-funding (your VC alt funds your founder main to farm both reputations) plus Discord quid-pro-quo funding rings — the rings ARE real VC syndicate behavior, but wash-funding needs hard capital scarcity or the reputation economy hollows out.
Steal: ghosting as the emergent optimal play of a real counterparty population, never a script.

### 4. Runway — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 16/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: A solo roguelike wearing an MMO pin — the aggregate meta-drift needs population mass to move at all (at 400 the meta barely shifts), and deck progression is exactly the collectible-grind the brief forbids.
Exploit: Meta-tracker Discords centralize into one oracle everyone reads, homogenizing pitches and accelerating drift — half of which IS the lesson (everyone pitching the same thing dies) while reducing actual play to tracker-checking.
Steal: playerbase-aggregate thesis drift — last cycle's winning pattern becomes this cycle's automatic pass.

### 5. Cap Table — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 15/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: A 1v1 ranked ladder is matchmaking, not an MMO — no persistent world, no economy, and the social layer is entirely decoration at any population.
Exploit: Win-trading plus published opening books turn the ladder into memorized clause theory within one season, killing the bluff-reading the teach depends on — the exploit kills the lesson.
Steal: post-deal reveal of the counterparty's hidden constraints — the debrief showing the squeeze or the pass was never about you.

### 6. Churnyard — MMO: 5/10 | TOTAL: 17/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: A parasite design — it only works if a host economy of real player businesses already needs tools, and at 400 players any one SaaS has a customer base of dozens, making cohort curves unreadable lumps; at 10,000 it works, but as a layer inside a bigger game, never standalone.
Exploit: Rival vendors subscribe to competitors purely to mass-cancel in coordinated waves, weaponizing churn dread and poisoning cohort data — semi-realistic (review-bombing exists) but it drowns the honest signal the whole teach depends on.
Steal: real-player dependence as customer base — churn arrives from humans who actually relied on your product.

### 7. API Wars — MMO: 7/10 | TOTAL: 21/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: The dependency graph is a genuine player-made economy where every seat is both producer and consumer, but at 400 players the graph is shallow and one whale IS the market, and mature servers ossify into incumbent politics that need seasonal protocol resets to stay enterable.
Exploit: Integrator-guild cartels fixing standards and extorting platform rent, plus alts wash-integrating your own API to fake usage traction — both are real platform economics (the exploit IS the lesson), though wash-usage needs cost friction or fake traction becomes free.
Steal: breaking-change trust economics — your reputation is denominated in other players' uptime.

### 8. Cold Start — MMO: 5/10 | TOTAL: 18/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: The city sim carries the game solo; rival-operator PvP is the only multiplayer and works fine at small counts, but 10,000 players demands sharded cities, and sharding fragments the exact rivalry that is the social layer.
Exploit: Discord non-aggression pacts — rival marketplaces carve up neighborhoods cartel-style instead of competing, which is precisely what real duopolies do (the exploit IS the lesson) but it flattens the poaching pressure that keeps the sim honest.
Steal: learning-by-burning — every elasticity is knowable only at a cash price.

### 9. Standup — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 17/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Async daily cadence scales to any population and respects real lives, but a shared-seed market means early-timezone players publish the day's answer key by lunch — structural timezone unfairness that per-player seeds fix only by killing the shared-world hook.
Exploit: The answer-key Discord — collective solving of the daily hidden market reduces every downstream player to copying, and unlike cartels this exploit is not the lesson, it is a spoiler that deletes attribution fog entirely.
Steal: identical-seed ghost companies — friends face the same hidden market so luck and skill visibly separate.

### 10. Post-Mortem — MMO: 7/10 | TOTAL: 23/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: The codex is a server-scale epistemic commons that gives mature-server newcomers the best cold start in the batch — inherit the wiki, then learn the hard way that the wiki rots — and it tunes at both 400 (sparse codex, fast drift) and 10,000 (dense codex, freeloader-heavy).
Exploit: Private-codex cartels — guilds re-validate entries in secret, trade on freshness, and sell stale "mastermind" advice to newbies while the public codex rots — which is exactly real startup-advice grifting, so the exploit IS the lesson.
Steal: knowledge decay — verified community wisdom silently rots as the hidden economy drifts.

### 11. The Feed — MMO: 6/10 | TOTAL: 21/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: A recommender retrained on aggregate playerbase behavior is inherently population-scale reflexivity — glorious at 10,000, but at 400 a single coordinated guild can steer the retraining and effectively own the store.
Exploit: Deliberate data-poisoning to bend the model toward a cartel's apps — fatal capture at low population, but at high population it decays into just another self-destroying hack, where the exploit genuinely IS the reflexivity lesson.
Steal: self-destroying growth hacks — every discovered edge dies by mass adoption.

### 12. Voicemail — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 17/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Voice-only real-time solo calls exclude commuters, parents, and anyone with roommates, and cost a fortune in concurrent LLM inference at 10,000 players — the shared org-chart map is the lone multiplayer organ and it is decoration.
Exploit: Traded jailbreak phrasebooks that talk past any gatekeeper turn sales craft into prompt-injection speedrunning — the exploit kills the teach because real receptionists do not have system prompts.
Steal: shared intel map that is part wiki, part rumor mill — community knowledge with unverifiable freshness.

### 13. Burn — MMO: 2/10 | TOTAL: 14/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Two-player co-op horror has no MMO surface at all — no market, no population effects, and nothing changes between 400 and 10,000 because no third player ever enters your run.
Exploit: The dominant grief is your own cofounder draining the shared account or ragequitting mid-payroll — on-theme for cofounder risk, but with matchmade strangers it is just a griefing lottery with no reputation backstop.
Steal: split financial authority — two players share one bank account and neither can act alone.

### 14. Term Sheet Poker — MMO: 2/10 | TOTAL: 13/30 | VERDICT: KILL
Structure: A four-assembled-friends voice party game is the structural opposite of an MMO, and with matchmade strangers the trust drama that carries it evaporates.
Exploit: Partners colluding over out-of-game voice to signal hidden hands — trivial, undetectable, fatal in any stakes lobby, and it teaches nothing.
Steal: nothing — poker with startup-flavored cards; its one insight (liquidation preference as belief tell) travels with Cap Table's post-deal reveal mechanic.

### 15. Fake Door — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 16/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Two player classes with only one specified game — the builder seat is an empty chair, and at 400 players the concept exchange is too illiquid to price anything.
Exploit: Signal-pumping — alts drive fake conversions on your own landing pages to inflate a concept before dumping it on the exchange — which IS the lemons-market lesson if diligence tools exist and pure fraud-victory if they do not.
Steal: validated demand as a tradeable, perishable commodity — proof of demand that decays between measurement and sale.

### 16. Null Result — MMO: 2/10 | TOTAL: 10/30 | VERDICT: KILL
Structure: A single-player puzzle set with a community answer wiki — no MMO surface, and the pitch celebrates its own route-publication death spiral.
Exploit: Day-one route wikis reduce 90% of players to executing known solutions — the exploit is the entire game dissolving on contact with its community.
Steal: nothing — its core (experiment-design inference) is delivered fresher by Post-Mortem's drifting economy without the solved-puzzle decay.

### 17. Warm Intro — MMO: 6/10 | TOTAL: 20/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: The player-built graph IS the engine — the most genuinely social design in the batch — but it ossifies into a dozen oligarchs by month three, and the by-design newcomer lockout becomes a churn machine on any mature server that only a permanent live-ops subsidy treadmill can offset.
Exploit: RMT for warm intros and alt-chain reputation laundering — paid intro brokers uncomfortably ARE real and despised, but RMT drains the staked reputation of all meaning, so the exploit kills the mechanism carrying the lesson.
Steal: staked-reputation introductions — the introducer's standing burns if you flame out in the meeting.

### 18. Ticket Queue — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 16/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: A solo inference game whose only multiplayer surface is rival-planted sabotage tickets — at 400 players there are not enough motivated rivals to keep the paranoia real, so the PvP layer decays into scripted noise.
Exploit: Cartel flooding of a target's queue with coordinated plausible decoys — cheap, undetectable, and it overshoots the lesson into pure unplayable noise.
Steal: adversarial signal injection — rivals can plant fake customer signals in your information feed.

### 19. Soft Landing — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 16/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Corp-dev counterparties must be players with hidden mandates, but nobody's main character is "acquirer" — the buy side is a part-time role, so deal flow starves at 400 players and the weeks-long dead-air pacing guillotines retention even at 10,000.
Exploit: Buyer cartels agreeing in Discord to never bid against each other and split distressed companies at floor prices — which is EXACTLY real distressed M&A, so the exploit IS the lesson, brutal as it plays.
Steal: parallel exit tracks where silence on one track becomes readable strategic signal instead of dead air.

### 20. Replumb — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 17/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: A solo Factorio machine with blueprint-sharing as its only multiplayer — decoration, and the game plays identically alone at any population.
Exploit: Blueprint-meta sites publishing the current-quarter optimal machine within days — but the built-in demand drift blunts it, the rare case where the wiki defeats itself and stale blueprints ARE the lesson.
Steal: measurement as buildable infrastructure — better gauges, tracer dyes, and A/B valves are subsystems you construct, not menu purchases.

### 21. Cofounder — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 18/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Matched pairs over real weeks is a matchmaking pool, not an MMO — no world, no economy, the modal failure (partner vanishes) strands a player alone in a two-seat game, and the lifestyle ask is brutal at any population.
Exploit: Serial-defector alts farming the solo-acquisition betrayal payout on fresh accounts — without hard identity persistence the reputation ledger resets freely and the trust lesson inverts into "always defect."
Steal: stranger equity-split with vesting — the contract you must write before trust is knowable.

### 22. Compliance — MMO: 5/10 | TOTAL: 17/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: The reverse-engineering forum IS the social engine and needs a critical mass of filers — thin at 400 — while ninety real days of silence on your own filing is retention suicide the compressed-clock fix only partially patches.
Exploit: The snitch button as griefing meta — coordinated false-flag reporting buries rivals in regulator scrutiny, which real fintech competitors genuinely do, so the exploit IS the lesson provided false reports carry a cost.
Steal: the share-or-snitch dilemma — hard-won regulatory intel is equally valuable published to allies or weaponized against a rival.

### 23. Out of Office — MMO: 2/10 | TOTAL: 10/30 | VERDICT: KILL
Structure: A push-notification obligation is a lifestyle demand, not an MMO — friends'-crises pings are decoration on an anxiety subscription that punishes having dinner and gets uninstalled by week two at any population.
Exploit: Scripted notification-bots auto-answering timed windows optimally — trivial to build, and once replies are botted the entire game is a cron job.
Steal: nothing — the 11pm-investor-reply ambience is a coat of paint any design in the pool can wear.

### 24. Board Deck — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 12/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: One authored beat per month makes players spectators to their own company 29 days in 30 — no live game survives that cadence — and investor-player board seats are an asymmetric-population role with no game of their own between meetings.
Exploit: Meter archaeology — the community reverse-engineers the narrative-debt math and publishes optimal-deception calculators, converting the moral mechanic into spreadsheet-optimal lying; the exploit kills the lesson dead.
Steal: narrative debt — a compounding meter on the gap between reality and the story you told.

### 25. Fork Threat — MMO: 5/10 | TOTAL: 18/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Fork wars and contributor-poaching are real multiplayer with EVE-corp-theft energy, but contributor players need their own game for the role split to hold, and a 400-player server supports maybe a dozen viable repos before attention fragments.
Exploit: Fork-extortion rackets — guilds threatening a hostile fork unless paid protection — uncomfortably close to real OSS drama (the exploit half-IS the lesson), but at scale it converts every repo into a shakedown queue.
Steal: fork-and-defect — rivals can legally copy your product and court your people, with the exit visible only as a quietly bending graph.

### 26. Dead Corner — MMO: 6/10 | TOTAL: 20/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Traffic-from-real-errands is a genuine engine at 10,000 players but at 400 every corner reads dead — the till cannot distinguish a bad location from a dying server, so the core signal is population-confounded.
Exploit: A guild routes members' errands past a corner to pump its apparent footfall, then flips the lease to a mark — location pump-and-dump that is half the lesson (traffic data is manipulable) but at low population one cartel owns the entire ground truth.
Steal: ghost signage — a persistent, visible survivorship record of every operator who failed at a location, correcting survivorship bias at the exact point of capital commitment.

### 27. Form 27-B — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 15/30 | VERDICT: KILL
Structure: The whole game is hostage to clerk-counter staffing across timezones, and the applicant seat is a queue rather than a game — a role asymmetry the NPC-understudy fix already concedes.
Exploit: Clerk alts fast-tracking their main's permits and selling stamps for Discord-side RMT turns the maze into a pay-to-skip toll booth, and since bureaucracy is the only content, buying past it deletes the game.
Steal: nothing — Inspection Day (38) delivers human gatekeepers with private criteria inside an actual running business, without the staffing dependency.

### 28. Fade — MMO: 7/10 | TOTAL: 22/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: Players-as-each-other's-customers makes the social graph itself the retention engine with no NPC crutch, though cold-starting on a mature server means facing incumbents' locked-in regular networks and churn is the newcomer's only door.
Exploit: Coordinated silent no-show rings against rival shops cost nothing and are indistinguishable from organic churn, poisoning the exact experimental signal the game exists to teach — booking deposits are mandatory, not optional.
Steal: overheard partial feedback — customers gossiping about you in a channel you can read but not control, the missing valve between total silence and a review score.

### 29. Quarters — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 12/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: A solo maintenance loop with hired runners bolted on — the social layer is decoration, and the depreciation treadmill is precisely the attribute-grind progression the brief forbids.
Exploit: Runner-skimming is the one human system, and it degenerates into alt-runners skimming your own tills for consequence-free transfers — self-dealing theater, not employment risk.
Steal: physically consolidating scattered coins to make rent day — runway rendered as a tactile, non-negotiable ritual instead of a dashboard number.

### 30. The Landlord's Table — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 16/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: One poker seat per eleven waiting rooms is a fatal fun-distribution ratio, and at scale landlordship calcifies into the endgame position everyone grinds toward — positional rather than attribute grind, but grind all the same.
Exploit: Landlord alts fabricating phantom competing offers is undetectable by design since winning terms are never revealed — authentically how real estate works, but an unfalsifiable haze turns the tenant game into pure hazing.
Steal: silence as a legal move — proposals that can simply expire unanswered, the sharpest rejection-without-explanation primitive in the batch.

### 31. Footfall Futures — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 14/30 | VERDICT: KILL
Structure: A data market needs deal-flow liquidity that 400 players cannot supply, and the pitch's own Akerlof collapse means the rational equilibrium is a market nobody participates in.
Exploit: Broker rebranding plus alt-laundered track records makes fraud costless and reputation meaningless, which kills the trust-calibration lesson rather than embodying it.
Steal: nothing — Word on the Street (45) runs the same information-for-sale lesson with structural wiki-immunity and an actual game underneath it.

### 32. Ghosted Gym — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 13/30 | VERDICT: KILL
Structure: Simulated members make the multiplayer pure decoration — league chat around a solo dashboard — and a fixed hidden churn model is a communal regression problem with a solve date.
Exploit: League spreadsheet collectives regress the model and publish optimal settings; the celebrated theory-swapping community IS the degeneration engine.
Steal: nothing — Fade (28) teaches silent churn with real humans, which can never be regressed into a config file.

### 33. Bench Fee — MMO: 8/10 | TOTAL: 23/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: A two-sided async market where the adversarial arms race IS the content pipeline — both seats have a real game, no timezone demands, holds at 400 and 10,000 — the best pure structure in the batch wrapped around too narrow a curriculum for the final game.
Exploit: Breaker-diagnostician collusion rings quote-dumping easy boxes to allies to farm calibration stats and stump bounties — rating fraud that blind routing manages, and it echoes real bid-rigging closely enough to half-count as lesson.
Steal: quote-blind pricing on player-authored problems — commit to a price before you may look inside, with the adversarial submission economy as a self-renewing content pipeline.

### 34. Ninety Days — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 17/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: A single-run roguelike where other players are Discord meta-collaborators rather than customers or counterparties — no economy, no roles, no persistence; a community puzzle, not an MMO.
Exploit: Seed-solving cartels selling early-week findings turn the leaderboard into a paid answer key on a fixed weekly schedule — the game's own social loop is its spoiler.
Steal: personally-salted seeds under a shared community skeleton — folklore raises your odds but can never fully solve your run; the cleanest anti-wiki pattern in the pool.

### 35. Endcap — MMO: 7/10 | TOTAL: 21/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: Supplier and retailer are both real seats with persistent reputations, pitching is async so nobody is timezone-hostage, and newcomer retailers get cold-pitched on arrival — the rare design where onboarding is other players' self-interest.
Exploit: Vertical cartels shill-buying their own SKUs off allied shelves to fake demand before dumping product on outsiders — half slotting-fee corruption (real retail politics, so partly the lesson) and half signal-poisoning that needs velocity auditing.
Steal: living on both sides of cold outreach — you ignore pitches while being ignored, learning what makes outreach land by being the silence yourself.

### 36. Hold Music — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 13/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Real-time callbacks are lifestyle-hostile at any population, the which-numbers-are-human layer gets listed within days, and other players have no role beyond being occasionally dialed — opacity here is cartographic, so it maps once and dies.
Exploit: Number-list wikis plus auto-dialer macros reduce the entire game to a solved phone tree, deleting the trust-calibration layer that justified the interface.
Steal: voicemail-quality-gated callbacks — response rates that depend on what you actually said in the message, making outreach copy a learnable skill instead of a dice roll.

### 37. The Strip — MMO: 7/10 | TOTAL: 20/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Twelve-seat street shards are the correct population architecture — pods scale from 400 to 10,000 by adding streets and the vacant unit is a built-in newcomer onboarding ramp — but the fixed synergy formula hands the entire teach to a regression wiki.
Exploit: Bloc-voting cartels controlling vacancies across multiple streets become de facto zoning mafias — real merchant-association politics right up until it operates as a newcomer-exclusion machine.
Steal: the twelve-seat street shard with tenant-mix politics over vacancies — the pod structure that solves density, cold-start, and social stakes in one move.

### 38. Inspection Day — MMO: 8/10 | TOTAL: 22/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: Inspector is a genuine second career track — role asymmetry with both seats playable — audits are async so nothing is timezone-bound, and per-human private checklists mean the folklore economy works identically at 400 and 10,000.
Exploit: Inspector protection rackets — pass-for-RMT, or guild inspectors hammering rivals — are regulatory capture, which IS the lesson, but only survives if the appeals theater makes sustained corruption bleed standing faster than it earns.
Steal: gatekeepers as players with private, unpublishable criteria — opacity that regenerates per-human instead of eroding per-wiki.

### 39. Change of Hands — MMO: 5/10 | TOTAL: 16/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Rotation works mechanically at any population since it is intra-pool, but weekly dispossession means no seat ever owns consequences — the anti-stewardship incentive is structural, not tunable.
Exploit: Rotation rings cycling a shop among colluding alts, strip-mining on every handoff and leaving the wreck to strangers — pure griefing that inverts the stewardship lesson entirely.
Steal: inherited-ledger archaeology plus trailing equity — past operators keep a slice of future performance, so the shop's history has authors with skin in the game.

### 40. Till Tape — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 16/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: A single-player forensics puzzle in MMO clothing — staff seeded "from other players' styles" is data import, not multiplayer, and guild pattern-libraries are the only (and degenerative) social loop.
Exploit: Shared anomaly-signature libraries convert every tape into a lookup table — cooperative wiki-building is the natural meta and the fatal one.
Steal: numbers-only perception — experiencing a business exclusively through its financial exhaust, a fog-of-war layer worth grafting onto any richer sim.

### 41. Variance — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 17/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: A scheduled live event excludes whole timezones by design and needs a critical attendance mass per sitting that 400-player servers cannot guarantee, while the shop between hearings remains unspecified filler.
Exploit: Juror-packing guilds farming board seats and whisper-brigading verdicts — machine politics that is half the lesson and half an unappealable griefing lever with no counterweight.
Steal: the rotating player-juror board delivering reasonless final verdicts after pre-hearing lobbying — fundraising-panel theater as a periodic ritual inside a larger game.

### 42. The Playbook — MMO: 5/10 | TOTAL: 17/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Franchise trees give both seats a game, but the pyramid needs many franchisees per franchisor — at 400 players two chains own the meta — and months-long proof-out cycles are incompatible with live-service pacing.
Exploit: Rug-pull franchising — churn a fake manual, collect fees, rebrand, repeat — is exactly the guru-course economy it satirizes, but at game speed it processes newcomers into exit liquidity before they learn anything.
Steal: player-authored operating manuals you stake real savings on — trust in someone else's system as a purchasable, slowly falsifiable asset.

### 43. Open Up — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 13/30 | VERDICT: KILL
Structure: Watching neighbors' rituals is spectation, not interaction — a five-minute solo Wordle under an MMO skybox, with a deterministic signal library that is wiki-complete within a season.
Exploit: Signal-dictionary wikis ("papered window = +10% traffic in 7 days") automate the noticing, and noticing is the only verb the game has.
Steal: nothing — ambient street-diff observation survives as a free subsystem inside any living-street design (26, 37) where real player causes sit behind the changes.

### 44. Store Hours — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 13/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Binding shop hours to real-life availability makes timezone and lifestyle the primary stat — streets only function if players happen to be co-located in time, and the design gets uninstalled in self-defense before any lesson lands.
Exploit: Coverage alts and account-sharing to keep shops always-open turn commitment into a logistics arms race the time-rich win — hostage design breeds hostage-escape metas that erase the opportunity-cost lesson.
Steal: the silhouette of the missed customer — visible, identity-free evidence of demand you were not open to serve, the sharpest opportunity-cost render in the pool.

### 45. Word on the Street — MMO: 8/10 | TOTAL: 24/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: The information economy is the social engine itself, not decoration — undocumented-by-design with rotating ground truth stays rich at 10,000 and merely thins at 400, and it is the only design here whose community cannot wiki the game without becoming the game.
Exploit: Free out-of-game Discord aggregation should kill the paid in-game info market — except planted misinformation infects Discord too and seasonal rotation stales external archives, so the exploit is literally the curriculum: you cannot outsource epistemics.
Steal: information as a decaying, purchasable, poisonable resource — gossip with a shelf life and an author with motives.

### 46. As-Is — MMO: 7/10 | TOTAL: 22/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: Deal flow is self-renewing because the auction inventory is other players' failures — a live-ops perpetual-motion machine — with both seats playing a real game and grudges compounding across seasons; only sub-500 populations thin the docket.
Exploit: Alt-account shill bidding and history-laundering (sell the lemon to your alt to reset its record) is classic auction-house fraud — winner's-curse-by-design survives it, but only with identity friction and public provenance chains on every storefront.
Steal: one-photo-one-lie acquisition — due diligence compressed into bidding against a seller's self-reported number, with cross-season grudges as the reputation ledger.

### 47. The Regulars — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 16/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: If regulars are NPCs it is a solvable single-player decoder ring; if they are players the math fails — every shop needs twelve devoted patrons whose seat has no game of its own, an unfillable role at any population.
Exploit: Poaching cartels running scripted charm campaigns against rivals' regulars turn relationship-tending into raid-defense scheduling — griefing that punishes having a life rather than misreading a person.
Steal: the invisible referral-graph cascade — losing one customer silently pulls their unseen cluster weeks later; customer concentration rendered as network topology.

### 48. Across the Street — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 17/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: A 1v1 dueling ladder is matchmaking, not an MMO — density-proof but economy-free, and abandonment or mismatch makes half of all week-long matches dead air with no bystanders to absorb them.
Exploit: Win-trading and alt-feeding on the rivalry ladder — standard duel-game rating fraud that says nothing about markets and strips the bluffing lesson of its stakes.
Steal: stageable signals — every observable (the line, the dumpster, the signage) can be theater staged for your competitor, making competitive intel a bluffing layer rather than a data feed.

### 49. Small Fires — MMO: 6/10 | TOTAL: 20/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: The favor-debt web needs stable street pods of mutually known neighbors (37's architecture) to function and then works at both scales — but the fire dispenser underneath is single-player RNG chore generation with no market in it.
Exploit: Favor loan-sharking — veterans farming debts from drowning newcomers, then calling them in simultaneously to force cascade-failure and vulture the shop — is predatory-lending realism that curdles into a newcomer-processing machine without debt caps.
Steal: the favor-debt ledger with human creditors who choose when to collect — reciprocity as a real balance-sheet liability with adversarial timing.

### 50. Curb Appeal — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 12/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: The walk-by feed only exists at high density — at 400 players your storefront card is dealt to nobody — and pass 1 already ruled the blind funnel formally uninterpretable, leaving no game beneath the decoration.
Exploit: Tap-farming rings and bot scrollers inflating conversions turn the single metric into noise about noise — no lesson survives a faked numerator over an invisible denominator.
Steal: the ambient walk-by feed — your storefront surfacing as a card inside other players' ordinary play, marketing rendered as interruption of someone else's session.
