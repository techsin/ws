### 401. North Star Problem — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Vanity metrics versus the one metric that matters, and that it must be re-discovered from scratch every time the market era shifts.
Cheats: Revenue comes from an idle simulation, not real customers, so "watching your empire starve" is a number sagging — no cash-flow terror and nobody to win back.
Note: Epistemic and era-shifting, so wikis rot on schedule; the market for unverifiable dashboard layouts is genuine folk science, which is the rare community meta that deepens the lesson instead of solving it.

### 402. Grease — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: You can never afford full observability — choosing which blindness to keep is a real budget line every operator eventually meets.
Cheats: It is a QA/ops simulator with zero customers, market, or revenue discovery; entrepreneurship has been reduced to maintenance anxiety.
Note: Drift is properly epistemic and lying freelance auditors form a real trust market, but auditor reputations will be settled on an out-of-game Discord within a week.

### 403. The Numbers Person — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: The dashboard and the shop floor are different countries, and a business dies when nobody reconciles them.
Cheats: "20% wrong in undisclosed ways" is a designer dial, and the post-game reveal converts opaque reality into a solvable party puzzle with an answer key.
Note: Brilliant as a two-player communication game, but session-based co-op is not an MMO economy — the lesson lives in one argument, not in a market, and veteran pairs will catalog the lie types.

### 404. Restatement — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Keep your own books, because whoever controls the record controls the negotiation when the acquisition offer lands.
Cheats: A platform that edits your past is Kafka, not markets — real founders fight ambiguity and their own bad memory, not a hostile god rewriting history.
Note: Player notaries whose seals are worth exactly their reputation is the best organ here; the journal-diffing loop is clerical homework most players will script away, killing the intended paranoia.

### 405. Reconciliation — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: Reported numbers are estimates, and the truth arrives only when you close the books.
Cheats: The "detective story" has three suspects — breakage, market, thief — so the epistemics collapse into pattern-matching a small enum by week four.
Note: Weekly-dread pacing is genuinely clever, but an idler whose only real decision happens on Sunday teaches bookkeeping hygiene, not entrepreneurship.

### 406. Survivor Bias — TEACH: 9/10
Teaches: Your analytics only show survivors — the silent thousands who looked and left are where the business actually lives, and you must go out and find them.
Cheats: "Dark data expeditions" risk becoming a vending machine where cash buys the answer instead of judgment earning it — priced wrong, this becomes an unlock tree.
Note: Silence is the literal data model and the invisible majority are real players whose behavior drifts, so it is structurally wiki-proof; this is the brief, executed.

### 407. p-Hacker — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: The seduction of stopping an experiment the moment it flatters you, and paying for the false discovery long after you forgot which insight caused it.
Cheats: Customers are simulated noise, so it trains a scientist's statistical discipline more than a founder's judgment about actual people.
Note: The replication leaderboard is inspired anti-degeneration design, but once "run tests to full power" is internalized, the run itself risks going rote — the lesson is real but finite.

### 408. Snake Oil Exchange — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Vendor due diligence — everything you buy might be quietly bleeding you, and reputation is a lagging indicator by nature.
Cheats: The designed reputation lag gets routed around by out-of-game blacklists and streamer callouts, so the trust weather may clear permanently.
Note: Fully real-player adversarial and epistemic; scam waves as seasonal weather is exactly the right shape, if off-platform coordination is priced into the design rather than wished away.

### 409. The Placebo Panel — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Most of what you do does nothing — proving which lever is real requires interventions expensive enough to hurt, which is causal inference under cash pressure.
Cheats: It is you versus a noise generator; no customers or rivals live inside the causal loop, so the illusion-of-control lesson floats free of any actual market.
Note: Forged wiring diagrams and superstitions inherited through mergers keep the epistemics churning even after common panels are wiki'd — unusually hard to degenerate.

### 410. Goodhart's Garden — TEACH: 3/10
Teaches: Goodhart's law — any metric you pin becomes a lie.
Cheats: The lesson is a scripted curse that fires "within days" on NPC staff, so it is learned in one sentence and the remaining hundred hours are crop-rotation scheduling.
Note: Degenerates immediately into a wiki rotation table; the uncertainty is neither epistemic nor RNG — it is a rule printed on the box, which is the opposite of an unknown unknown.

### 411. Tick — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Timing is the whole trade, and the market's clock is nobody's friend.
Cheats: The entire game is one hidden variable, and cartel data-science will solve the heartbeat unless the devs keep secretly reshuffling it into unlearnable noise.
Note: False tick-tables are a real disinformation economy, but there is no product, customer, or unit economics anywhere — this is cryptanalysis wearing a business costume.

### 412. Denominator — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: A green ratio can mean you are winning or that your denominator is dying, and finding out which costs cash you may not have — unit-economics intuition under budget pressure.
Cheats: "Pay to reveal absolutes" flattens investigative judgment into a single recurring purchase decision.
Note: Predator players lowballing shops with beautiful dying percentages make the lesson load-bearing and social; stays epistemic because the hunters keep hunting after the wiki is written.

### 413. It Does That Sometimes — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: At scale your company develops behaviors nobody designed, and you debug them by experiment and observation, not documentation.
Cheats: Discrete scripts with enumerable interactions are catalog-able — the community bestiary will replace personal behavioral zoology within a month.
Note: Loop contagion across real-player supply contracts is the standout MMO mechanic and the one part that cannot be wiki'd; the single-company version is a solvable puzzle box.

### 414. Calibration — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: Your instruments are decisions too, and every reading is only as good as the trust chain behind it.
Cheats: Cozy metrology gardening — tending sensors is maintenance, not entrepreneurship, and nothing pushes back except entropy; no customers, no rivals, no runway.
Note: The calibration guild selling certified trust is the one genuinely entrepreneurial organ in an otherwise stakes-free terrarium.

### 415. Lorem Ipsum, Inc. — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Confident-looking data can be silent interpolation, and your delusion propagates through everyone who trusts your numbers.
Cheats: Synthetic-data tells (too smooth, no bad days) are statistically detectable, so players will run screeners and the trained "nose" becomes a downloadable plugin.
Note: Economy-wide delusion cascades through real trade networks are a superb systemic lesson; the individual detection loop is where it degenerates first.

### 416. The Average Customer — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: The average customer is a mathematical fiction — segmentation is discovered one real receipt at a time, and the segments migrate while you map them.
Cheats: Customer discovery is priced as a currency sink (pull a receipt) rather than a social act — no outreach, no rejection, nobody hangs up on you.
Note: Real-player customers whose clusters drift make this structurally unsolvable; epistemic all the way down, and the superposition-lie of aggregates is a lesson real founders pay dearly to learn.

### 417. Quiet — TEACH: 3/10
Teaches: Silence is ambiguous — smooth sailing and total collapse feel identical, which every founder inbox confirms.
Cheats: The entire game is one recurring decision (check or don't), so the profound theme sits on an expected-value calculation players will solve in an afternoon.
Note: An evocative anxiety toy, not an entrepreneurship game; there are no beliefs to form beyond an optimal polling interval, which makes the uncertainty decorative.

### 418. Turing Traffic — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Aggregate dashboards are garbage — true demand is measured by designing offers only a real human would answer, which is demand validation by behavior.
Cheats: Nothing stated stops the bot mix from being tuned designer noise, and shared trap libraries could commoditize the naturalism into a meta-build.
Note: Real humans learn to dodge known traps, so the epistemics self-refresh; studying your customers like a naturalist is exactly the founder posture the brief wants.

### 419. Fog of Ledger — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: You cannot afford to know your whole business — ignorance allocation is a strategic budget every quarter.
Cheats: Rendering accounting as RTS fog turns judgment into scouting-efficiency math with a wiki-ready audit-ROI table.
Note: Rivals knowing your margins before you do is a delicious real-player twist, but the core loop is spatial optimization, not belief formation — the metaphor is stronger than the mechanics.

### 420. Yesterday It Worked — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Churn is silent — real customers don't complain, they vanish, and no dashboard graphs an absence.
Cheats: If quality drift is imperceptible even to a player who tastes the product and walks the line, the game slides from epistemic learning into unfair noise you can only pray about.
Note: Silence from real player-customers is load-bearing exactly as the brief demands; ritual paranoia toward green lights is a lesson working founders recognize in their bones.

### 421. Oracle, Inc. — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Your model was trained on a market that no longer exists — knowing when to obey and when to defy your own forecasts is the actual job.
Cheats: Quarterly actuals as the only ground truth makes feedback glacial, so most players will meta-game the oracle's quirks rather than the market underneath it.
Note: Undocumented tradeable rival models are pleasingly opaque, and era shifts mean reverse-engineering never finishes — durable epistemic churn, weak cash-pressure texture.

### 422. The Telephone Org — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Orders decay downward and reports inflate upward — the CEO steers a company perceived only through layers of human distortion.
Cheats: Scoring the org collectively removes the individual incentive to deceive that the entire premise depends on — the distortion must be paid for or it evaporates into cooperation.
Note: Real players ARE the noise, which is permanently wiki-proof; but the transcript-reveal comedy framing says party game, not livelihood, and there is no market outside the org chart.

### 423. Alarm Fatigue — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: Every muted feed is a bet, and base rates make the one real crisis statistically identical to noise.
Cheats: This is an SRE on-call simulator — real judgment, wrong profession; nothing here touches customers, cash flow, or markets.
Note: Threshold-tuning converges to solved detection theory; the 3 a.m. "probably nothing" handoff between real friends is the one human, non-degenerating touch.

### 424. Commission — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Your most helpful advisor earns undisclosed commissions — every founder eventually learns to cross-examine bankers, agencies, and platform reps for lies of emphasis.
Cheats: A scripted copilot's tells and commission tables get catalogued fast, turning trust calibration into a community lookup table.
Note: Technically-true-but-misleading statements are exactly the right epistemic texture; but it is mostly single-player against the house unless the cross-company gossip economy carries real player stakes.

### 425. Shift Change — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Partner trust built and broken entirely through self-serving prose checked against the hard evidence of the numbers — cofounder trust calibration with strangers.
Cheats: The company itself is off-screen — the pitch specifies the forensic reading loop in detail but hand-waves what running the business between handoffs actually involves.
Note: Four real humans as each other's primary uncertainty is permanently wiki-proof; the closest thing in the batch to reading people under stakes, which founders will recognize instantly.
