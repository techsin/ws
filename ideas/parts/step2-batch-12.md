### 276. Earnout — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Post-acquisition value is decided by whoever controls measurement, so the contract you negotiate before dependence begins is the only leverage you will ever have.
Cheats: It starts after the entrepreneurial part is over — you're an employee in a political knife-fight with one adversary, not a founder facing an indifferent market.
Note: Epistemic (the acquirer is a real player whose incentives are learnable), but zero-sum 1v1 politics risks collapsing into grievance roleplay rather than judgment training.

### 277. Term Sheet in the Dark — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: Terms you didn't understand detonate hours later — deal literacy as scar tissue is a real founder wound.
Cheats: The skill actually trained is reading card typography and dealer tells — font forensics, a parlor trick standing in for clause comprehension, which is the thing it claims to teach.
Note: One wiki page mapping typography to clauses kills all opacity permanently; this degenerates faster than almost anything in the batch.

### 278. The Float — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Adverse selection in the flesh — the customers most eager to buy from you are exactly the ones you should fear, and you price opacity or die.
Cheats: Collusion-triggered correlated disasters may make griefing coordination, not underwriting judgment, the dominant variable, punishing skill with pure coordination attacks.
Note: Epistemic and self-renewing — pricing models can be shared, but the risk pool is adversarial humans who adapt, so the meta never settles into a wiki.

### 279. Rug — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Pseudonymous trust forensics — reading intent from public actions when identity is cheap is a genuine crypto-founder skill.
Cheats: It's Werewolf in a token costume; the betrayal is dealt by a role card rather than emerging from incentives, so nobody learns why cofounders actually defect.
Note: Social deduction has famously stable genre metas (vote reads, scripted accusations) — it degenerates into deduction skill, not entrepreneurial judgment.

### 280. Dilution Tower — TEACH: 3/10
Teaches: Dilution compounds, and taking someone's money makes your structure load-bearing on their goodwill.
Cheats: The mapping from cap-table math to block physics is arbitrary — players build tower-stacking intuition, not equity intuition, the textbook case of learning the game instead of the domain.
Note: The "can't inspect internal stress" opacity is fake — real cap tables are fully inspectable and dangerous anyway; this is a dexterity minigame wearing a term sheet.

### 281. Waterfall — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: The $200M-exit-pays-common-zero pour ceremony delivers preference-stack horror more viscerally than any textbook ever has.
Cheats: It's a visualization with a lobby, not a game — the pitch never identifies a decision the player makes between signing and pouring.
Note: Waterfall math is deterministic; the community ships a calculator in week two and the "nobody can simulate it" opacity evaporates entirely.

### 282. Microloan Mesh — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Defaults arrive without explanation or apology, so trust must be priced from thin behavioral signals and portfolio spread — real underwriting instinct.
Cheats: It trains lender judgment, never founder judgment — the player is always the bank, never the desperate borrower watching a runway.
Note: Epistemic and self-renewing since borrowers are adversarial humans, but a shared credit-scoring meta could flatten the trust graph into a FICO clone.

### 283. The Data Room — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Diligence is adversarial curation — the fatal fact is technically disclosed, boringly filed, and the skill is knowing where sellers bury bodies.
Cheats: 72 hours of folder-spelunking rewards search-UI grinding as much as skepticism; the winning skill may end up being filename regex, not judgment.
Note: Hider/seeker co-evolution keeps it epistemic, but it's a 1v1 teaching one narrow skill, and a community heat-map of hiding spots erodes it fast.

### 284. Peg — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Solvency is reflexive — your company survives exactly as long as people believe it will, and a history of not lying is your only durable asset.
Cheats: The founder experience is compressed to a defense minigame — no customers, no product, just a depth chart and a bluff, training trader nerves more than builder judgment.
Note: Attacker/defender meta is genuinely epistemic and player-driven, though disciplined whale coordination may make defense structurally hopeless (admittedly true of real pegs too).

### 285. Redemption Run — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: A fund — like any startup — dies of mood before it dies of math, and rosy investor letters are borrowed time at compound interest.
Cheats: "Honest values only you can see" hands the player a certainty real managers never have — in reality you are guessing your own book too, and that doubt is half the lesson.
Note: Bank-run cascades are player-generated and unwikiable, but the honesty dial risks converging on a community-known optimal spin level.

### 286. Sourced By — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: The brutal truth that venture feedback arrives years late and hopelessly confounded with luck, so humans substitute politics and folk statistics — exactly as in life.
Cheats: It teaches surviving a fund's org chart, not entrepreneurship — the player never validates, builds, or risks anything except reputation.
Note: Honest to the point of anti-learnability — when skill and luck are deliberately unidentifiable, it hovers between profound and pure RNG frustration.

### 287. Note Bomb — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: Stacked convertible notes interact at conversion in ways nobody at the table modeled — a real and underrated founder wound.
Cheats: Hiding your own notes' caps and discounts from you is fabricated ignorance — real founders signed those exact docs and got hurt anyway, which is the more interesting lesson the game dodges.
Note: Once the note-interaction table is wikied this is a solved timing puzzle with angel players as flavor text; the bomb skin is a cartoon.

### 288. Blind Book — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Pricing sealed information off seller reputation alone — where there is no regulator, trust history is the entire market infrastructure.
Cheats: The collections loop runs on procedurally voiced NPCs, so the emotional core — the human on the phone — is canned content players will skip-click by week three.
Note: Seller-side lying stays epistemic and self-renewing (sellers are players), but the buy side degenerates into tracked seller winrates, i.e., a spreadsheet.

### 289. The Charter — TEACH: 9/10
Teaches: The purest rendering in this batch of opaque institutions — "DEFICIENT" with no elaboration, and your only path is constructing a mental model of a rulebook you will never be shown.
Cheats: It risks presenting regulatory masochism as the whole of entrepreneurship — there are no customers, no revenue, no market, just you and the stamp.
Note: Structurally wiki-proof — seasonal rule drift decays all institutional knowledge, and the bazaar of possibly-false veteran templates IS the real startup-advice economy, perfectly reproduced.

### 290. Reinsurance Pass — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Systemic opacity — risk passed along a chain stops being understood by anyone, including its current holder; 2008 in miniature.
Cheats: Between catastrophes player agency is thin and the quake itself is a pure RNG event you cannot learn from — mostly you are waiting to discover what you already signed.
Note: The diligent player who meticulously tracks every treaty dissolves their own opacity, which is either the lesson landing or the game ending, depending on patience.

### 291. Two-Sided Mirror — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Both chairs of the disclosure game — technically-true answers, question craft, and pricing what you cannot verify — with the side-by-side reveal as brutal calibration feedback.
Cheats: Real life never shows you the other screen; the post-game reveal is a training-wheels mechanic that teaches faster but slightly falser than the fog founders actually live in.
Note: The community will taxonomize the procedural "trouble" into a diligence checklist, but the founder is a live human routing around it, which keeps the arms race breathing.

### 292. Burial Society — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Founding at its most naked — your product is other people's willingness to believe each other, growth imports fraud, and every trust ritual you design fails a new way each season.
Cheats: Member-vote claim adjudication can rot into clique politics where the operative lesson becomes "recruit friends," not "design mechanisms."
Note: Epistemically self-renewing by construction — fraudsters are players who adapt to your rituals, so no wiki ever closes the loop.

### 293. Forty-Eight Hours Ago — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Committing capital into a future you can only forecast, never react to — the two ghost portfolios are a genuinely novel discipline mechanic.
Cheats: It's a trading game — no customers, no rejection, no runway; the judgment trained is market forecasting, roughly the one skill founders famously don't need.
Note: Guild-shared forecasting models converge into a quant meta fast, and the player following the guild spreadsheet learns nothing but obedience.

### 294. Pay-to-Play — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: The cold-start problem of every bridge round — nobody commits until someone commits, and manufacturing that first yes from hidden reserves and portfolio politics is real founder work.
Cheats: Telling each investor a different story is mechanically rewarded until caught, flirting with teaching cross-briefing as a technique rather than a career-ending habit.
Note: Fully epistemic — five humans with hidden constraints cannot be wikied — and the post-close reserves reveal converts every loss into a lesson about who never had the money at all.

### 295. The Audit Lottery — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Integrity economics — liars beat you on the leaderboard this season, and boring honesty only compounds if you survive long enough to be believed.
Cheats: The entire game is one dial (how much to inflate), and rational players will solve for the equilibrium inflation rate just under detection value, reducing ethics to arithmetic.
Note: The pollution is player-generated so it never fully wikis, but a single-mechanic morality slider is a lesson, not a game.

### 296. Power Law — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Silence is the dominant information problem of early-stage investing — dead, heads-down, and fund-returner are indistinguishable, and misreading one silence outweighs every other decision combined.
Cheats: It's the investor's chair again, and a patient player can drift into a waiting simulator whose core loop is refreshing an inbox that stays empty.
Note: Deeply epistemic — founder comms are strategic behavior by real players — and the forced quarterly marking of pure fog is the single best judgment-training mechanic in this batch.

### 297. Secondaries — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Markets will happily price total fog — with zero disclosure, gossip quality and founder behavior become the only fundamentals, a true and usefully cynical lesson.
Cheats: It's a parasite game that only functions bolted onto an entire working venture MMO, and "4% of a lapsed account" makes player churn, not judgment error, the tail risk.
Note: Uncertainty stays epistemic only while founders emit learnable signals; if they simply go dark, pricing collapses to coin-flipping and the game teaches fatalism.

### 298. Parametric — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Quotes met with unexplained silence — too expensive, too complicated, or just not trusted, and you will never learn which — while real-world weather keeps your models permanently humble.
Cheats: The entrepreneurial surface (distribution, trust, product design) can be outgunned by raw meteorology — the best NOAA-API jockey wins regardless of business judgment.
Note: Real weather data is the rare uncertainty no wiki can ever solve, making this one of the few structurally degeneration-proof cores in the pool.

### 299. The LP Dinner — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Soft-circled money is not money — closing means engineering mutual belief that the others are already in, one conversation at a time.
Cheats: The "random-but-influenceable evaporation" of commitments is a dice roll wearing a psychology costume; the game concedes it cannot actually model why soft-circles die.
Note: Voice-chat humans keep it epistemic and script-resistant, though 20-minute sessions train a party-game reflex version of fundraising rather than its months-long grind.

### 300. Exit Liquidity — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: You cannot read investor intent from behavior, so the only real lever is shipping utility fast enough to convert mercenaries before the divergence moment — build through the fog.
Cheats: Making believer and wolf "mechanically identical" is honest to crypto but anti-epistemic by design — on the read-your-whales axis there is literally nothing to learn, only to endure.
Note: The chain-museum case-law archive is a lovely community learning institution, but it risks archiving the outcomes of coin flips as if they were strategy.
