# Step 4 — Critique Pass 3 (MMO-STRUCTURAL + VERDICT) — Batch 04 (Ideas 76–100)

### 76. Redshift Freight Exchange — MMO: 6/10 | TOTAL: 19/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Blind-bid boards scale gracefully from 400 to 10k and the intel market gives a second seat a game, but the shippers are NPCs, so the social layer is a side-market bolted onto a solo bidding loop.
Exploit: Discord aggregates everyone's bid outcomes into a public shipper-response spreadsheet that dissolves the silence fog, while in-game bid-rotation cartels split lanes — the cartel IS a real freight lesson, the spreadsheet kills the core one.
Steal: silence taxonomy — non-response with distinct hidden causes (overpriced vs blacklisted vs lost) that players learn to distinguish only through their own bid history.

### 77. Tongueless Bazaar — MMO: 6/10 | TOTAL: 20/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: The phrasebook knowledge-market works at any population and server-local meaning drift resists cross-server wikis, but every player sits in the same trader seat and the merchants are NPCs — multiplayer is metagame, not world.
Exploit: Alt accounts absorb all risky experimentation so mains never eat a permanent stall-fold, gutting the cost-of-failure economy the whole design depends on; poisoned phrasebooks, by contrast, ARE the adverse-selection lesson.
Steal: competitor-poisoned community knowledge base — shared documentation that is profitable to corrupt, forcing trust calibration on every "fact" you didn't verify yourself.

### 78. Franchise of the Overmind — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 11/30 | VERDICT: KILL
Structure: The only multiplayer surface is a shame leaderboard and a rule-lore back channel that the wiki devours in weeks, leaving a single-player anxiety sim wearing an MMO badge.
Exploit: Full citation-code concordance plus datamined inspector schedules turn the Kafka premise into checklist compliance — the exploit is the exact death pass 1 predicted, and nothing survives it.
Steal: nothing — inferred-rules-from-violations is done better by Stamp God with rotation and a live theory market.

### 79. Greenfall Contractors — MMO: 5/10 | TOTAL: 15/30 | VERDICT: KILL
Structure: Shared-biosphere entanglement is real multiplayer, but the payment loop is you versus an NPC satellite, and at 400 players most planets host one contractor — the attribution drama needs density it won't reliably have.
Exploit: Sabotage-seeding a rival's plot hours before the verification pass is legal, untraceable by design, and rage-inducing rather than instructive — attribution ambiguity curdles into a pure griefing meta.
Steal: nothing — "paid only for what the client happens to measure" is carried better by Maydays' open-channel coverage economics.

### 80. Long Silence Trading Co. — MMO: 7/10 | TOTAL: 22/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: Async notification play is timezone-proof and lifestyle-light — the rare MMO frame that works identically at 400 and 10k — and intro-brokering is genuine role asymmetry where the social graph IS the endgame economy, with no attribute grind anywhere.
Exploit: Broker rings selling warm intros to dead colonies, and Discord regression of NPC response functions — the fake-intro fraud IS the lesson (diligence your introductions), but settlement responses must drift or the spreadsheet wins.
Steal: warm-intro brokering — relationships as a scarce, tradable, fraud-prone asset that veterans sell and newcomers must earn.

### 81. Deadweight & Daughters — MMO: 5/10 | TOTAL: 19/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Wreck races need population density and timezone overlap that a 400-player server cannot deliver, and the counterclaim layer gives claim-lawyers a seat but makes them parasites on the only fun verb.
Exploit: Industrial counterclaim-trolling — file on every haul, settle for nuisance fees — is patent-troll economics that partially IS a litigation-risk lesson but at MMO scale taxes every payday into misery.
Steal: provisional revenue — money you already spent staying legally clawback-able for weeks, so cash-on-hand and cash-you-can-keep are different numbers.

### 82. Stamp God — MMO: 7/10 | TOTAL: 23/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: Short async puzzle sessions fit any timezone, the theory market runs at 400 or 10k, emergent roles (experimenter, broker, counterfeiter) come free, and newcomers cold-start by buying cheap theories — clean structure, zero attribute grind.
Exploit: Guild Discords pool experiment results and solve rule-sets in hours for free, undercutting the paid in-game theory market where the counterfeit mechanic lives — survivable only with per-server rule seeds and scheduled rotation, at which point stale scholarship becomes lore.
Steal: cheap-probe experiment design — spending small to interrogate an opaque system before betting big, with purchasable rule-theories of unverifiable quality.

### 83. Lanefinder — MMO: 5/10 | TOTAL: 18/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: The substrate being the live player economy is the right instinct, but at 400 players traffic data is statistical noise — the design has a hard population floor, and between Census Days there is no session to log into.
Exploit: Cartels wash-hauling fake traffic before the census snapshot to pump lane data, then dumping depots on believers — index manipulation that genuinely IS a markets lesson, LIBOR with a starfield.
Steal: deliberately stale data — a six-month-lagged census as the only market information, graded all at once on a communal Census Day event.

### 84. Cold Chain — MMO: 6/10 | TOTAL: 18/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Convoys are a real social engine and the share-protection-then-split-the-market dilemma works at modest density, but real-time decay dictates session length and punishes anyone who can't finish a haul tonight.
Exploit: Convoy betrayal — sell the manifest to port speculators or break formation to land first and crash the market on your escorts — which IS the coopetition lesson, executed by the players on each other.
Steal: loans collateralized by decaying inventory with automated repossession — runway pressure you feel physically, ticking in your hold.

### 85. Concourse — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 16/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: The social layer is the entire engine — which means an off-peak or 400-player concourse is a dead mall with no single-player floor, the steepest density cliff in the batch.
Exploit: A community Bloomberg — one shared Discord price sheet — deletes the no-price-feed premise in a week; realistic, yes, but here the exploit doesn't teach, it just uninstalls the game.
Steal: scheduled market-day windows — compressing the population into guaranteed-density sessions so a social economy always opens to a crowd.

### 86. Snacks for the Vorth — MMO: 5/10 | TOTAL: 19/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Staff-poaching makes trade secrets ambulatory and gives the player-vs-player layer teeth, but customers are static NPC biology that will be datamined into a cookbook, and the poaching market is thin at 400.
Exploit: Alt accounts taste-test risky batches so the main's species-standing is never exposed — identity cheapness deletes the permanent-shunning economy exactly as in Tongueless Bazaar.
Steal: reorders-or-silence — retention as the only customer feedback channel, with no reviews, ratings, or explanations ever.

### 87. Void Where Prohibited — MMO: 5/10 | TOTAL: 19/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Player captains as your installed base is the right MMO instinct, but a warranty book needs thousands of sold units — at 400 players the liability tail is a liability thread — and the daily loop is solo inbox triage.
Exploit: Rival brands buying your units to stage failure clusters and brigade fake warranty claims, forcing a ruinous recall — warranty fraud IS a real lesson, but coordinated at guild scale it drowns the diagnostic signal the game runs on.
Steal: the liability tail — revenue already spent, defects arriving months late and garbled, and one recall-vs-deny decision that prices your brand against your cash.

### 88. Static Prospectors — MMO: 2/10 | TOTAL: 9/30 | VERDICT: KILL
Structure: A journal leaderboard is the only multiplayer organ in a game whose core verb is unspeedable solitary waiting — this is a single-player screensaver with a comments section.
Exploit: The community ships an out-of-game signal-classification tool within a month and the epistemic fog becomes a solved preprocessing step — the exploit is total and teaches nothing.
Steal: nothing — public reputation staked on noisy claims is done with actual gameplay attached in Perihelion and Surveyor's Gambit.

### 89. Maydays — MMO: 8/10 | TOTAL: 25/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: Every seat has a game — operators underwrite, crews subscribe, rivals poach, subcontracting builds an apprenticeship on-ramp for newcomers — demand emerges from live player behavior at any population, shift-window contracts solve the on-call lifestyle problem, and there is no attribute grind anywhere in sight.
Exploit: Staged-breakdown fraud rings — subscribers faking maydays in a colluder's cheap zone, or rivals engineering distress calls in your known dead spots to humiliate you on the open channel — and underwriting against fraud IS insurance, so the nastiest exploit is the curriculum.
Steal: overcommitted coverage sold as contractual shift windows — promising more than you can deliver, then positioning scarce capacity against demand you can only predict.

### 90. Splice & License — MMO: 6/10 | TOTAL: 18/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Licensor-to-player-colony is genuine role asymmetry that scales, but the tribunal is NPC dice, and the endgame seat — defendant — is the one nobody wants to play.
Exploit: Liability laundering through disposable shell alt-licensors so the tribunal always traces blame to an empty account — corporate-veil abuse is a real lesson, but free alts make exposure optional and the whole risk economy evaporates.
Steal: licensed assets mutating downstream in player ecosystems you can no longer observe — exposure growing precisely where your visibility ends.

### 91. Tolls of the Ancients — MMO: 5/10 | TOTAL: 15/30 | VERDICT: KILL
Structure: Chokepoint politics is EVE-grade social sport, but it needs heavy real traffic to tax, and the Ancients layer — the actual premise — is an NPC slot machine bolted above the only living system.
Exploit: Toll cartels plus gate-camp griefing tax the entire server's mobility and shred the new-player experience — EVE proved the politics works and also proved exactly who it churns out.
Steal: nothing — the platform-risk lesson lands as pure fatalism here, and the politics layer emerges for free in any design with player-owned infrastructure.

### 92. The Glyph That Means No — MMO: 5/10 | TOTAL: 18/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Pitch sessions are streamable theater at any population and the contested wiki is a real community organ, but the investors are NPCs, so the multiplayer is commentary on a single-player performance.
Exploit: Mass statistical analysis of glyph-to-funding-outcome correlations either completes the wiki (mystery patched out) or proves the glyphs random (astrology confirmed) — the design loses to its own community either way.
Steal: legible process, illegible verdict — the choir visibly reacting during your pitch while the final glyph stays forever untranslated, so craft feels real without the system ever explaining itself.

### 93. Debris Futures — MMO: 7/10 | TOTAL: 22/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: Value derives entirely from other players' unannounced plans, the obligation-resale chain is genuine player-made finance that no wiki can solve, and it functions at 400 (short chains) and 10k (full derivative ecology) — the one structural sin is newcomers spawning as exit liquidity, patchable with the wage-floor fix.
Exploit: Whale consortiums shorting the chain then triggering the cascade with fake expansion announcements — an engineered 2008, and it IS the systemic-contagion lesson, provided the wage floor keeps the blast radius off newcomers' rent.
Steal: seller-to-seller obligation chains — passing uncompleted work onward until someone holds the bag, breeding derivatives and periodic collapse from player behavior alone.

### 94. Crewminds — MMO: 7/10 | TOTAL: 22/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Ex-asset companies entering the live economy is a self-generating competitive landscape that even pads thin servers, but the crews are levelable assets — attribute grind by proxy — and daily agency is dashboard-thin between betrayal beats.
Exploit: Min-maxers discover that leasing crews only into zero-learning jobs prevents defection, so optimal play is enforced stagnation — the exploit precisely inverts the milk-vs-nurture lesson the game exists to teach.
Steal: progression with betrayal risk — the better your asset gets, the likelier it defects and incorporates as your competitor, making growth itself a priced decision.

### 95. Pheromone Exchange — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 17/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Rival wakes are technically player interaction, but there are no faces, no counterparties, and no gossip — the social layer is fluid dynamics, and a market sim without people is not an MMO seat worth holding.
Exploit: Wake-griefing — flying sampling runs purely to corrupt rivals' reads costs nothing and reads as vandalism, tipping the observer-effect lesson into a pollution meta nobody enjoys.
Steal: measurement that disturbs the market — sampling leaves wakes others can read and spoil, so market research is itself a market-moving, contested act.

### 96. Vend the Void — MMO: 5/10 | TOTAL: 17/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: The player-eyes trust market is a real principal-agent game, but it needs residents on every colony to hire, and the honest core loop — refreshing a stale dashboard — is idle-game structure the MMO layer decorates.
Exploit: Agents looting your machines then reporting vandalism they committed — unverifiable-informant fraud that IS the principal-agent lesson in its purest form; the exploit is the best thing in the design.
Steal: paid player informants whose reports you cannot verify — importing the trust problem as a hiring decision with real money on it.

### 97. Protocol Zero — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 16/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Inverse density problem — 10,000 players violating taboos burn procedurally-finite species at industrial rates, so the shared world is a content treadmill that only survives at small populations, where the commons drama then goes quiet.
Exploit: Scorched-earth guilds deliberately burning species to deny rivals — a genuine tragedy-of-the-commons lesson that, at MMO scale, terminates in a wasteland server with nothing left to teach on.
Steal: markets as a destructible commons — rival experimentation permanently consuming shared opportunity, making restraint a competitive decision.

### 98. Surveyor's Gambit — MMO: 8/10 | TOTAL: 24/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: True two-role asymmetry that degrades gracefully — at 400 players personal memory polices trust, at 10k player-built escrow and ratings institutions emerge, and the institution-building IS the endgame — with the miner seat needing the due-diligence verbs from pass 2 to be a real game.
Exploit: Reputation-farm-then-exit-scam is literally the lesson, but alt-cycling to shed a burned name is the design-killer — the entire trust economy stands or falls on identity being expensive to replace.
Steal: unenforced reputation — no system ratings, trust existing only in player memory and player-built institutions, with costly identity as the load-bearing prerequisite.

### 99. Perihelion — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 16/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Player tourists are real customers and counter-forecast FUD is real PvP, but a twice-a-decade payoff cadence is live-ops poison — no server population survives a decade of downpayments waiting for the batch's best screenshot.
Exploit: Ponzi rotation — new expedition deposits quietly funding old refund runs until one no-show sky collapses the whole book onto customer-players — which IS the pre-sale cash-flow lesson, taught at the cost of everyone it takes down.
Steal: deposit-funded ventures — operating on customer money against an unverifiable forecast, where a refund run is the death spiral.

### 100. Redshift Ledger — MMO: 5/10 | TOTAL: 20/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: Diegetic logout is the single best structural answer to MMO absence in the pool, but the hauler game is parasitic on an unspecified stay-behind economy that must be a complete second game — this is half a design, and at 400 players split across time-strata both halves thin out.
Exploit: Alt-pairing across time — your stay-behind alt manipulates the future market your main is hauling into, front-running your own arrival — insider trading that deletes the bet's uncertainty and with it the entire lesson.
Steal: diegetic logout — absence as in-fiction travel, so returning players arrive in a changed player economy instead of resuming a paused one.
