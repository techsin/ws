### 541. Initial Here
Mix: 56(syndicated slip underwriting + live mid-voyage reinsurance) + 104(risk priced on other players' real runs + spectator sweat rooms) + 200(provenance-free intel where planting lies about yourself is legal) + 258(fixed Friday payroll clock)
Pitch: A harbor capital where founder-captains finance real cargo runs and every other career prices them: underwriters initial slips in a live coffeehouse, reinsurers buy and dump open risk mid-voyage like poker hands, intel brokers sell manifest gossip the captain may have planted himself, and claims investigators earn commission unmasking scuttle rings. Uncertainty is adverse selection made human — the captains hungriest for your ink are precisely the ones who know something you don't — and the weeks at sea resolve on a nightly arrivals board, with rented galleries where rival syndicates sweat the same hulls together. Minute 1 you co-sign a sliver of someone's slip and feel the bell when a ship posts; month 3 you lead a syndicate, squeeze a captain's Friday payroll pressure against his premium quote, and plant one beautiful lie about your own manifest. Rejection is priced, never spoken: no underwriter ever tells a captain why his premium tripled.
Guards: Claims pay from underwriter capital, not an NPC faucet (no inflation), nightly arrivals plus mid-voyage reinsurance turn the dead wait into playable poker (retention), NPC hulls thicken thin servers (density), bonded seats and permanent slip lineage make every signature expensive identity, and the hidden state is live captains' intentions (no wiki).

### 542. Thursday Street
Mix: 147(invisible creditor patience + communal Thursday-night scramble) + 282(mutual-liability circles on a trust-graph terrain) + 355(offered loan terms as your only doom diagnosis) + 352(public deathwatch window)
Pitch: A stall-market district where every founder's rent lands Friday and the only credit is other players: Thursday night the lending street opens, and sharks, mutual-liability circles, and workout specialists haggle bailouts in public while the crowd reads who's desperate. Nothing ever rejects you outright — lenders just quote terms, and the rate is the only diagnosis you get of how doomed the street thinks you are, while the supplier who shrugged off last month's delay cuts you off wordlessly this month because his patience is a real player's mood. Stalls in terminal decline enter a public deathwatch where strangers wire rescues, bid on the corpse, or just gather to watch. Minute 1 the scramble is dark-comic street theater you can join with pocket change as a micro-lender; month 3 you're a circle steward whose co-signers' judgment is your balance-sheet risk, or a shark whose quoted terms move the street's gossip.
Guards: The scheduled weekly scramble solves lifestyle and payoff cadence in one move, a bankruptcy fresh-start valve keeps the curriculum from graduating only churn, patience lives in player lenders and drifts (no wiki), circles function from thirty players up (density), and stall lineage makes a defaulted name unlaunderable.

### 543. Hallmarks
Mix: 335(unobservable diligence + scandal contagion + playable inspections) + 404(notary seals worth the notary's live reputation + tribunal showdowns) + 98(no system ratings, trust only in player memory) + 45(certifier-vetting via decaying, poisonable gossip)
Pitch: A trade city that publishes no quality stats: founder-makers ship goods whose claims are just claims, and the trust industry is the other half of the server — certifiers run timed Papers-Please inspections while privately deciding how hard to actually look, notaries sell timestamped seals on merchants' books, and forgery is legal, only fatal to be caught at. Rejection lives in the mark you don't get and the client who stops booking inspections without a word; uncertainty regenerates because a corner-cutting certifier looks identical to a rigorous one right up until scandal day, when contagion retroactively poisons every product wearing the mark and the whole city rubbernecks the career funeral. Disputes convene as scheduled tribunals where your paper trail is worth exactly what your notary's name is worth that morning. Minute 1 the inspection minigame is a crunchy forgery-spotting puzzle anyone can enjoy; month 3 your seal is a business whose entire capital can burn in one afternoon — which is the lesson.
Guards: Inspection is a real-time verb, not a slider-and-dread wait (retention), scandal contagion makes identity catastrophically expensive so alt-churn is self-ruinous, trust living only in player memory and gossip cannot be wikied, and tribunal days plus scandal days give the server a reliable spectacle cadence at any density.

### 544. Regretfully Yours

### 545. Rot & Charter
Mix: 350(seeded NPC institutions that visibly decay into player hands) + 292(waiting periods, witness rules, and hearings as founder-designed products) + 60(vouching-with-stake admission) + 360(the dead bank's loan book as a player asset class)
Pitch: A frontier boomtown launches with a creaky NPC bank, escrow house, and courthouse that rot on a public schedule, and the real game is the succession: player founders charter replacement institutions whose actual rules — waiting periods, witness requirements, bonding levels — they design themselves, and every design visibly fails a new way each season. Traders need escrow to move anything, newcomers need a stake-backed vouch from an insider to enter any market, and when the old bank finally collapses its loan book is auctioned as pursuit-rights paper, birthing the collections career overnight. Uncertainty is institutional — nobody knows whether the escrow house you trust survives the season — and rejection is a vouch no insider will risk their own standing on. Minute 1 the NPC scaffolding makes trading safe enough to learn honestly; month 3 your charter's clever witness rule is being gamed in a way you must diagnose and patch in public, which is the founder experience in miniature.
Guards: Decaying NPC institutions solve cold start and density with the same mechanic, each season's novel institutional failure regenerates the curriculum against wikis, staked vouches price identity at admission, and collapse auctions and charter hearings give the server scheduled communal payoffs (retention, lifestyle).

### 546. No Ticker

### 547. Deep Premium

### 548. Skiptrace
Mix: 360(secondary debt market + pursuit rights + debtor tradecraft) + 355(seized collateral keeps living inside the winner's deals) + 45(informants selling decaying, poisonable early warnings)
Pitch: In a host city where failure is common and paper never dies, every founder's old defaults get securitized and sold, and the buyer gets pursuit rights: collections is a skip-tracing cat-and-mouse career against debtor tradecraft — aliases, shell shops, and bribed informants whose warnings decay and are sometimes planted by the collector himself. Getting found is never a jump-scare but a summons to a public workout table where terms, not tempers, settle it, and collateral you lose keeps existing: your seized Rolodex contact now works your pursuer's deals and remembers exactly what you did. Uncertainty lives in never knowing which of your informants sold you out; rejection is your old partner's new shop declining your alias's credit for reasons neither of you can say aloud. Minute 1 buying a five-coin slice of a stranger's debt and following the trail is instant detective fiction; month 3 you're either the city's best tracer or a re-founder running a clean second act with one loose thread you cannot afford to have pulled.
Guards: Pursuit can only trigger a scheduled workout hearing, never asset destruction at 4am (lifestyle plus catharsis instead of interruption), aliases cost escrowed capital and burn on discovery so identity is priced rather than free, informant intel is human, decaying, and poisonable (no wiki), and debt slices stay tradable at any population (density).

### 550. The Weathermakers
Mix: 269(one never-announced server-wide credit cycle) + 217(policy weather generated from aggregate player behavior) + 106(the bankrupted rotate into the examiner chairs) + 56(syndicated underwriting as the transmission mechanism)
Pitch: A banking city where the macro-economy is real and nobody — including the developers — can publish an answer key: reserve rules, permit quotas, and credit windows are set by an Examiners' Bureau staffed by rotation from bankrupted players, and their calls plus everyone's aggregate lending behavior generate booms and winters that arrive unannounced. Guards: Endogenous policy weather is structurally unwikiable since even the developers lack an answer key, chair rotation staffs the gatekeeper seat at every density and regenerates opacity per cohort, named crashes become shared server history with forums full of told-you-sos (retention), and an NPC deposit floor keeps 400-player servers solvent between booms.
