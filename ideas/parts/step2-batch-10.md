### 226. Commissary — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Operating in public under resource scarcity — competitors read your ingredient deliveries, and graduating to your own lease is a bet-the-company scaling decision made on part-time data.
Cheats: The silence/rejection axis is absent — everyone is in the same room, so the game trades the founder's loneliness for a social poker match about who borrows the mixer.
Note: Epistemic and hard to wiki because the intel is live player behavior, but the slot auction risks decaying into a bid-sniping meta divorced from the actual food business.

### 227. Shelf Space — TEACH: 9/10
Teaches: Distribution IS the business — the default outcome of a cold pitch is nothing at all, and you learn buyer psychology only by burning your three weekly sample boxes on falsifiable hypotheses.
Cheats: Real-player buyers may rationally ignore ALL inbound, turning the lesson into a dead mailbox with no gradient — the pitch never explains why a buyer would need discovery badly enough to open the box.
Note: Silence is fully load-bearing and structurally unwikiable since buyers are humans running idiosyncratic empires; slotting fees arriving as a surprise invoice is exactly how founders actually learn trade spend exists.

### 228. The Palate Problem — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Watch what customers do, not what they say — behavioral signal (cleaned, picked at, untouched) on a three-session lag is real validation epistemology.
Cheats: The "hidden palate vector" is an authored system bolted onto real players, quietly substituting reverse-engineering a designer's math for reading actual humans.
Note: Genuinely epistemic, but if vectors are static per-player the community datamines them within a season; the "evolving" claim must be real or the lesson dies in a spreadsheet.

### 229. Acquired Taste — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Category creation subsidizes your copycats — the second-mover trap is a sophisticated, true lesson almost no business game has ever touched.
Cheats: "No way to tell whether the market is forming or dead" can be indistinguishable from pure noise for weeks of play, and a lesson the player cannot perceive is not being taught.
Note: The real degeneration is game-theoretic: once the community learns second movers win, nobody evangelizes anything and the mechanic starves itself to death.

### 230. Saturday, 6 AM — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Attribution under confounds — position, neighbors, weather, and stock all move together and nobody debriefs you, so you must design your own controlled comparisons across weeks.
Cheats: One experiment per real week is brutally slow pedagogy, and the weather dice are pure RNG dressed up as uncertainty.
Note: Adjacency effects stay epistemic only because neighboring vendors are players who move; any algorithmic foot-traffic rule underneath gets wiki'd within a season.

### 231. Rot — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Perishable inventory is a cash-flow clock, and autopsies that hide the counterfactual (what rotted, never what would have sold) nicely mimic survivor-only data.
Cheats: Decay-while-offline is a dark-pattern retention mechanic wearing an entrepreneurship costume, and the whole loop is ops optimization — no customer discovery, no validation, no pivot, no venture judgment.
Note: Demand is partially epistemic via the player-fed spot market, but the game degenerates into a solved buy-curve once the community charts decay rates and margins.

### 232. RFP: Wedding, 150 Guests, Budget Undisclosed — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Calibrating unpaid effort against ambiguous demand — how much free tasting-menu work to gamble on a brief you must read between the lines of is the founder's daily proposal bet.
Cheats: Losers get zero information forever, and if player-hosts choose capriciously the silence stops teaching and becomes a slot machine with proposal-writing attached.
Note: Invisible reputation via host back-channels is realistic and unwikiable, but the design must let players eventually feel their reputation move or nothing compounds and nobody adapts.

### 233. The Cursed Corner — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Due diligence and the market for lemons — hidden residue plus veterans offloading haunted leases onto newbies is a genuine adverse-selection sandbox.
Cheats: "Curse residue" is a cartoon of why locations kill restaurants — real corner-death is traffic, parking, and rent math, not a smell debuff you secretly rehab for arbitrage.
Note: Residue values get datamined into a pre-lease checklist fast; the epistemic layer survives only in the player-landlord's nondisclosure game, which is the part worth keeping.

### 234. Everyone's a Critic — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: You cannot fake it for the important customer because you cannot identify them — consistency as the only viable strategy is a true, if thin, service lesson.
Cheats: The dominant strategy collapses to "play uniformly well," at which point the entire critic layer is paranoid flavor text on an ordinary restaurant sim.
Note: This is not entrepreneurship — no validation, runway, outreach, or quit decisions anywhere; the genuinely interesting economy belongs to the critics, not the founder.

### 235. Mother Culture — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: You scale processes you never fully understand — days-long confounded experiments with ambiguous readouts force real one-variable-at-a-time experimental discipline.
Cheats: Five confounded variables plus fuzzy feedback plus real-time delay sits exactly on the line where epistemic uncertainty becomes unlearnable noise, and the pitch never proves a gradient exists.
Note: Tradeable cultures with hidden lineage are a lovely trust mechanic, but lineage traits are precisely the kind of content a community wiki catalogs to death in a month.

### 236. Handshake — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Trust calibration with zero enforcement — reading counterparties, investing in relationships, and absorbing the tomato guy who simply stops answering is the true texture of small-business supply.
Cheats: "Reputation you can't see" will be made fully visible by community spreadsheets and Discord blacklists within a month, doing exactly what the design forbids.
Note: Fully epistemic and human on both sides; the out-of-game reputation layer arguably recreates real gossip networks, so the degeneration is half lesson, half defeat.

### 237. Blind Menu — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Attribution is the hardest problem in pricing — and the post-season unsealing that shows how wrong your mental model was is a genuine calibration-training mechanic almost nothing else in this batch has.
Cheats: It is a pricing duel, not a company — no product risk, no outreach, no runway decisions; one entrepreneurial muscle exercised beautifully, in total isolation.
Note: Epistemic and wiki-proof because rivals are live players; the seasonal reveal converts frustration into learning instead of leaving pure noise behind.

### 238. Preorder or Perish — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Validation by preorder with an opaque funnel — visits-versus-pledges with no "why" is exactly the founder's conversion hell, and having to actually deliver gives failure real teeth.
Cheats: The pitch admits mid-campaign pivots are "pure gambles," conceding the single most teachable moment to RNG instead of testable belief.
Note: Cross-campaign learning stays epistemic because backers are real players with real tastes, but the meta will converge on Kickstarter-formula guides — which is, uncomfortably, exactly what happened in reality.

### 239. Pop-Up — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Cold outreach met with being left on read, and building distribution out of borrowed audiences — the hustle texture is exactly right.
Cheats: The design brags that no event is repeatable, but unrepeatable experiments produce no learning — this teaches variance tolerance, which is to say gambling, more than judgment.
Note: The learnable layer (which hosts reply, which rooms convert, how a following compounds) is epistemic and human; the per-night outcome is dice and the pitch celebrates the dice.

### 240. The Redacted Code — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: Regulation as an undocumented system learned by citation, with fines as purchased information — a clever exploration-versus-exploitation frame on compliance.
Cheats: A fixed hidden rulebook is the definition of wiki-collapse, and the pitch builds its own solution market (photocopy trading) that un-redacts the game for everyone within weeks.
Note: The forged-rulebook wrinkle — trust-calibrating information vendors — is the only durable mechanic; everything else dies the moment the code is transcribed to a forum post.

### 241. Word of Mouth — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: You cannot buy distribution, only earn it and then wait in genuine silence — the invisible contagion graph makes product-as-marketing structurally true rather than aspirational.
Cheats: The lesson depends entirely on a dense, live playerbase; in a thin one the silence is not pedagogy, it is a dead game indistinguishable from a bad product.
Note: Seasonal contagion-map reveals make the uncertainty epistemic in retrospect, but the meta will converge on stunt-of-the-month formulas that hollow out the "remarkable moments" verb.

### 242. The Second Location — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: The founder is the one unmeasurable variable — forcing your body to a single venue per session turns key-person risk and delegation into a physical mechanic instead of a stat.
Cheats: If the returning numbers are permanently too confounded to decompose (manager versus block versus food), the game preaches humility but cannot reward the player who probes intelligently.
Note: Player-managers improvising keeps it permanently unwikiable — SOP drift is epistemic because managers are humans with incentives, not a scripted decay function.

### 243. Four Walls — TEACH: 9/10
Teaches: Designing cheap reversible probes before an irreversible bet — trial residencies, pre-sold memberships, an exit clause you knowingly overpay for — is literally the epistemology of validation, taught as the core verb.
Cheats: It is one momentous decision stretched into a whole game, and the pitch is silent about what the second act plays like after the lease is signed or declined.
Note: Structurally wiki-proof — the confound (food versus hunt versus price versus parking accident) is unique to each player's own history, so no guide can decompose YOUR success for you.

### 244. Regulars — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Churn is silent — the regular who simply stops appearing, no complaint, no exit interview, is the truest small pain in the entire batch.
Cheats: It is a hospitality intimacy sim, not entrepreneurship — remembering the oat milk is customer service, and there is no validation, capital, pricing, or pivot anywhere in the loop.
Note: Depends entirely on real players finding the diner side intrinsically worth playing; if they do not, the counter is empty and the game literally has no content.

### 245. Co-Packed — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Your brand absorbs your supply chain's sins weeks late — the sabotage-versus-sloppiness-versus-your-own-spec attribution puzzle is a genuine epistemic triad every CPG and hardware founder knows.
Cheats: Staged factory visits risk being a flat "audits are useless" cynicism dial — the pitch offers no way to get better at seeing through the staging, so diligence skill has nowhere to grow.
Note: Player co-packers with rational margin-squeeze incentives keep this game-theoretically alive and unwikiable; principal-agent problems do not have wiki solutions.

### 246. Worth — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Price is an argument and feedback lies — the table that compliments you and pays insultingly little is fundraising's "great meeting, no check" in perfect miniature.
Cheats: Pay-what-you-want is a pricing curiosity, not a business lesson, and hidden diner reputations will live in a shared community spreadsheet by week two, gutting the blindness the whole game rests on.
Note: Epistemic per-diner but the transferable judgment is thin — this is an art piece about valuation, not a venture; nothing else about running a company exists here.

### 247. Franchise Fever — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Scaling means selling control of your own name — screening applicants who lie, writing SOPs nobody reads, and pricing the revoke-versus-rot decision is real franchise economics.
Cheats: The shared brand score reduces the subtle reputational bleed of a bad franchisee to one visible meter, letting players manage a number instead of judging people.
Note: Real-player franchisees keep screening permanently epistemic; the meta will collapse into interview checklists traded on Discord — which is roughly how real franchisors operate, so partial credit.

### 248. Outbreak — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Boring compliance is insurance that only pays in a crisis, and correlated shocks punish the innocent — luck and industry beta made brutally visible.
Cheats: Once the community computes that traceability paperwork is +EV, it becomes a solved premium everyone pays, and the central "decision" evaporates into a wiki line item.
Note: The blame politics (lobbying, snitching, framing) is the durable part because it is player-versus-player; the outbreak itself is an event mechanic, not a game.

### 249. Line, Pick Up! — TEACH: 3/10
Teaches: At best, that operational information is lossy and errors compound — mishearing "no nuts" into a lawsuit is consequence, of a sort.
Cheats: The uncertainty is literally acoustic noise — pure perceptual RNG with no beliefs to form or test — the exact opposite of epistemic entrepreneurial uncertainty.
Note: A charming party-game UX gimmick in a restaurant apron; nothing here touches validation, cash flow, customers-as-market, or any founder judgment whatsoever.

### 250. Best Before — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: When to quit — reading ambiguous omens against unknown runway, and learning survival curves from a public graveyard of other players' corpses, is the rarest and truest skill in the pool.
Cheats: If expirations roll from a designer's table, the community graveyard becomes an actuarial solve — the pitch hands players the wiki as a feature and just hopes the distribution stays fuzzy.
Note: Epistemic only if omens carry real signal and death causes stay heterogeneous (player landlords, shifting trends); pure hidden timers would reduce quitting to expected-value arithmetic.
