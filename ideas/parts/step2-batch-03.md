### 51. Dead Letters — TEACH: 9/10
Teaches: Committing capital against silence whose cause you cannot know — shipwreck, insult, or a rival's better offer all look identical from your desk — which is cold outreach rendered as a core resource decision.
Cheats: If NPC reply behavior is a dice roll rather than a learnable disposition, half the correspondence pool is pure noise wearing an epistemic costume, and the player/NPC ambiguity becomes a shrug instead of a lesson.
Note: Structurally wiki-proof — real-hours latency and real-player correspondents cannot be datamined — but the design lives or dies on whether silence carries correlated, eventually-inferable signal.

### 52. The Incanto — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: The winner's curse — bidding blind on assets whose true value hinges on information someone else holds, then living with the leverage you took on to win.
Cheats: "Profitability hinges on diplomacy the Senate knows and you don't" is a slot machine unless the game leaks leading indicators; losing bidders learning nothing is realistic but risks teaching only "bid lower," not why.
Note: Spies and syndicates make the hidden state purchasable, which keeps the uncertainty epistemic; community bid-shading heuristics will emerge, but that's real auction markets, not degeneration.

### 53. Kontor — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Unexplained rejection by a gatekeeping in-group — the anonymous blackball with no feedback is the most honest model of "we passed, best of luck" in the batch.
Cheats: The hidden system is literally other players' moods, so it can collapse into a popularity contest where veteran cabals haze newcomers out of the funnel — and once admitted, the entrepreneurship evaporates into guild politics.
Note: Epistemic in the truest sense (you form theories about whom you offended and test them next season), but the business layer is thin garnish on a social-club simulator.

### 54. The Relay — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Operating a business on distorted secondhand signal — demand three handoffs away that you will never observe directly — plus silent counterparty churn when your partner simply stops appearing.
Cheats: If NPC traders backfill dead segments, the "distortion" becomes designed noise with a tunable dial, and grinders will solve crate-price arbitrage tables within a month.
Note: Epistemic and permanently shifting when populated by real players; catastrophically dependent on population density — an empty adjacent oasis is not uncertainty, it's a dead game.

### 55. Ledger of Little Falls — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Credit judgment with batch-delayed feedback — extending trust on gossip and gut all year, then getting graded once at settling day, which is exactly how receivables actually punish you.
Cheats: If crop failures and skip-towns are uncorrelated dice, the player's "judgment" is being graded on noise, and the settling-day reckoning teaches superstition, not underwriting.
Note: Falsifiable shared blacklists are a genuinely nasty trust-market mechanic; the risk is a cozy single-player lending sim with MMO trim rather than an MMO.

### 56. Underwriters' Row — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Pricing risk under radical ignorance with live adverse selection — the players buying your coverage are precisely the ones who know something you don't, including their own plan to scuttle.
Cheats: The entrepreneurial seat is arguably the wrong one — you learn underwriting and trust calibration, while the founders (the voyage-runners) live in a different game you only watch through shipping lists.
Note: Actuarial spreadsheets will emerge, but real players gaming the underwriters keeps the distribution non-stationary — this is epistemic uncertainty that regenerates itself, which is rare and valuable.

### 57. The Rumor Bourse — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Information as a depreciating asset of unknowable freshness — you must price a report without knowing how many copies already exist, which is real information-market economics.
Cheats: Out-of-game Discord verification shreds the opacity on day one, and legal false rumors push the market toward a lemons equilibrium where rational players simply stop buying — pedagogically correct, commercially dead.
Note: Source-reputation death "without anyone announcing it" is beautifully load-bearing silence; the degeneration vector is external tooling, not internal solving.

### 58. Dead Reckoning — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: Executing with honest error bars — planning margins for water and landfall when you cannot know your own position.
Cheats: The uncertainty is about your latitude, not your market — this is a navigation roguelike with cargo flavor, and nothing in the loop resembles customers, validation, or cash-flow judgment.
Note: Navigation is a masterable skill, so the epistemic content burns off with practice and the game converges to execution; the poisoned position-swap is the only entrepreneurial cell in the body.

### 59. The Revenue Men — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Playing under rules that even the enforcers haven't received yet — regulatory ambiguity where both sides are guessing at the decree is a real founder feeling.
Cheats: Ten-minute bluffing rounds make this a poker variant in a tricorn hat — no runway, no customers, no compounding — so the "psychological metagame" teaches yomi, not judgment.
Note: A good session game whose business theme is a costume; the secret-decree mechanic deserves a better host body.

### 60. Hawala — TEACH: 9/10
Teaches: The cold-start problem in its purest form — established networks refuse your paper and never say why, so you overpay on the fringe until someone vouches, which is exactly how reputation capital is actually bootstrapped.
Cheats: The incumbent oligarchy that makes the lesson true also makes the newcomer experience miserable enough to kill retention — the game's pedagogy and its funnel are at war.
Note: Defaults surfacing months later when a chain unwinds is epistemic uncertainty with realistic lag; unwikiable, because the hidden state is living players' trust in you specifically.

### 61. The Sample Case — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Selling the promise before you own the goods — committing to today's price for next season's delivery while inputs drift underneath you is genuine working-capital entrepreneurship.
Cheats: If price and fashion drift are unreadable RNG curves rather than inferable trends, the sourcing scramble grades luck, and the game quietly becomes commodity-spreadsheet homework.
Note: Cancellation-as-absence (the retailer simply isn't at the depot) is load-bearing silence, and player retailers with long memories make reputation compound structurally.

### 62. Fair Season — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Committing everything blind against a market you can only imagine, then receiving all feedback at once in four brutal days — the fair calendar is planning-under-zero-signal made architectural.
Cheats: Scheduled synchronous weeks exclude anyone with a job, and the between-fair phase risks being shallow menu-clicking dressed up as strategy.
Note: Community price-history archives will make fairs efficient fast, but that mirrors real market maturation rather than killing the lesson; reputation as the only carried-in signal is elegant and unpatchable.

### 63. Bottle Post — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Making unenforceable one-sided offers into a void — shipping product to strangers you'll never meet and learning only from what's there when you return.
Cheats: Individual feedback is hopelessly confounded (robbed? taken honestly? never found?), so the epistemics live at the server-population level while each actual player experiences pure noise — the community learns, the person doesn't.
Note: Gorgeous emergent-norms art piece; as a teacher of entrepreneurial judgment it's homeopathic, and the no-chat purity that makes it special is what starves the learning loop.

### 64. One Hull — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Ruin risk versus expected value — the founder's real lesson that a positive-EV bet is wrong when you can't rebuy in, because survival compounds and drowning doesn't.
Cheats: Once the community datamines loss rates per route, it collapses into an EV spreadsheet with permadeath stakes, and "upgrades, crew, and reputation on one keel" is character-attribute grinding in boat cosplay — exactly what the brief forbids.
Note: The dubious-quality paid intelligence is the sole epistemic organ; everything else is a solved actuarial table waiting for the wiki.

### 65. Terra Incognita, Ltd. — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Product quality as a trust asset with months-long feedback lag — you choose your own honesty and the market learns the truth through who fails to come back.
Cheats: Your defrauded customers are dead and can't leave reviews, so the feedback loop may exceed a play-lifetime and the operative lesson becomes "fraud pays if the lag is long enough" — plus out-of-game map-seller reviews patch the opacity overnight.
Note: The inverted opacity (you are the hidden system) is the freshest design in the batch; forgeable imprints add a real brand-protection dimension nobody else attempts.

### 66. The Saffron Bubble — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: The greater-fool dynamic and the eerie truth that crashes arrive as silence — everyone simply got quiet at once — which is emotionally exact.
Cheats: There is no product, no customer, no unit economics — this teaches speculation, not entrepreneurship — and it's a one-lesson game: after a server's first crash, the scar tissue means the asset never trades again and the game is over.
Note: Reflexive crowd-reading is Keynesian beauty-contest guessing, not learnable hidden structure; brilliant as a subsystem inside a larger economy, hollow as a standalone.

### 67. The Factor — TEACH: 9/10
Teaches: Silent churn — clients fire you by never consigning again, no exit interview — plus the service-business truth that good communication retains clients through bad results, which every consultant and fund manager will recognize in their bones.
Cheats: Performance leaderboards (in-game or Discord) could commoditize factors into a stats market, flattening the relationship game the design depends on.
Note: The book of business eroding invisibly until dockside gossip explains it is epistemic uncertainty with realistic lag and a learnable social system behind it — the strongest rejection-mechanic-to-real-lesson mapping in the batch.

### 68. The Patient Market — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Reading information staleness — inferring whether a standing price reflects a world that still exists is a real and subtle market skill.
Cheats: The master skill is trivially tooled — a community timestamp-tracker patches "no freshness shown" within a week — and the loop degenerates into sniping AFK players, which teaches predation, not judgment.
Note: Trading against the absent is a haunting premise, but it's a market-microstructure lesson, not an entrepreneurship one; no customers, no runway, no venture.

### 69. Entrepot — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: The platform-owner's dilemma — monetizing the flow data your position grants you versus the discretion that keeps merchants routing through you — which is literally the Amazon-marketplace conflict in a warehouse.
Cheats: The "optimal skim rate" could get community-solved into a known tolerance threshold, though living player tolerance shifting under reputation shocks resists a fixed answer.
Note: Learning from the empty bays — churn announced by absence, never complaint — is load-bearing silence done right, and the epistemics (probe, overstep, lose a client, recalibrate) are genuinely learnable.

### 70. Thirty Coins — TEACH: 4/10

Cheats: The core loop is a sensory reflex minigame — weight, ring, shear under queue pressure — which is Papers Please with ducats, and the community publishes debasement alerts and counterfeit guides on day two, amputating the epistemic core.

### 71. Change Alley — TEACH: 7/10

### 72. The Tea Race — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: Committing to speed-versus-safety tradeoffs against competitors you cannot observe — racing ghosts on days-old intelligence is honestly how competitive strategy feels.

### 73. Peddler's Mile — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Cold outreach economics felt in your legs — volume knocking, mostly silence, doors that give no signal before the knock, and a precious few compounding regulars with expectations.
Cheats: Procedurally placed doors with fixed open-rates make this a pedometer skinner box — literal step-grinding for RNG rolls, the exact attribute-grind the brief forbids — unless door behavior is learnable, which the pitch never claims.

### 74. House of Years — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Institutional knowledge as the founder's true estate — deciding what your successor needs to know is a real and rarely-gamified lesson in building things that outlive you.

### 75. The Uncodified Port — TEACH: 8/10

Cheats: If tariffs and taboos are static, external wikis solve every port in weeks and the lesson dies — the pitch needs secret decree drift and doesn't say it has one.
Note: Making documentation itself an in-game commodity — pilot books that may be lovingly accurate or deliberately wrong — is a structural anti-wiki design that poisons the well the community would otherwise drain; genuinely clever.
