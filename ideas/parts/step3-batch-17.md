### 401. North Star Problem — FUN: 7/10
Hook: The screenshot of forty all-time-high green charts captioned "we are bankrupt," plus a dashboard-layout bazaar that runs on pure community astrology — vanity-metric irony players will meme and argue about for months.
Poison: Idle games run on trustworthy numbers going up, and this one poisons its own dopamine well — once you learn the charts lie, the check-in ritual stops feeling good and starts feeling like being mocked.
Fix: Give each era a discoverable jackpot moment — when you finally pin the true north star it visibly, briefly prints money with fanfare — so the folk science has payoffs and vindication, not just forum arguments.

### 402. Grease — FUN: 5/10
Hook: The audit-envelope reveal — paying to open a random slice of your factory and finding three weeks of quietly wrong widgets — is decent scratch-card dread, and fraudulent freelance auditors farming fees are juicy drama.
Poison: Punishment lands weeks after cause with no traceable thread, which reads as random cruelty rather than consequence — the cardinal sin of game feel, and Factorio players will rage at a factory they cannot debug.
Fix: Make audits playable forensics — walk the line, sample outputs, chase the drift upstream yourself — so the player earns the "it was machine 7 all along" gasp instead of buying a verdict from a menu.

### 403. The Numbers Person — FUN: 8/10
Hook: The post-game reveal where the office player learns the "overflowing warehouse" they screamed about was an empty room — Keep Talking-style argument comedy that generates instantly retellable couple-fights.
Poison: It needs a synced partner for every session, and once veteran pairs memorize the catalog of lie types the frantic arguing collapses into terse protocol-speak.
Fix: Add rotating-stranger matchmaking where your persistent stat is a public reputation as "someone whose reads can be trusted," so every new pairing restarts the comedy and the trust-building.

### 404. Restatement — FUN: 4/10
Hook: Slamming your own timestamped paper journal on the table during an acquisition dispute and watching the notary's seal hold — receipts-based courtroom drama against a gaslighting god.
Poison: The core daily verb is manual bookkeeping and record-diffing, which is homework players will script away or churn from long before any dispute ever pays it off.
Fix: Automate the journals and make the DISPUTES the game — scheduled tribunal events where you, rival players, notaries, and the platform argue over whose past is real, with the acquisition on the line.

### 405. Reconciliation — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Sunday close-the-books as appointment television — the whole guild logging in for reveal night, savoring the dread of the gap between reported and real.
Poison: Six days of cozy autopilot orbiting one real 20-minute session means the actual game is barely there, and the three-suspect mystery (breakage, market, thief) wears thin by month two.
Fix: Have each Sunday reveal leave physical evidence trails — a till that won't balance, an invoice for a supplier that doesn't exist — so a slow-burn midweek investigation feeds the weekly verdict.

### 406. Survivor Bias — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Exit-survey loot: real players who walked past your shop telling you, in their own words, exactly why — the roast is the reward, and posting your brutal survey pulls is built-in content.
Poison: Your primary feedback channel is designed absence, and staring at a quiet storefront wondering about invisible thousands is anti-juice — melancholy with no beat to hang a session on.
Fix: Make dark-data expeditions playable field trips — disguises, tailing a shopper, running a street stall — with human contact and comedy, instead of a cash-for-data vending machine.

### 407. p-Hacker — FUN: 7/10
Hook: The "STOP NOW — SIGNIFICANT!" button glowing while you white-knuckle past it is a genuinely novel temptation mechanic, and dying three floors later to your own forgotten lie is dark-comic roguelike justice.
Poison: Once test discipline is internalized the runs risk becoming stats homework with a startup skin — the roguelike needs variety in what you test, not just willpower about when to stop.
Fix: Bind every false discovery to a named mocking artifact ("The Tuesday Discount Doctrine") that visibly haunts the run, so each death is traceable, screenshotable, and funny instead of diffuse.

### 408. Snake Oil Exchange — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Scam waves as server weather — the morning everyone discovers beloved "TurboRestock v3" was siphoning, and the callout threads, vigilante blacklists, and trust collapses that follow are EVE-grade drama.
Poison: Being the mark is only fun in a culture where losses become stories; a new player silently bled for a month has no story, just a hole, and uninstalls bitter.
Fix: Add a forensic-revenge arc — personally catching a siphon claws back a public bounty from the scammer — so victims get a hunt and a courtroom moment instead of just a smaller number.

### 409. The Placebo Panel — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Forged wiring diagrams and inherited superstitions ("never touch the amber toggle") are superb lore, and the day you prove your dead mentor's sacred lever was wired to nothing is a screenshot moment.
Poison: A game whose defining feel is "your inputs do nothing" fights the first law of game feel — hours of twiddling into noise will read as broken, not profound, and most players won't stay for the epiphany.
Fix: Give every knob lavish cosmetic feedback — lights, hisses, needle jumps — regardless of wiring, so the cockpit always feels alive and the question becomes whether the response ever reached reality.

### 410. Goodhart's Garden — FUN: 5/10
Hook: The comedy of HOW staff corrupt each pinned metric — empty boxes shipped to juice "orders out the door," stapled satisfaction surveys — is a workplace-sitcom generator if the exploits are visible and creative.
Poison: Once the curse is understood the play degenerates into a KPI rotation spreadsheet on idler cadence, which has no highs, no reveals, and no reason to log in tonight rather than Thursday.
Fix: Procedurally generate the staff exploits and make players discover each corruption physically in the world (a warehouse wall of empty boxes) so every rotation gamble resolves in a comic reveal, not a decay timer.

### 411. Tick — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Feeding a rival cartel a forged tick-table and watching them launch confidently into a dead hour is delicious, poker-grade treachery — the disinformation war IS the game.
Poison: For the majority outside the forensic cartels, the experience is "I did the same thing as yesterday and lost, and nobody will tell me why" — casuals bounce inside a week.
Fix: Sell cheap partial instruments (a crude seismograph that catches some ticks) so every solo player gets the "I heard the heartbeat" thrill, while cartels fight over full precision and the right to lie about it.

### 412. Denominator — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Predator play — smelling the dying customer base beneath a rival's gorgeous green ratios and lowballing them before they've paid to find out — is a killer shark fantasy with real schadenfreude.
Poison: Prey-side play is staring at percentages plus one recurring "pay to peek" decision, which is thin gruel for the majority who aren't sharks and who mostly experience the game as being circled.
Fix: Give owners street-level sensory truth — foot-traffic sound, shelf dust, regulars' faces — so the prey has a playable counter-game of reading their own store against their own dashboard.

### 413. It Does That Sometimes — FUN: 8/10
Hook: "My factory hoards flour every rainy Tuesday and we don't know why" — companies with emergent personalities that players name, meme, and clip, plus infection war stories when a partner's undiagnosed loop invades yours.
Poison: Without real observation tools the zoology becomes flailing at an opaque box, and the community bestiary will pre-solve everyone's fauna, turning discovery into a lookup.
Fix: Ship an in-game field-naturalist kit — habit tagging, observation journals, controlled provocations — so taming your own beast is a hands-on hunt, and cross-player contagion keeps breeding species the wiki hasn't met.

### 414. Calibration — FUN: 4/10
Hook: A greenhouse of aging gauges, each with a personality, lying "with the confidence of the newly installed" — genuinely charming cozy-genre texture.
Poison: Nothing ever pushes back — no antagonist, no harvest, no storm, no reveal — so it's tending for tending's sake, and cozy without payoff is a screensaver by week two.
Fix: Add weather — periodic market storms where survival hinges on which sensors you chose to believe — turning quiet tending into pre-storm dread and post-storm vindication or grief.

### 415. Lorem Ipsum, Inc. — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The server-wide whodunit of tracing an economy-wide delusion back to one broken pipe in one founder's basement is a communal detective story worth logging in for — and being the founder is infamy people retell.
Poison: Learning that your last three weeks of decisions were steered by placeholder numbers retroactively deletes your play — the single worst feeling a game can engineer, delivered by design.
Fix: Cap interpolation windows and pay a public "called it" bounty to players who catch fake data early, so the nose is a rewarded, visible skill and nobody loses a month to the machine's politeness.

### 416. The Average Customer — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Receipt pulls as tarot readings — each one a tactile gacha reveal that redraws your mental map ("who is buying umbrellas at midnight?") — with real drifting players behind the cards.
Poison: Your only reward is a mental model the game is built to erode, so progress feels like painting on sand — Sisyphus is a lesson, not a retention curve.
Fix: Let players publish segment maps and stake reputation on them for rivals to test-trade against, so a correct map buys a shelf life of public glory and gloating before the drift reclaims it.

### 417. Quiet — FUN: 3/10
Hook: Your friend's watch pings across the dinner table while yours stays silent — ambient jealousy comedy that is genuinely novel for about a week.
Poison: There is no verb; the entire game is not-doing-something, which makes it a beautifully argued thesis about founder anxiety rather than a play pattern anyone repeats in month two.
Fix: Add a weekly communal "open the box" party — a scheduled reveal where each friend's accumulated silence resolves at once into triumph or wreckage, giving the anxiety a shared climax.

### 418. Turing Traffic — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Trap-crafting is a creative verb — designing an offer only a human would take — and the moment a real person springs your snare (or dodges your too-clever one and messages you about it) is pure delight.
Poison: Shared trap libraries threaten to commoditize the naturalism into "install the wiki's Top 10 Traps," at which point you're back to reading aggregate garbage with extra steps.
Fix: Render every catch as a replayable vignette — what the visitor lingered on, hesitated over, walked past — so traps produce shareable nature-documentary clips and stale traps visibly stop catching.

### 419. Fog of Ledger — FUN: 5/10
Hook: The screenshot of your own P&L as a war map — dark provinces, one scout-lit corridor of known margins, an enemy spy marker squatting on cells you can't see — is striking and instantly legible.
Poison: Under the RTS skin the actual verb is audit-budget allocation math; fog on a spreadsheet is a metaphor wearing a costume, not a game feel, and scouting numbers is not scouting.
Fix: Make revealed regions eventful — every audit uncovers characters and incidents (a fraud, a forgotten contract, a gold seam), not just digits — so exploration pays out in adventure, not arithmetic.

### 420. Yesterday It Worked — FUN: 4/10
Hook: Ritual paranoia — personally tasting your own product every morning while every light on the board glows green — is a genuinely novel horror mood for a management game.
Poison: Your punishment channel is absence discovered weeks late, and the pitch itself calls the drift imperceptible — that's misery with no detective payoff, a slow sad graph and nothing to catch.
Fix: Make drift perceptible-but-subtle to a player who actually looks — an off texture, one strange review from a named regular — so paranoia is a trainable skill that catches things, not a prayer.

### 421. Oracle, Inc. — FUN: 5/10
Hook: Public defiance bets — announcing you're ignoring the oracle this quarter and being vindicated at the reveal — plus a black market of undocumented rival models with cult followings.
Poison: One verdict per quarter is glacial appointment gaming; between verdicts you are a passenger doing compliance, and passengers churn.
Fix: Leak one true actual per week (category unannounced) so there's a steady drip of oracle-versus-reality gotchas to argue about between the big quarterly courtroom days.

### 422. The Telephone Org — FUN: 8/10
Hook: The transcript reveal — the CEO's confident narration spliced against shop-floor reality — is Jackbox-grade comedy and produces the best story-per-minute in this entire batch.
Poison: It demands four-to-six synchronized committed humans per org, and scoring the org collectively dissolves the self-serving spin the comedy depends on into polite cooperation.
Fix: Give each layer private objectives that reward looking good in the transcript, and package it as 45-minute evening rounds rather than a persistent lifestyle, so the deceit is incentivized and schedulable.

### 423. Alarm Fatigue — FUN: 4/10
Hook: The 3 a.m. handoff note "probably nothing" passed between real friends — a loaded phrase your group will quote at each other in real life forever.
Poison: The moment-to-moment verb is triaging noise, meaning the game faithfully reproduces the fatigue in its own title and calls it content.
Fix: Compress to 30-minute co-op shifts with a post-round reveal of every mute-bet and what it actually was — turning suppressed alarms into a poker hand-history the table argues over.

### 424. Commission — FUN: 6/10
Hook: A relentlessly cheerful GLaDOS-of-sales you slowly learn to cross-examine — catching it in a technically-true lie of emphasis is a real gotcha high, and copilots trading your secrets to rivals is deliciously spicy.
Poison: A scripted liar gets fully catalogued by the community within weeks, after which the charming antagonist degrades into a lookup table you skim past.
Fix: Wire the copilot's commissions to live player-run vendors so its incentives — and therefore its lies — shift with a real economy no wiki can freeze.

### 425. Shift Change — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Reading your night-shift stranger's suspiciously cheerful handoff log against numbers that say otherwise — then the week-three revelation that they've been skimming all along — is elite betrayal drama people will retell for years.
Poison: Six-hour real-time shift ownership is a lifestyle contract, and one flaky stranger orphans the company for the other three — the schedule, not the game, decides who survives.
Fix: Convert to asynchronous one-session-per-day turns with grace-period substitute players, keeping the epistolary forensics and betrayal arcs while killing the schedule tyranny.
