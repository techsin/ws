### 101. Ravenpost & Rue — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 16/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: The receiving seat has no game — at 10,000 players guild inboxes drown in merchant spam and at 400 every guild shares one Discord, so the reply channel the entire design depends on is an unpaid chore for the other side.
Exploit: Merchants bypass ravens by DMing guild officers out-of-game while guilds start selling reply slots, turning silence into a paywall — both moves kill the cold-outreach lesson dead.
Steal: the vanishing outbox — outreach with no read receipts, no bounce, no state at all, forcing iteration on pitch and targeting with zero attributable feedback.

### 102. Offal & Ichor — MMO: 6/10 | TOTAL: 19/30 | VERDICT: MINE
Structure: With seller-authored slang both seats have a real game (bluff vs. decode), but auctions only fire when raid parties land, so at 400 players off-peak docks are a dead venue and the venue is the game.
Exploit: Sellers alt-shill their own sight-unseen lots while buyer rings split lots to suppress prices — the ring is a legitimate commodity-market lesson, but shilled descriptions destroy the literacy game that is the whole point.
Steal: counterparty-authored opacity — the seller writes the lot description in their own drifting dialect, so appraisal skill is reading people rather than memorizing a wiki.

### 103. The Last Lantern — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 14/30 | VERDICT: KILL

Exploit: Alliances pump inn-adjacent land by announcing raid plans, sell to marks, then deploy elsewhere — insider real-estate manipulation that converts the meta-reading lesson into being farmed by guilds.
Steal: nothing — the bet-then-spectate loop is the batch's weakest verb set, and its ghost-town monuments are set dressing other reputation mechanics deliver with agency attached.

### 104. Underwriter of the Deep — MMO: 7/10 | TOTAL: 21/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: Perfectly parasitic design — adventurers are already playing their own game and insurance simply attaches to it, so every seat is full at any population and nothing anywhere is an attribute grind.
Exploit: Fraud rings — insure an alt or a friend, die on purpose, split the payout — and that IS the lesson, because moral hazard, fraud detection, and blacklists are the actual insurance business.
Steal: risk products sold against other players' real gameplay outcomes, where adverse selection (applicants privately know their own recklessness) survives any wiki.

### 106. Guildcharter — MMO: 7/10 | TOTAL: 22/30 | VERDICT: KEEP

### 107. Sevenday — MMO: 9/10 | TOTAL: 25/30 | VERDICT: KEEP
Structure: The weekly wipe is the best cold-start solution in the pool — every newcomer joins a fresh server each Monday — and demand regenerated from prior aggregate behavior works identically at 400 and 10,000 while progression lives in the notebook, not the character.
Exploit: Guild-scale demand poisoning — coordinated mass purchases in week N distort week N+1's regenerated demand for exploitation with foreknowledge — which is market manipulation and reflexivity, i.e., the lesson wearing a black hat.
Steal: knowledge-only persistence across resets — the world wipes weekly, the notebook survives, so hypothesis velocity is the only durable capital.

### 109. Gone Below — MMO: 8/10 | TOTAL: 23/30 | VERDICT: KEEP

### 110. Contract of the Brass Djinn — MMO: 4/10 | TOTAL: 16/30 | VERDICT: MINE

### 112. Waystone & Toll — MMO: 3/10 | TOTAL: 11/30 | VERDICT: MINE
