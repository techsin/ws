### 101. Ravenpost & Rue — FUN: 5/10
Hook: The jackpot morning a reply raven actually lands — even a rude one — and you screenshot it to the guild Discord like a winning lottery ticket.
Poison: Nine sessions in ten are opening an empty outbox with no other verb in the game, and no amount of folklore makes an empty mailbox a game.
Fix: Give the shop a tactile daily loop — brewing stock, raising and dispatching the ravens themselves — so the inbox check is dessert on a real meal instead of the whole menu.

### 102. Offal & Ichor — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Prying open a crate you won on a hunch — "wyrm-wet" decoded correctly at last — is loot-box juice you earned with your own hard-won literacy.
Poison: Auctions only exist when real raid parties land, so off-peak docks are a dead game, and a wikied glossary reduces the thrill to spreadsheet bidding.
Fix: Let sellers write their own lot descriptions so the slang stays drifting, dialectal, and bluffable — the decode game becomes reading people, not memorizing terms.

### 103. The Last Lantern — FUN: 4/10
Hook: Waking up to find the meta broke your way and your taproom is the loudest room on the server is a genuine screenshot-forever high.
Poison: The losing majority spends an entire real season staring at an empty inn with no verbs — spectating your own mistake is misery without gameplay.
Fix: Give innkeepers active demand levers — festivals, bard bookings, rumor-planting, poaching regulars — so a bad location is hard mode to fight, not a bench to sit on.

### 104. Underwriter of the Deep — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Sweating a dungeon run you underpriced, watching your client limp out at 1 HP, is sports-betting adrenaline in a fantasy skin.
Poison: A bad-luck streak that bankrupts you with no way to know if your pricing was wrong lands as a cosmic mugging, and the time between resolutions is dashboard-watching.
Fix: Sell scrying-feed spectator rooms where rival underwriters watch insured runs together and talk trash, converting the wait into communal sports night.

### 105. The Identifier's Dilemma — FUN: 4/10
Hook: Buying blind, flipping the item for fifty times the price, and watching the seller realize what they handed you is a delicious predator moment.
Poison: Sanctioned review-griefing means strangers can tank your livelihood with lies you can never contest — helpless rage, not war stories, and helpless rage churns.
Fix: Make disputed reviews trigger public accusation trials with stakes on both sides, so malice becomes courtroom theater the town gathers for instead of silent damage.

### 106. Guildcharter — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Three weeks of tavern dinners, a charter lost to a nobody, then winning the council seat and discovering the machine was never coherent — a payoff arc players will monologue about for years.
Poison: The entire game is scheduling humans, so off-peak players and non-schmoozers have nothing to do, and the feedback-free early losses make a brutal first ten hours.
Fix: Leak a gossip fragment after every lost bid — possibly true, possibly planted — so rejection always hands you a clue-shaped toy and a reason to scheme tonight.

### 107. Sevenday — FUN: 8/10
Hook: Sixday arson night — the whole server torching their own shops for data — is a built-in weekly festival people will schedule their lives around.
Poison: The optimal play is notebook homework between sessions, and players who bond with their builds will resent losing even a week-old shop to the wipe.
Fix: Add a persistent museum of legendary runs — best weeks, weirdest products, record burns — so mastery and infamy visibly accumulate across wipes.

### 108. Mimic, Incorporated — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The clip of a reformed mimic cracking after forty days sober and eating a haggler mid-transaction is the most streamable single moment in the batch.
Poison: Once the joke lands it's a willpower bar stapled to shop chores, and one-slip-erases-years guarantees your most invested players quit at their first accident.
Fix: Make slips survivable dark-comedy arcs — witness bribes, church-mandated probation, a "reformed" placard — so failure generates content instead of deleting the save.

### 109. Gone Below — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Hiring a repo crew of players to descend after your missing mule — and the tale they surface with — is an emergent quest generator other games would kill for.
Poison: Between the morning rush and the returns the day is accounts-receivable anxiety, and a quiet week at the gate is a dead one.
Fix: Turn dawn at the dungeon gate into a timed rush-hour of sizing up parties, haggling deposits, and gut-call refusals, so every day has one guaranteed high-energy verb.

### 110. Contract of the Brass Djinn — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The server-famous scam clause — "sold him a dragon's weight of gold, in feathers" — becomes tavern folklore, and everyone wants to author the next one.
Poison: The core verb is drafting legal prose, which is homework to most humans, and losing your savings to a comma you can't even identify is misery unless you're the one laughing.
Fix: Ship standard-form contracts with a few editable clause slots so play is spotting and planting traps in familiar text, not authoring from a blank page.

### 111. Bard & Banner — FUN: 5/10
Hook: Your ditty for another player's sausage stand becoming an actual server-wide earworm is creative-expression gold no other genre offers.
Poison: The design structurally refuses to ever confirm you did a good job, and unconfirmable wins produce zero dopamine — the game withholds its own high notes.
Fix: Add rare smoking-gun moments — NPCs humming your tune outside the client's shop — that let you feel impact occasionally without ever making it measurable.

### 112. Waystone & Toll — FUN: 3/10
Hook: The one night a player war reroutes an army through your backwater stone and you hundred-x while sleeping is a great story — once, for someone.
Poison: Between rare toll tweaks there is nothing to do but watch a traffic line sag in silence — an idle game that withholds the very numbers idle games exist to shower on you.
Fix: Put the operator bodily at the waystone — greeting travelers, hearing rumors, upselling supplies — so traffic becomes faces and gossip instead of a declining graph.

### 113. Hoardkeepers — FUN: 5/10
Hook: A dropped dragon client publicly scorching your office, smoke visible across the server, is rejection as spectacle nobody forgets.
Poison: Your game loop is another player's mood and calendar, so when your whale gets bored and logs off, the game about employment quietly unemploys you.
Fix: Backstop with NPC understudy dragons running modeled personalities so everyone always has clients, and make player-dragons the prestige tier you graduate into.

### 114. Spawnwrights — FUN: 4/10
Hook: Seeing your restocked dungeon become the season's hot raid — your firm's name on the plaque at the entrance — is a legitimate builder's high.
Poison: You plant, then wait a real season for feedback the design itself admits is unreadable, so the correct play is to log off — the one thing a live game cannot ask.
Fix: Add running fieldwork — scout expeditions, monster censuses, tracks and scat to read — so the wait fills with detective verbs and early leading indicators.

### 115. Necropolis Staffing — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Inquisitors kicking your door in mid-contract while your skeletons keep hammering nails in the background is sitcom-grade dark comedy.
Poison: If raids stay arbitrary, the second unexplainable shutdown teaches players that effort doesn't matter, and they stop showing up to be raided.
Fix: Give each inquisitor a discoverable personality and pet peeves so paranoia matures into profiling — the raid you dodge because you knew Brother Aldous hates graveyard sourcing.

### 116. Caravanserai — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Recognizing your own stolen boots on the protection sergeant you've been paying — betrayal by your bodyguard is the story genre this game mass-produces.
Poison: For the un-betrayed majority the loop is a shipping menu with off-screen dice, and for the betrayed it's a huge loss with zero counterplay in the moment.
Fix: Let owners ride along or embed a spy in any caravan so the run itself becomes a playable, white-knuckle journey instead of a resolution roll.

### 117. The Cursed Goods Exchange — FUN: 5/10
Hook: The shopkeeper visibly accumulating curses — hair gone, shadow missing, cat refusing to enter — is streamable body-horror comedy that markets itself.
Poison: With zero tells before purchase the core buy is a scratch-off ticket, so players endure variance instead of expressing skill, and endured variance churns.
Fix: Add faint sensory tells — whispers, cold spots, how your lantern gutters near a crate — so buying becomes a learnable perceptual ghost-detective skill.

### 118. Familiar & Co. — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The wordless cat on your doorstep at dawn is the most gut-punch image in the batch — misery pre-formatted for memes and fan art.
Poison: Your failures accumulate as living animals staring at you from your own window — a permanent HUD of inadequacy that reads cute in a pitch and demoralizing by month two.
Fix: Give returned familiars redemption arcs — shop staff, second-chance placements, one that chooses to stay with you — so the failure stock becomes an evolving cast instead of a shame counter.

### 119. Chokepoint Concessions — FUN: 7/10
Hook: A poisoned party crawling into your torchlight to pay anything for antivenom — the vendor as angel and extortionist at once — is a perfect twenty-minute drama.
Poison: A flawless party walks past and your entire session was watching someone else be good at the game while your concession fee burns.
Fix: Give vendors in-run verbs — heckling, flash pricing, side-bets, reviving the downed for equity — so even a no-sale delve is a show you're performing in.

### 120. The Prophecy Pit — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Settlement day, when the whole server argues in open court what "the river will forget its name" meant while fortunes hang on the verdict, is communal theater at its best.
Poison: Losing a winning bet because a jury voted your interpretation down is the rage-quit moment, and whales will absolutely try to buy juries.
Fix: Use staked, randomly-drawn, non-bettor juries with a public arguments phase so adjudication becomes legitimate courtroom drama instead of a vibes coin-flip.

### 121. Both Banners — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Walking out of a two-year alliance war rich while both sides burn — the war-profiteer fantasy consummated — is an endgame players will scheme toward for months.
Poison: Punishment arrives as unexplained loss weeks after the sin, so newer players experience it as arbitrary ruin from a game that refuses to say what it wants.
Fix: Sell informants — expensive, unreliable, occasionally doubled — who leak glimpses of the faction files, turning the unreadable ledger into an espionage minigame.

### 122. The Quest Board — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Every lie, feud, and botched quest in town crosses your desk first — the board owner gets a front-row seat to all the server's drama and gets paid for it.
Poison: The core tragedy means your best relationships permanently leave you, a structurally Sisyphean loop where playing well slowly drains the game of the people you liked.
Fix: Give leavers a graduation ritual that pays you — alumni plaque, referral cut, farewell fee — so disintermediation lands as bittersweet victory instead of slow theft.

### 123. Patch Eve Outfitters — FUN: 8/10
Hook: The patch-day warehouse reveal — the entire server learning simultaneously whose stockpiles are gold and whose are landfill — is a perfect recurring communal spike of screams and schadenfreude.
Poison: Between patches you're just running a normal shop, and if the leaks are pure noise the whipsaw feels like taxation by developer dice.
Fix: Run weekly mini-patches beneath one big seasonal patch so speculation is a constant drumbeat of small bets instead of a quarterly lottery with homework.

### 124. The Effluvium Cartel — FUN: 5/10
Hook: A rival's wagon leveling the market square while the whole server sprints over to gawk at the crater is the most spectacular failure state in the batch — schadenfreude on tap.
Poison: Your own learning curve is "explode in public until literate," and the day the mix-chart hits Discord this becomes a fantasy trucking sim with paperwork.
Fix: Drift the alchemy per-region and per-season so safety literacy is local and perishable — haulers keep gossiping, craters keep happening, and no spreadsheet stays true.

### 125. The Silent Partner — FUN: 5/10
Hook: The update letter that finally, in paragraph four, admits the monster-ranch "incident" you'd been reading between the lines about for weeks is a genuinely delicious slow reveal.
Poison: The playable surface is an inbox and a checkbook — whole sessions can pass without a single decision, and being lied to in prose is only fun when the prose is good.
Fix: Add diligence verbs — hire snoops, visit ventures in disguise, cross-reference gossip across your portfolio — so between letters you play detective instead of voicemail.
