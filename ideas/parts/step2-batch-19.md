### 451. Corner Ghost Market — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Location is a hypothesis you can only test by paying for it, and your best discoveries get competed away the moment they work — edge decays, exactly like real distribution channels.
Cheats: The bare receipt tape is only epistemic if real player foot traffic is dense enough to carry signal; in a thin city the game silently becomes a slot machine wearing an AR skin.
Note: Word-of-mouth location lore self-corrects degeneration — any corner that hits the wiki gets undercut by rivals overnight, which is the actual lesson about arbitrage closing.

### 452. Proofing Overnight — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Irreversible commitment ahead of demand — you bet tonight and live with it at dawn, the truest small-business inventory lesson there is.
Cheats: "Standing orders cancelled without explanation" is only load-bearing if cancellations trace to real player behavior with causes; otherwise the dawn verdict is a coin flip and the tension is manufactured, not earned.
Note: Solo forecasting loop with little strategic surface — once you've mapped your handful of neighbor-customers, this degenerates into a habit app with bread on it.

### 453. Dead Drop Freight — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: The pipeline-uncertainty problem — you cannot distinguish a slow deal from a dead one, and founders burn months on exactly that ambiguity.
Cheats: Blacklisting places instead of people is clever but dodges the harder entrepreneurial skill of judging counterparties; geography absorbs the blame humans deserve.
Note: Epistemic (drop reliability is learnable and shifts with live players), but weak on customers, pricing, or unit economics — it teaches logistics patience, not judgment.

### 454. Pardon Our Dust — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Betting on illegible external timelines and buying folk expertise with no warranty — the guru/accelerator economy rendered honestly, advice sold as-is.
Cheats: If construction durations are hidden RNG timers rather than a system with readable tells (cranes, permits), the "veteran instinct" is astrology and the game rewards confident nonsense.
Note: The instinct-selling market is the anti-degeneration engine — half-wrong paid intel keeps the wiki permanently unreliable, which is realistic and structurally sound.

### 455. The Empty Chair — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Iteration is expensive because experiments run in calendar time — a pricing test costs a real week, which is the single most under-taught startup truth.
Cheats: The "service" being sold is hand-waved, so demand may be circular (players buying slots to sell slots), hollowing the unit-economics lesson into pure schedule Tetris.
Note: Epistemic — the 3pm-vs-4pm mystery is learnable from real player rhythms — but the slow feedback loop that makes it honest also makes it a retention graveyard.

### 456. Blockholders — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Market discovery by losing money on the wrong offering — validation-by-loss is real and painful.
Cheats: Real neighborhoods get demographically mapped by the community within weeks, permanently killing the "invisible demographics" premise for everyone after the first cohort.
Note: High degeneration risk plus land-grab first-mover lock-in — late joiners inherit a solved map and a landlord class, which teaches feudalism, not entrepreneurship.

### 457. Skyline Auction House — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Marketing attribution fog — bidding real money into aggregated, three-day-late data is literally how ad spend works, and calibrating sealed bids with no losing feedback is genuine belief-formation.
Cheats: With zero information on losses, early-game learning is so starved that new players churn before the epistemic loop ever closes.
Note: Cartels-and-betrayal is the anti-wiki mechanism — collusion equilibria are player-made and unstable, so the meta can't be solved from outside.

### 458. Meridian Traders — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Delegation without observability — hiring strangers sight unseen and auditing them only through your balance is the remote-agency problem every founder eventually eats.
Cheats: "Your edge is being awake when others aren't" quietly rewards sleep deprivation and 24/7 co-ops, turning trust calibration into a coverage grind.
Note: Trust in specific people is epistemic and per-person learnable; the commodity layer underneath risks collapsing into standard market-bot meta once spreadsheeted.

### 459. Flashfront — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: The emotional truth of zero customers — standing physically present at a stall nobody visits is the most honest founder simulator moment in the batch.
Cheats: Beyond "show up on a rhythm," there is almost no decision surface — it taxes presence, not judgment, and physical check-in is grinding an attribute with your body.
Note: The loyalty-rhythm players "feel but never articulate" is either a rich hidden system or vibes-flavored RNG; the pitch never commits, and the game lives or dies on that.

### 460. The Sourcing Eye — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: You never learn whether the design or the timing killed you — unattributable failure in a taste market, plus paid intel that is half lies, forcing real trust calibration.
Cheats: Photograph-to-mint invites content spam floods where volume beats taste, and if listing is cheap the silence carries no cash-flow sting.
Note: Structurally degeneration-proof — trends are raw live player taste, so there is no wiki to write, only scouts to distrust; genuinely epistemic and permanently shifting.

### 461. On Consignment — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Designing your own audits — seeding identical goods with rival hosts and comparing the silences is actual entrepreneurial experiment design, the principal-agent problem made playable.
Cheats: Honest hosts have no mechanism to prove honesty, so the market can lemons-spiral into universal distrust and the game economy dies of its own realism.
Note: Deeply epistemic (host behavior is learnable per-person) and MMO-native; watch the AFK-passive-income meta, which could reduce craft to portfolio spreadsheets.

### 462. Standing Offer — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Three indistinguishable failure modes — wrong price, wrong corner, or no supply — and the discipline of designing experiments that separate them one variable at a time.
Cheats: "Weeks of nothing" in a genuinely dead district is unresolvable pure noise no experiment can escape in tolerable time; the game punishes players for a map fact they cannot learn.
Note: Buy-side framing is refreshingly original, but pacing is brutal enough that the lesson will mostly be taught to players who already quit.

### 463. Lanes — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: Reliability is an invisible decaying asset — clients notice your slippage before you do, which is true of every service business.
Cheats: You cannot pivot your commute — the core "business decision" is having a regular life, which is grinding a character attribute with your legs and calendar.
Note: Degenerates into routine-havers winning by default; the coffee-corridor discovery is epistemic once, then permanent, with no reason for the answer to ever shift.

### 464. Dead Air — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Creating into a void with week-late lagging metrics — the content-creator cold start, which is a legitimate modern founder experience.
Cheats: Sponsors pulling money "without warning or reason" is only a lesson if driven by real player-business budgets; as written it reads as pure RNG dressed up as market cruelty.
Note: Demands actual audio production from players while starving them of feedback — the honesty of the loop is exactly what will make nobody play it.

### 465. Gutter Gold — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Inferring upstream economy health from one noisy derivative signal — a bare tonnage number — which is precisely how B2B suppliers actually read their customers.
Cheats: Between quarterly auctions the player mostly watches a number and theorizes; diagnosis without decisions is spectating, not entrepreneurship.
Note: Genuinely epistemic because tonnage derives from live player economies, but the action loop is too thin to carry the excellent inference lesson.

### 466. Night Ledger — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: Intimacy with a tiny market — knowing a small moody crowd personally beats analytics, a real small-business truth.
Cheats: A crowd this small makes nightly variance statistically indistinguishable from noise, so the "moody taste" reads as dice, and the design literally charges sleep as an entry fee.
Note: "Your spot taken with no record of by whom" is cheap opacity for its own sake — withholding information that any real merchant would trivially have.

### 467. Field Agents — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: When there is no arbiter and no transcript, reputation is the only enforceable contract — pricing counterparty risk into blind quotes is real agency-business judgment.
Cheats: The same no-arbiter purity invites a scammer meta that can lemons-collapse the whole bounty market, and the design offers no immune system beyond hope.
Note: Structurally degeneration-resistant — trust networks are player-specific and non-wikiable — and the uncertainty is fully epistemic: every agent is a learnable human.

### 468. Ghost Landlord — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: Pricing with no comparables and absorbing exogenous shocks (the bus route moved) — reading the real world as your risk surface.
Cheats: Landlording is rent extraction layered on players doing the actual entrepreneurship, and the community will crowdsource a comps spreadsheet within a month, deleting the entire premise.
Note: The degeneration is structural, not patchable — "no vacancy data" cannot survive players talking to each other, which they will, immediately.

### 469. Foot Fleet — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: Committing capacity before you know what your week holds, and the beautiful detail that good service earns silent rebooking, not thanks.
Cheats: Step count is the definition of grinding a character attribute — the fit and the free get structural alpha, and rain or injury is RNG you can never learn from.
Note: The co-op politics over who's really walking is the one MMO-true mechanic; everything else is a pedometer app with default states.

### 470. The Sunrise Clearing — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Calibrating sealed bids from wins only — no losing feedback — while an intelligence-brokerage industry emerges because information scarcity is itself a business opportunity.
Cheats: The east-west timing asymmetry is structural unfairness by longitude, punishing players for their address rather than teaching them anything.
Note: The intel trade is anti-degenerative in the right way — the "wiki" becomes a paid, unreliable, player-run product, which is exactly what real market data is.

### 471. Sticker Empire — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: The overwhelming default outcome of any offer is silence — most stickers are never scanned by anyone, ever — which is the coldest, truest cold-outreach lesson in the batch.
Cheats: If the noise floor is that high and stickers are cheap, no single placement carries stakes, so players spray-and-pray instead of forming beliefs — volume replaces judgment.
Note: Epistemic underneath (scan rates track real foot traffic), but surface-heatmap wikis will form; the archaeological turf war is charming and load-bearing against them.

### 472. Press 2 for Prosperity — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Iterating a product from where-users-quit data alone, never why — funnel optimization on strangers who owe you nothing is startup analytics rendered with brutal fidelity.
Cheats: The pitch never explains why any real player calls your phone tree in the first place — without genuine caller intent the hang-up data is noise about nothing.
Note: Deeply epistemic and hard to wiki (every tree is a unique product), and trees-hiring-trees is a genuinely original composable-firm endgame; solve the demand side or it's a beautiful corpse.

### 473. While They Sleep — TEACH: 3/10
Teaches: Delegation across trust boundaries where betrayal is always discovered too late — a real lesson buried in the wrong game.
Cheats: "Market share" taken by attacking sleeping opponents is a conquest wargame in a business costume — no customers, no validation, no economics, just a war-dec timer keyed to bedtime.
Note: The dominant strategy is degrading your own sleep or outsourcing to mercenary time zones; that's not a cartoon of entrepreneurship, it's a cartoon of Eve Online.

### 474. Handshake Protocol — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Deals die of logistics and ghosting — the no-show investor who costs you the trip and tells you nothing is painfully, recognizably true.
Cheats: Gating all deal flow on physical proximity makes metro density a pay-to-win stat and rural players structurally uninvestable — geography as paywall, not lesson.
Note: Emergent player-run meetup exchanges are the best organic institution in the batch; the per-hour learning rate, though, is miserably low.

### 475. Annual Report — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Veterans are just people who have survived more winters — experience as the only teacher of seasonal judgment is a lesson every real operator would salute.
Cheats: One experiment per real year is pedagogically bankrupt inside a game — nearly all learning must arrive secondhand, and expecting twelve months of retention for one Christmas is fantasy.
Note: Epistemic in principle, but the feedback loop is so long that year-one seasonal playbooks will circulate and teach faster than the game ever can, hollowing out its own premise.
