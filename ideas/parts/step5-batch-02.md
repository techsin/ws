### 511. Dead Man's Lease

Pitch: You take the vacant unit on a twelve-seat street whose walls are papered with the ghost signage of every operator who died there, and the site's researchable history is your only prospectus — but your actual demand is invisibly warped by what your eleven neighbors sell, so the same crepe stand thrives on one street and starves on the next. The loop is: read the graveyard, pick a concept, trade tenant-mix votes over the next vacancy, and parse silent foot traffic to learn whether the street's chemistry loves you or is quietly strangling you. Uncertainty lives in unattributable adjacency — you can never cleanly separate "my product is wrong" from "the barber next door changed everything" — and rejection arrives as a district council of conscripted strangers voting to move the bus stop that fed your door. Minute 1 is moving into a haunted storefront and reading your predecessors' failure plaques; month 3 is being the street elder whose bloc vote decides which newcomer gets the cursed corner unit.
Guards: Wiki death is impossible because street chemistry is authored by eleven live neighbors, density is solved by pods that merge when thin (with a lobbyable NPC management company absorbing dead tenancies), and identity is expensive because your name is permanently welded to the plaque of every location you kill.

### 514. The Mutual

Pitch: You found or join a mutual credit society — the guild-pool IS the startup — where the founder authors the trust rituals (waiting periods, witness rules, claim hearings) and every member publicly attests solvency in reports they are allowed to lie in. ### 515. Masquerade Bazaar
