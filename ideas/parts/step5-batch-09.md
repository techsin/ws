### 581. The Shop That Wasn't There
Mix: 500(unmasking economy + purpose-built host) + 51(NPC/player ambiguity as population shock absorber) + 123(blind inventory commit before patch)
Pitch: A free loot-and-dungeon MMO where every vendor stall is either scripted or a human entrepreneur paying real rent, and the game never says which; merchants stock shelves, haggle live, and plant quest rumors while committing next season's inventory blind before announced-but-undocumented patches that reveal to the whole server at once. Adventurers get a detective metagame for free — suspicion-testing vendors and filing bounty-paid unmasking claims — and being confirmed human permanently reprices every negotiation you touch. Silence lives in the till: a dead sales day never tells you whether the meta moved, a rival undercut you, or foot traffic is simply thin. Minute 1 is haggling with someone who might be a person; month 3 is running a rumor-fed supply chain where your concealed identity is your most leveraged asset.
Guards: NPC vendor agents backfill any density so a 400-player server looks full, the patch cadence and unmasking cat-and-mouse are human-authored so no wiki ever stabilizes, adventurers are bounty-paid detectives rather than unpaid content, and identity is expensive because unmasking is permanent per shop charter.

### 582. Actuary of the Underworld
Mix: 104(risk products sold on other players' real gameplay) + 250(public graveyard as the only actuarial table) + 500(businesses embedded in a purpose-built host)
Pitch: In the host dungeon MMO death is expensive by design — corpse runs, gear loss — and the only relief is insurance sold at the gate by "temple clerks" who may or may not be human underwriters pricing policies with zero official statistics. The community-maintained graveyard of post-mortems is the server's only actuarial table, and it is actively poisoned by staged deaths and quiet alt transfers, so reading it critically is the core skill. Rejection is the applicant who takes your quote and walks wordlessly to the next temple; catharsis is the scrying room where rival underwriters watch insured runs live and talk trash like sports night. Month 3 you are running a fraud blacklist and lobbying graveyard editors — an actual insurance company.
Guards: Demand is structural because insurance IS the host's death mechanic (every adventurer is a customer at any density and their seat is a free game), NPC applicants backfill thin hours indistinguishably, the poisoned actuarial table can never be wiki-solved, and scrying rooms convert the wait between resolutions into a communal verb.

### 583. Rope & Ruin
Mix: 109(working capital that walks away + repo crews) + 382(unenforced forward contracts, whispered reputation) + 60(vouching-with-stake newcomer funnel)
Pitch: In the host MMO all serious gear is rented, never owned — your stall's mules and lanterns descend strapped to real parties who return rich, late, dead, or not at all, and non-return never explains itself. Nothing is enforced: deposits, return dates, and damage terms are handshakes you price yourself, newcomers can rent only when an insider stakes their own reputation vouching for them, and dawn at the gate is a timed rush-hour of sizing up strangers and refusing gut-call bad risks. When gear vanishes you hire adventurer repo crews to descend after it — paid emergent quests that surface with a tale either way. Month 3 your real inventory is a tradeable blacklist of names and hunches that is sometimes catastrophically wrong.
Guards: Rental-as-gearing makes outfitters load-bearing at any population (with NPC parties renting through dead hours), the credit surface lives in player heads rather than solvable tables, the dawn rush and repo tales replace silent-waiting with daily verbs, and vouch-stakes make alt identity laundering cost the voucher, not the system.

### 584. Patch Eve Prophets
Mix: 123(blind commit + simultaneous public reveal) + 359(planted-lie whisper network) + 45(information as a decaying, purchasable, poisonable resource)
Pitch: The host's patches are written by a live design cell that also deliberately leaks — each cycle it grants random adventurers genuine test-realm visions, grants others convincing fakes, and lets merchants buy testimony they can never fully verify. The merchant loop is running a rumor portfolio: purchasing, cross-checking, and reselling patch intelligence with a shelf life, then committing warehouse capital blind before the reveal screams across the server as a communal spectacle of genius and landfill. Rejection is your best source going silent the week it matters; uncertainty is never knowing whether the leak you paid for was marketing. Weekly mini-patches keep speculation a constant drumbeat of small bets beneath each seasonal earthquake.
Guards: Opacity is adversarially poisoned at the source so datamining channels and Discords inherit the poison instead of solving it, vision-selling makes adventurer witnesses a paid first-class seat, the reveal is a scheduled communal spike that works at any density, and mini-patches kill the one-payoff-per-month cadence.

### 585. The Teller Window
Mix: 357(unlabeled human/NPC behind the desk) + 147(creditor patience as hidden state in real lenders) + 500(embedded business + patch-day shocks)
Pitch: Raids in the host MMO are financed — parties borrow at the bank window before descending — and every teller is either script or a human moneylender whose patience, grudges, and rollover policy are private state a borrower meets only when it snaps. Lenders read applicants across the counter with no stats, price desperation, and decide nightly which late borrowers get a quiet extension and which get sold to the repo boards; borrowers can never confirm whether charm is working on this teller or whether anyone is even listening. Patch shocks reprice whole loan books overnight when the dungeon your collateral assumptions lived in gets rebalanced. Minute 1 is a tense cross-counter negotiation; month 3 is managing a book of relationships you cannot verify are human.
Guards: Credit is the host's run-economy so borrower demand exists at any density with NPC tellers absorbing off-peak hours, patience-state lives in human heads (and script mimicry of them) so it never wikis, both seats are full games — borrowing is bluffing, lending is underwriting — and ledger play is async so no timezone owns the window.

### 586. Billboards for a World That Ignores You
Mix: 122(disintermediation as the win-lose condition) + 444(population-trained banner blindness) + 132(read-receipts-then-nothing outreach)
Pitch: You run an advertising and routing agency inside the host — authoring quest rumors, tavern gossip, and signposts that steer real adventurer foot traffic past your merchant clients' doors. Client acquisition is cold outreach to other entrepreneurs, answered mostly by read receipts and nothing, and your creative decays as a live function of how many players copy it, because adventurers learn to ignore whatever everyone posts. Your best clients eventually learn the routes themselves and cut you out, so success at steering is priced as failure at retention, forcing perpetual new-client hunting and reinvention. Every rumor you plant resolves visibly within days: you literally watch the crowd turn left, or not.
Guards: Adventurers consume rumors as free flavor content so no seat is unpaid labor, banner blindness makes every tactic self-expiring so no template or wiki survives adoption, foot-traffic dashboards supply a daily verb between contracts, and outreach is costed and rate-limited so spam is unprofitable rather than merely banned.

### 587. Keys to a Dead Stall
Mix: 446(predecessor-authored world-state, salted CRM) + 498(post-bankruptcy haunting, era-locked advisors) + 500(host-embedded shops)
Pitch: When a host-world merchant goes under, the shop keeps trading blandly as a zombie NPC until someone buys the keys — the dead owner's ledgers, customer notes, and supplier grudges included, possibly salted with fake gold entries before the sale. The buyer excavates unreliable relationship archaeology under live rent pressure, while the bankrupt previous owner may haunt their old counter as an era-locked ghost advisor whose worldview froze at their death date and whose advice is stale exactly where the market has moved. Minute 1 is reading a stranger's paranoid margin notes; month 3 is discovering which of forty "loyal customers" were the dead owner's alts. Failure becomes a role in the world, not an uninstall.
Guards: Zombie-NPC continuation keeps shop density constant through churn and keeps every acquisition target live, salted books are per-shop and human-authored so nothing generalizes to a wiki, haunting converts busted players into retained ones, and note-stuffing fraud IS the due-diligence curriculum rather than an exploit around it.

### 588. The Balance Committee
Mix: 494(conscripted-stranger infrastructure councils) + 358(one server-wide macro variable) + 123(blind commit before the ruling lands)
Pitch: The host's seasonal patches are not fully dev-authored: a randomly conscripted council of adventurers and merchants votes on which regions buff, which dungeons open, and where the trade roads move, while a single server-wide prosperity rate driven by aggregate behavior sets everyone's borrowing costs. Merchants lobby councilors who might ghost them, might be scripted seat-fillers, and might be taking three bribes at once — then everyone commits inventory blind before the ruling drops as one public patch-day shock. The uncertainty is political: you can do everything right and lose to a backroom you never saw. Month 3 you keep a whisper file on forty past councilors and a healthy fear of committees.
Guards: Conscription hands every adventurer a real political turn (no unpaid seat) and rotates capture out of any cartel, human-voted patches cannot be datamined in advance, the macro rate stays felt-by-all and controlled-by-none at 400 or 10,000, and lobbying is async letter-craft so no timezone owns the council.

### 589. The Fence's Ledger
Mix: 500(embedded vendor + patch shocks) + 133(real, consistent, unpublished rules reconstructed from penalties) + 485(provenance-weighted rumor network)
Pitch: Adventurers must sell their loot through player-run fences, and the host's contraband law is real, consistent, and unpublished — you learn which goods are "hot" only when inspectors seize a shipment and fine you, and each seasonal inspector rotation quietly rewrites the code. The loop is pricing legal risk into every across-the-counter purchase, running cheap probe shipments to map the law before betting big, and trading provenance rumors through a network where rivals deliberately seed poison about what got whom caught. Rejection is the inspector who takes your ledger away and says nothing for six days. Month 3 you are a compliance department with a smuggler's heart, and your rumor sources are your actual balance sheet.
Guards: Fencing IS the host's vendor system so seller traffic is structural at any density, penalties-only rule discovery plus scheduled rotation puts an expiry clock on every wiki while rival poison keeps community intel permanently suspect, probe shipments give a daily experiment verb, and rulings arrive async so nobody must be online for the knock.

### 590. Sevenday Bazaar
Mix: 107(knowledge-only persistence across weekly wipes) + 500(businesses inside a host others play for fun) + 162(capital committed before a synchronized zero-sum demand window)
Pitch: The host world wipes every Monday — dungeons reroll, metas reset, adventurers happily restart a weekly race — but merchant charters persist, and stock must be committed Sunday night, blind, against a demand curve regenerated from last week's aggregate adventurer behavior. Nothing carries over but your notebook: hypotheses about how racers behave on day one versus day six, which no attribute can substitute for and which rots as ten thousand strangers adapt. Saturday's close is a synchronized server-wide reckoning of whose Sunday bets were genius and whose are landfill; the silence is midweek, when a misread meta simply, quietly, does not sell. Newcomers join every Monday on equal footing with veterans whose only edge is better notes.
Guards: The weekly wipe is a standing cold-start solution for both populations (fresh adventurer cohorts, no mature-server moat), demand regenerated from live behavior makes last week's wiki this week's trap, missing a week costs one cycle rather than your business since charter and notebook persist, and progression is purely epistemic — there is no number to grind.
