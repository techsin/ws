### 476. Overflight — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Demand sensing from weak, adversarially poisoned proxies — you form falsifiable beliefs about an economy nobody publishes and pay real money when you misread it.
Cheats: The entire venture collapses into one perception skill — photo forensics — while validation, cash flow, and actual customers wait offscreen as an afterthought.
Note: Epistemic and degeneration-resistant because rivals actively poison the signal, but it risks becoming a GeoGuessr-style pixel-peeping contest rather than a business.

### 477. The Fog Exchange — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Silence as data — the bid that sits untouched for an hour is the truest cold-outreach mechanic in the batch, forcing inference from your own fills alone.
Cheats: It teaches a trader's epistemics, not a founder's — no product, no customer, no runway, so "entrepreneurship" here is a costume on market microstructure.
Note: Fully epistemic and non-degenerating since the hidden system is other players groping back, but fill-rate sample sizes may be too thin to ever learn from — the line between fog and pure noise needs ruthless tuning.

### 478. No Clocks — TEACH: 3/10
Teaches: That deadlines you failed to internalize will gut you — the payroll-blindside feeling, manufactured through ambient-cue reading.
Cheats: It fabricates opacity exactly where real founders have none — every founder alive knows precisely when rent is due; the terror is whether they can pay it — so the core mechanic is a cartoon of the wrong uncertainty.
Note: Degenerates on contact: one shared community almanac spreadsheet solves the server's timescale and the whole game reverts to a normal tycoon with a hostile UI.

### 479. The Museum of Winners — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Survivorship bias as architecture — the locked basement of ten thousand failures that did the exact same things is the best one-shot lesson in the pool.
Cheats: It is a one-shot — the pitch itself lets diggers publish base rates, so the epiphany gets wikified within a month and the museum becomes a lobby attached to a venture game it never bothered to design.
Note: The teach lives in the framing, not the operating loop; after the aha, nothing about running the business outside enforces the lesson.

### 480. Guru Bazaar — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Trust calibration under unverifiable track records — separating skilled, lucky, and fraudulent advisors with your own runway as the test instrument.
Cheats: The whole edifice stands on an unbuilt foundation — if the underlying venture game is shallow, advice quality is undetectable in the noise and the loop collapses into buying lottery tickets.
Note: Structurally degeneration-resistant — reputation lists will emerge and then be gamed, which is precisely the real-world lesson; the hidden system is other players, so it never solves.

### 481. The Luck Ledger — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: Anti-resulting — outcomes lie, process matters, and the richest player ranked 400th is a genuinely great poster.
Cheats: Grading "decision quality" requires an oracle that knows the true odds, meaning the game is a solvable EV table in a startup costume — it hands out quarterly the one thing real entrepreneurship never gives you.
Note: Degenerates completely: the community reverse-engineers the odds table and it becomes wiki-following EV homework; the uncertainty is dice, not beliefs.

### 482. Hands & Eyes — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: Briefing under bandwidth constraint — a faint echo of delegation and founder-operator trust.
Cheats: Ten-word notes are a party-game gimmick no firm has ever faced; it is a co-op compression puzzle with a business skin — nobody validates, sells, or runs out of money interestingly here.
Note: Degenerates into shared codebooks — once community-standard ten-word ciphers circulate, the constraint that is the entire game is dead.

### 483. Vitrine — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Marketing causal inference with confounded, unprovable attribution — "did the blue cloth work" is the honest daily texture of real demand generation.
Cheats: At street-level sample sizes with drifting player tastes, the causal signal may be unrecoverable in principle — at which point it stops teaching humility and starts teaching helplessness, i.e., RNG with curtains.
Note: Epistemic only if foot traffic is tuned high enough to learn from; non-degenerating because the interpreters are live players whose semiotics keep shifting.

### 484. No Second Run — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Knowledge as the only capital — deciding whether to trust a dead stranger's notebook is genuine secondhand-diligence judgment.
Cheats: "Deep but knowable" static rules are a countdown — the notebooks converge into a verified community wiki, the trust decision evaporates, and the anti-meta-progression manifesto becomes a walkthrough-following game.
Note: Permadeath-without-acknowledgment is honest, but the design's central act self-destructs on exactly the timescale gaming communities are most competent at.

### 485. Whisper Street — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: That markets run on unverifiable human information — provenance-weighting, poison detection, and the discovery that your reputation is your real balance sheet.
Cheats: Out-of-game Discords and player-run price oracles will pierce the fog — though the design half-absorbs the blow, since a trusted oracle is just a powerful in-fiction node by another name.
Note: Fully epistemic and permanently shifting because the hidden system is other players positioning against you; silence — nobody owes you any data — is genuinely load-bearing.

### 486. The Failure Number — TEACH: 4/10
Teaches: Goodhart's law and reflexivity — being priced off a metric moves the metric, which is real credit-score and ARR-multiple physics.
Cheats: Real founders get no honest oracle, and handing one out converts the whole game into optimizing a single visible stat — the attribute-grind failure mode wearing a memento-mori mask.
Note: Community regression-mining will decode what moves the Number and turn it into a wiki build-order; "never explained" is a bluff the playerbase will call within weeks.

### 487. The Fan of Fates — TEACH: 5/10
Teaches: Luck made visceral — seeing your triumph rendered as a 94th-percentile fluke is a lesson most real founders never get even once.
Cheats: Rendering a thousand counterfactual yous requires a fully simulated generative model, which quietly deletes the MMO — real rival players cannot be replayed — so the profound chart is only true of a dice world, not a market.
Note: One magnificent aha, then it degenerates into EV-grinding with a prettier report card; the status economy around fat right-shifted distributions is the only durable piece.

### 488. The Herd — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Cash flow as something you can hear starving — payroll eating animals monthly makes runway visceral in a way no dashboard ever has.
Cheats: It is metaphor-skinning — the herd is a resource bar with legs, the uncertainty is weather-and-rustler RNG, and no belief about any market ever gets formed or tested.
Note: Handshake breeding-stock credit is real trust calibration with real players; the rest risks being a logistics survival game in a poncho.

### 489. Wire Deals — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Negotiating under permanently unverifiable identity claims — counterparty reading with no resolution even after close mirrors how real deals die unexplained.
Cheats: Refusing to ever resolve the fog means the game can never confirm your reads, so learning rides entirely on slow long-horizon pattern memory — and bots will flatten style-recognition into noise.
Note: Epistemic and adversarially shifting; the biggest degeneration threat is out-of-game stylometry tooling deanonymizing the wire.

### 490. The Lying Manual — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: The founder's core scar — confidently delivered best practices that are specifically wrong, discoverable only by betting your business and doing the paranoid re-read afterward.
Cheats: The MMO is vestigial — the multiplayer is a shared annotation project rather than customers and competitors — and "wrong" is only meaningful if the underlying sim is deep enough to make the guru's lies falsifiable instead of arbitrary.
Note: The estate re-poisoning settled knowledge with revised editions is a genuine structural anti-wiki engine — rare in this pool, and exactly what degeneration-proofing looks like.

### 491. Strata — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: The backtest trap — richly legible historical demand that may describe a dead regime is non-stationarity taught mechanically, and "everyone is also a fossil" is a real epiphany.
Cheats: Your customers are recorded ghosts, so the live-player market the product demands is deferred to a hand-waved surface layer — who actually pays you today is the unpitched half of the game.
Note: Degeneration-resistant by construction since live behavior keeps depositing new strata; the uncertainty is fully epistemic and self-renewing.

### 492. Burn to Speak — TEACH: 6/10
Teaches: Costly signaling in the flesh — quality claims mean nothing unless they hurt, and the newcomer who cannot afford to say anything is the honest cold-start problem.
Cheats: One econ-seminar theorem inflated into an entire game — entrepreneurship reduced to bonfire sizing, and the poor-but-honest player's experience is designed misery with no counterplay.
Note: The counterfeit arms race keeps the signaling equilibrium moving, so it resists wikification better than it first appears; epistemic for customers, punishing for sellers.

### 493. Anecdata — TEACH: 8/10
Teaches: Statistical humility under N=1 — resisting the overcorrection reflex that genuinely kills companies who reroute roadmaps around one furious email.
Cheats: A single daily sample may be too little signal to ever act on rationally, so the optimal strategy could be ignoring the anecdote entirely — a game best played by not engaging with its one mechanic.
Note: Epistemic and non-degenerating — customers are live players and the folk census is unverifiable and lie-able by design; the absence of aggregates is genuinely load-bearing.

### 494. Eminent Domain — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Platform and regulatory risk — your distribution is a bridge a committee of strangers can vote away, so you learn lobbying, hedging, and building resilience over location bets.
Cheats: Randomly conscripted councils may vote as chaos rather than politics, tipping the uncertainty from readable-people epistemics into pure civic dice.
Note: Vote-trading cartels are the inevitable degeneration — and also an accurate simulation of zoning boards, so the rot is arguably the curriculum.

### 495. Control Group — TEACH: 7/10
Teaches: Causal attribution — was it me or the market — via a counterfactual twin, plus the meta-lesson that counterfactuals decay and trusting your own baseline is itself a judgment call.
Cheats: It gifts founders the one thing reality never provides — a control — and only partially earns forgiveness by corroding the gift with drift.
Note: Epistemic and per-company, so hard to wikify; the danger is it plays as an A/B-testing seminar rather than a company you grieve losing.

### 496. The Seeded Deck — TEACH: 3/10
Teaches: Attribution under adversity — deciding whether the strike was weather or an enemy is a real, if narrow, judgment.
Cheats: Real markets almost never sabotage you on purpose, so this trains malice-attribution — an anti-lesson that makes players worse founders — atop mechanics that are literally purchasable dice-griefing.
Note: It is Diplomacy-flavored whodunnit in a business skin; the uncertainty is opponent mischief, not a market you can form beliefs about.

### 497. Honesty Box — TEACH: 7/10

### 499. Same Product, Different Day — TEACH: 5/10

Note: The uncertainty is learnable-but-permanently-repatched — the exact structure of real markets — and being unmasked as human adds reflexive social epistemics no wiki can solve; degeneration is continuously reset by the host's balance team.
