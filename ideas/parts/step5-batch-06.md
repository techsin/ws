### 551. Probate Row
Mix: 446(predecessor-authored world-state) + 138(generational ledger compile) + 250(public graveyard as actuarial table) + 208(adverse-selection auctions)
Pitch: Every dead venture enters probate as an estate — its annotated CRM, cryptic ledger, debts, and grudges compiled automatically from the departed player's real actions — and newcomers start not by founding but by bidding on estates at adverse-selection auctions where the graveyard's public post-mortem is the only prospectus. The loop is archaeology under payroll pressure: test which inherited notes are gold, which are three years stale, and which were salted by the dying owner to pump the price, while live counterparties (backed by persistent NPC memory when absent) test whether you honor promises that appear nowhere in the record. Silence lives in accounts that never call back and debts that may or may not exist; rejection arrives as a customer who remembers a commitment you cannot find. Minute 1 is opening a dead stranger's ledger and cold-calling "ask about the boat"; month 3 is realizing your own annotations are compiling into somebody's future inheritance — and writing them accordingly.
Guards: Wiki-proof because every estate is a unique human-authored artifact; cold-start inverts (older server = richer probate docket); auctions run async all week so no timezone lock; note-stuffing fraud is the caveat-emptor lesson the graveyard teaches buyers to price.

### 552. Lifeline

Pitch: Ventures run on hard runway clocks, and terminal decline opens a 72-hour public deathwatch where any stranger can wire a rescue — but a lifeline is a stake, not charity: each player holds exactly one per season, and wiring it seats you as a director with real claw-back power in the survivor's company. The dying player's verbs are triage: spend your final runway-priced meetings pitching watchers who mostly stay silent, choose which pre-death promises to honor, and stage-manage your own spectacle. Conduct memory cuts both ways — founders who burned rescuers die unwatched next time, and rescuers who governed like tyrants find their wire refused. Minute 1 is lurking a stranger's deathwatch deciding whether they deserve your only lifeline; month 3 is your own vigil, watching the silent spectator counter and not knowing whether one of them is holding.
Guards: One-lifeline scarcity voids insurance-ring collusion; 72-hour async windows with escrowed pledges defeat timezone locks and off-peak density; unrescued death routes into a purgatory role instead of churn, giving the misery catharsis and the game retention.

### 553. Coroner's Office

Minute 1 is a true-crime autopsy of a real player's collapse; month 3 is founders privately lobbying you to rule their death "bad luck" so their next raise survives.
Guards: The archive cannot be wikified because it is player-authored, drifting, and profitably poisonable (staged deaths ARE the survivorship-bias lesson); coroner work is fully async and solo-paced, so it survives 400-player off-peak while solving newcomer cold-start outright.

### 555. Foreclosure Sunday

### 560. Vulture Season

Pitch: When a venture dies its customers do not despawn — they are real players whose supply chains just broke — and the public funeral doubles as the server's fiercest lead-gen event: rivals circle the orphan book, pitching strangers who are grieving, furious, and holding promises the dead firm made that were never written down.
