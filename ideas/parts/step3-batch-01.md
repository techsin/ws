### 1. Reply-All Ghosts — FUN: 4/10
Hook: Getting scammed on a stale lead list by another player and dragging the seller through the forum is the lemon-market drama people will actually retell.
Poison: The core verb is writing cold emails into a void — literally the soul-crushing job people play games to escape, with silence as the modal reward and no catharsis valve.
Fix: Make replies rare jackpot events that open into playable live negotiation threads, so every send becomes a lottery ticket with a visible, thrilling payout state instead of a pixel that maybe fires.

### 2. The Dashboard Lies — FUN: 3/10
Hook: Publicly catching a rival inflating their benchmark numbers and watching their reputation crater is the one moment with teeth.
Poison: Moment-to-moment play is staring at noisy charts and moving budget sliders — a game that is indistinguishable from the worst hour of an analyst's workday, in minute 1 and hour 100 alike.
Fix: Make instrumentation diegetic and playable — customer interviews, spy visits, support-call eavesdropping as short scenes — so gathering signal is an act you perform, not a line item you buy.

### 3. Ghost Round — FUN: 7/10
Hook: The whisper network — cracking a fund's secret thesis from demo-day crumbs and sniping the round before your rivals connect the dots — is detective-plus-gossip gold.
Poison: Founder-side fun is hostage to a healthy, engaged VC-player population; if that side thins or trolls, weeks of pitching become unpaid theater performed for an empty room.
Fix: Let every player simultaneously run a founder and a small angel checkbook, so nobody is ever purely waiting on the other population and everyone feels both sides of the ghosting.

### 4. Runway — FUN: 8/10
Hook: Losing the final partner meeting with three days of runway left because the boss pattern-matched your deck to last month's dead meta is an instantly retellable roguelike death.
Poison: Once the community maps the cycle, it risks becoming just another solved deck-builder with startup art, where the pitch flavor stops mattering and runs feel like grinding the meta tracker.
Fix: Let real investor-players occasionally guest-pilot boss seats in high-stakes runs (with their own scoring incentive), injecting an unwikiable human table-read into the run structure.

### 5. Cap Table — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The post-signing reveal that your opponent's fund was desperate and you left 2x on the table — or that you fleeced them — is a real gasp-or-groan beat.
Poison: Clause-by-clause legalese is homework-flavored, and the audience that finds liquidation preference inherently thrilling is measured in thousands, not the tens of thousands a ladder needs.
Fix: End every match with a fast poker-style showdown replay of both hidden hands and bluffs, so the emotional payoff is automatic, visual, and clippable rather than buried in a term sheet.

### 6. Churnyard — FUN: 4/10
Hook: The day a major guild adopts your tool and your MRR curve goes vertical on a graph other players can see is a genuine screenshot moment.
Poison: Silent churn with no confrontation and no explanation is misery without catharsis — the design takes the single worst feeling in SaaS and makes it the entire emotional diet.
Fix: Add rare overheard "exit interview" moments — churned customers occasionally surface in shared spaces muttering why they left — converting the void into detective breadcrumbs and dark comedy.

### 7. API Wars — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Shipping a breaking change and watching the whole dependent economy convulse — or being the whale-dropped victim the forum spends a week theorizing about — is EVE-grade drama.
Poison: The minute-to-minute is watching usage graphs, and late joiners arrive into an ossified political map where the only move is supplicating incumbents, which is fun for exactly nobody new.
Fix: Run seasonal protocol eras that periodically scramble the dependency graph, keeping platform politics liquid and giving newcomers a recurring fresh land-rush to surf.

### 8. Cold Start — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Watching a stubbornly dead neighborhood finally ignite on the city map after weeks of subsidy seeding is a slow-burn payoff worth the screenshot.
Poison: Most experiments are cash burned into silent streets — a slot machine that usually pays nothing and never explains — and having your supply side poached can feel like an unrecoverable mugging.
Fix: Give poaching a counterplay window (loyalty plays, win-back offers, public raids) so supply wars become visible rivalry arcs with comeback stories instead of quiet theft.

### 9. Standup — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Sharing your "Day 47: died to a payroll miss" grid and discovering your friend's ghost company survived the identical market by one different Tuesday call is perfect argument fuel.
Poison: One menu-pick per day risks feeling like a horoscope — too little authorship for deaths to feel earned, and foggy attribution reads as arbitrary when your agency was a single click.
Fix: Make the daily move a composed play — allocate three chips across fronts plus a one-line rationale that resurfaces verbatim in your postmortem — so the death grid tells a story you wrote.

### 10. Post-Mortem — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Death as a loot drop — the server watching your company die and your verified fact get inscribed in the codex with your name on it — makes losing the best moment in the game.
Poison: The actual run between deaths is an undefined vanilla startup sim, so as pitched this is a brilliant meta-layer wrapped around a hollow core verb.
Fix: Let players stake reputation betting on which codex entries still hold, so each economy drift becomes a server-wide market crash of received wisdom with public winners and losers.

### 11. The Feed — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Your guild's forbidden growth hack printing money for exactly nine days before the server piles in and kills it is a repeatable gold-rush story with built-in dark comedy.
Poison: For the unlucky majority the loop is shipping variants into an indifferent slot machine, and rank-twitch watching trains compulsive checking rather than play.
Fix: Broadcast retraining events as visible earthquakes — a shared land-grab season with leaderboards — so algorithm shifts are communal spectacles instead of private, unexplained dips.

### 12. Voicemail — FUN: 7/10
Hook: The clip of you sweet-talking past a hostile LLM receptionist in your best founder voice is shareable comedy the community will generate endlessly.
Poison: Three real weeks of calls to learn your champion never had budget is a punchline with a fatal setup length, and voice-only play excludes commutes, offices, and anyone with roommates.
Fix: Make every call drop intel fragments for the shared org-chart map regardless of outcome, so even voicemail purgatory pays a small collectible dividend and no session ends at zero.

### 13. Burn — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Split bank authority in co-op — two cofounders screaming over which invoice to pay as the payroll monster's midnight approaches — is Overcooked with money and friendships at stake.
Poison: Explicitly unlearnable revenue RNG means deaths feel dealt rather than earned, and horror with no mastery curve curdles into resentment by hour ten.
Fix: Give each client discoverable payment tells so veterans learn to read the dark — the dread stays, but survival becomes a skill you can brag about instead of a dice roll.

### 14. Term Sheet Poker — FUN: 8/10
Hook: The showdown flip revealing someone's traction cards were pure vapor after you funded them is a betrayal your group will relitigate over drinks for weeks.
Poison: It needs four assembled, startup-literate friends on voice — a brutal population precondition — and with matchmade strangers the trust drama that carries the game evaporates.
Fix: Ship a ten-minute quick-hand mode with persistent cross-hand reputation ladders in public lobbies, so strangers accumulate the betrayal history the fun depends on.

### 15. Fake Door — FUN: 5/10
Hook: Flipping a validated concept on the exchange at peak hype hours before the signal evaporates is a legal pump-and-dump with a straight face — pure trader adrenaline.
Poison: The maker loop is A/B-testing landing pages — a job, not a verb — and the dread mechanic rug-pulls diligent sellers without explanation, punishing exactly the players who engaged deepest.
Fix: Lean all the way into the exchange — public price charts, short-selling rivals' concepts, seller reputation ratings — so market drama and lemon-hunting carry sessions.

### 16. Null Result — FUN: 3/10
Hook: The eureka of a staggered-launch control cleanly proving "wrong audience, not bad product" is real detective catharsis — for the first solver.
Poison: A game whose primary feedback is literally nothing happening is anti-juice, and the pitch celebrates its own death: 90% of players will run a published route and feel neither mystery nor payoff.
Fix: Procedurally reroll every player's market instance so published designs become techniques to adapt rather than answers to copy, keeping the detective work first-hand for everyone.

### 17. Warm Intro — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Becoming a kingmaker node whose intro chains are courted — or burning the founder who flamed out with your name attached — is court-intrigue gossip fuel of the highest grade.
Poison: New players start locked out of the game's only verb, and by month three the fun belongs to a dozen oligarchs while everyone else experiences a velvet rope simulator.
Fix: Seed a constant stream of small deals that only pay when routed through low-connection players, forcing incumbents to compete at recruiting the bottom of the graph.

### 18. Ticket Queue — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The Papers-Please rush of spotting the one real signal ticket in a flood of noise — then learning a rival planted the decoys you fell for — is paranoia done right.
Poison: Reading fake support tickets for a hundred hours is data-entry cosplay unless the writing is consistently brilliant, and a purely reactive player never gets a power-fantasy beat.
Fix: Let deductions become visible moves — publicly accuse a rival of sabotage or ship a mid-crisis fix and watch the queue's tone shift within the same session — so inference closes into action fast.

### 19. Soft Landing — FUN: 4/10
Hook: Walking a doomed company to a fire-sale that somehow nets 2x, then telling the story of the landmine you found in the books, is a genuinely novel triumph fantasy.
Poison: Counterparties going dark for real weeks mid-diligence parks the player in an unplayed waiting room at precisely the moment tension should peak — the pacing is a retention guillotine.
Fix: Run three exit tracks in parallel on staggered rhythms so some table is always warm, and silence on one track becomes readable strategic information instead of dead air.

### 20. Replumb — FUN: 8/10
Hook: The mid-pivot crisis — half your machine dismantled, cash pressure falling, revenue still dripping through old pipes you haven't dared cut — is the best tension screenshot in the batch.
Poison: Fluid demand behind crude gauges frustrates the exact optimizer audience Factorio verbs attract; they came to solve, and the game deliberately withholds the numbers.
Fix: Make measurement itself buildable — better gauges, tracer dyes, A/B valves as machine subsystems — so epistemic uncertainty becomes more factory to engineer rather than fog imposed from outside.

### 21. Cofounder — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Discovering your cofounder took the secret solo acquisition on day 23 produces the single best water-cooler betrayal stories in the entire pool.
Poison: The modal failure is a partner silently quitting, sentencing you to weeks of solo-running a two-person game — abandonment as a game state is simply unplayable dead time.
Fix: Make abandonment a playable phase — vesting clawbacks, emergency recruitment from a pool of fellow abandoned founders, and a "survivor arc" scoreboard — so betrayal opens a chapter instead of ending the book.

### 22. Compliance — FUN: 5/10
Hook: The snitch button — weaponizing a rival's non-compliance and watching the regulator descend on them — is exquisite schadenfreude the forum will never stop litigating.
Poison: Ninety real days of silence on your own filing is retention suicide, and silently patched rules make the community's hard-won archaeology feel like the DM cheating.
Fix: Compress regulatory time to a quarter per real week and leak cryptic case-status breadcrumbs, so waiting reads as a ticking mystery to decode instead of a void to endure.

### 23. Out of Office — FUN: 4/10
Hook: A simulated investor reply landing on your real lock screen at 11pm is the most viscerally real notification any game has ever sent — screenshot guaranteed.
Poison: A game that punishes you for being at dinner is an anxiety subscription; it gets uninstalled the first time it costs the player a real human moment, which is week two at the latest.
Fix: Replace hard reply-windows with a player-scheduled daily "office hours" block where queued crises resolve in one juicy ten-minute triage session — ambient world, bounded obligation.

### 24. Board Deck — FUN: 3/10
Hook: Sweating through your framed metrics in front of real investor-players who might smell the spin is legitimate performance-drama — once a month.
Poison: One gameplay beat per month while the sim runs itself makes players spectators to their own company 29 days out of 30 — a cadence no live game has ever survived.
Fix: Inject weekly micro-fires — a leaked metric, a director's off-cycle question — that force interim spin decisions, so narrative debt is something you feel compounding between meetings.

### 25. Fork Threat — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The fork war — a rival forks your repo, contributors publicly pick sides, and the whole server watches two download graphs race — is a spectacular communal drama.
Poison: Between crises the loop is issue triage and mood divination, which gamifies the exact unpaid maintainer chores the target audience already resents doing in real life.
Fix: Make contributors named, courtable, poachable characters so the between-crisis game is roster-building politics with faces and grudges rather than inbox weeding.
