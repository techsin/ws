# Step 5 — 100 Hybrid Ideas (#501–600)

New designs mixing mechanics from the critiqued 500, built by ten agents with distinct strategies: KEEP-fusion, chassis+organs, silence stack, anti-wiki engines, economy-of-careers, failure afterlife, lifestyle-native async, every-seat-a-game, host-world gambit, wildcards. Each hybrid cites parent mechanics and guards against the five established failure modes (wiki death, density cliffs, lifestyle hostility, identity cheapness, retention graveyard).

### 501. Patchday Bazaar
Mix: 500(unmasking economy + purpose-built host game) + 382(unenforced forward contracts, whispered reputation) + 148(wear-reveal delay + Inspection Day ritual)
Pitch: You run a shop hidden among NPC vendors inside a purpose-built free adventurer MMO, and everything upstream of your stall — stock, credit, delivery dates — is an unenforced handshake contract with another hidden merchant, so the game never enforces promises and trust is priced entirely in whispers; counterfeit stock you knowingly or unknowingly moved reveals itself days later on the adventurer who bought it, detonating your name on a delay. Uncertainty lives in patch notes that rewrite adventurer demand on a schedule no wiki can front-run, in suppliers who simply stop answering, and in the adventurer who walks mid-haggle. Minute 1 is fun because the adventurer game is genuinely good and free (you are someone's content before you ever open a shop); month 3 you're managing a forward-contract supply web where being unmasked as human permanently reprices every negotiation, and the weekly Inspection Day concentrates accusations and downfalls into appointment theater. The fusion is stronger than its parents because Shopkeeper finally gets a real economy behind the counter (382's trust physics) and Handshake Futures finally gets a demand engine that isn't other speculators (500's adventurer population).
Guards: The purpose-built free host game solves density (no unpaid seat — adventurers are having fun), patch-shocks plus human-authored trust defeat wiki death, and scarce shop charters bought with played history make identity expensive enough that a burned name can't reroll.

### 502. Mayday Supply Co.
Mix: 89(coverage sold as contractual shift windows) + 202(seller-side quality discrimination in sourcing) + 258(payroll hits Friday regardless)
Pitch: Rescue operators sell breakdown coverage to hauler players as contractual shift windows, but every winch, rig, and spare that makes a save possible is sourced by RFQ from factory players who privately route good-line or night-shift output per customer — so when your gear fails mid-mayday on the open channel, you can't tell luck from bad positioning from a supplier who quietly demoted you, and crew payroll clears Friday whether or not your claims did. Rejection lives in RFQs that go read-then-silent and in subscribers who churn to a rival without a word; the multiplayer is a full food web of operators, subscriber crews, factories, and subcontracted newcomer tugs. Minute 1 you're a subscribed crew watching a live mayday resolve as sector legend; month 3 you're underwriting an overcommitted coverage book while managing supplier trust that decays the moment you rely on it. Stronger than either parent because Maydays' unexplained failures gain a learnable human cause (the supply chain) and Golden Sample's crate-crack reveal gains life-or-death stakes on an open channel.
Guards: Shift-window contracts amputate on-call lifestyle hostility, per-human supplier discrimination is opacity that regenerates instead of wikifying, subcontracting is the built-in newcomer on-ramp at any density, and staged-breakdown fraud is the underwriting curriculum rather than a break.

### 503. The Monday Port
Mix: 107(weekly wipe with knowledge-only persistence) + 75(documentation as tradeable, forgeable commodity) + 45(information as decaying, poisonable resource)
Pitch: Every Monday a fresh port spawns with drifted, undocumented harbor decrees; the world wipes weekly but your handwritten pilot book survives, and pilot books are tradeable, forgeable goods — so the truth about this week's rules is itself a market with adulterated supply, and the intel you bought may be salted with one beautiful lie by the seller's rival. Fines and seizures always arrive as scenes with partial tells (the inspector's glance at your flag), so punishment feeds the detective loop instead of feeling like a mugging. Minute 1: everyone lands equal on a fresh server and the first cracked unwritten rule is an Outer Wilds rush; month 3: your book imprint carries brand reputation, forgers counterfeit your covers, and the museum of legendary runs preserves your infamy across wipes. Stronger than either parent because the weekly rotation permanently solves the Port's wiki problem, while the book market gives Sevenday's notebook an economy — your knowledge is literally the capital.
Guards: Scheduled rotation plus legally salted books make wiki death structurally impossible, the wipe means a missed week costs nothing (lifestyle-proof, perfect cold start), and the only persistent asset is authored knowledge — the cleanest anti-attribute-grind in the pool.

### 504. Actuary Row
Mix: 250(public graveyard as the only actuarial table) + 56(adverse-selection underwriting with live reinsurance) + 46(one-photo-one-lie salvage auctions)
Pitch: Players run ventures, other players underwrite them, and the only actuarial data anywhere is the community-maintained public graveyard of post-mortems — which cartels can and will poison with staged deaths; when a venture folds, its carcass hits an As-Is auction where due diligence means bidding against the deceased's one self-reported number. Adverse selection is the renewable uncertainty: the founders most eager for your policy know something you don't, and your quote being silently shopped is the rejection channel. Minute 1: browse the graveyard like true crime and bid on a carcass; month 3: your initials are on slips across the server, you hedge open risk in a live reinsurance market instead of spectating, and a well-timed founder exit lands as a champagne jackpot that feeds claims and auctions. Stronger than the parents because every business death now feeds three interlocking games — post-mortem, claim, salvage — so failure is content with catharsis instead of a funeral.
Guards: The hidden state is living players' intentions (unwikiable), reinsurance gives the underwriter verbs between events (retention), everything is async auction-and-claim play (lifestyle/density), and policies priced off permanent venture lineage make identity laundering ruinously expensive.

### 505. Postage Due

### 506. Spec Work

### 507. Vouchsafe

### 508. Both Sides of the Table
Mix: 3(ghosting as emergent optimal play of a real counterparty population) + 329(runway-priced meetings + zombie funds) + 307(cohort as sharding-plus-cold-start unit)
Pitch: A fundraising MMO played in cohorts of twenty where every player holds both a founder deck and a checkbook; some checkbooks are secretly zombie funds that keep taking meetings they can never fund, and every meeting burns literal runway days — so qualifying who can actually pay is the core skill, and ghosting is never scripted, just the emergent optimal play of real investors managing scarce attention. Weekly parallel pitch hands mean a bad read costs you a hand, not a month, and Demo Day is the cohort's communal reckoning with a mid-batch pivot-week wildcard for public redemption arcs. Minute 1: your first meeting is with a real human whose money may not exist; month 3: your batch alumni are now the investors you must read — and having been a known zombie follows you to the other chair. Stronger than the parents because cohorts solve Ghost Round's empty-room density problem by definition, while both-seats-per-player gives Zombie Fund's corpse-detection a karmic loop that makes ghosting socially priced.
Guards: Cohorts of twenty shard identically at 400 or 10,000 and solve cold start by definition, both-seats kills role asymmetry, zombie status is live player state no wiki can capture, and runway-priced meetings turn silence from dead waiting into a cost you chose — with weekly cadence as the retention heartbeat.

### 509. Bureau of Unwritten Rules

### 510. Soft Yes
