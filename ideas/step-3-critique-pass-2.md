# Step 3 — Critique Pass 2: Is It Actually Fun?

Second critical iteration over all 500 ideas, building on pass 1 (TEACH). Player's-advocate lens: moment-to-moment verb, emotional payoff arc (does the pain convert to anticipation/comedy/war stories or just misery), session shape, social fun, month-3 retention. Each entry: Hook (best moment), Poison (biggest fun-killer), Fix (one change that improves fun without killing the lesson). Scored /10.

### 1. Reply-All Ghosts — FUN: 4/10
Hook: Getting scammed on a stale lead list by another player and dragging the seller through the forum is the lemon-market drama people will actually retell.
Poison: The core verb is writing cold emails into a void — literally the soul-crushing job people play games to escape, with silence as the modal reward and no catharsis valve.
Fix: Make replies rare jackpot events that open into playable live negotiation threads, so every send becomes a lottery ticket with a visible, thrilling payout state instead of a pixel that maybe fires.

### 2. The Dashboard Lies — FUN: 3/10
Hook: Publicly catching a rival inflating their benchmark numbers and watching their reputation crater is the one moment with teeth.
Poison: Moment-to-moment play is staring at noisy charts and moving budget sliders — a game that is indistinguishable from the worst hour of an analyst's workday, in minute 1 and hour 100 alike.
Fix: Make instrumentation diegetic and playable — customer interviews, spy visits, support-call eavesdropping as short scenes — so gathering signal is an act you perform, not a line item you buy.

### 3. Ghost Round — FUN: 7/10
Hook: The whisper network — cracking a fund's secret thesis from demo-day crumbs and sniping the round before your rivals connect the dots — is detective-plus-gossip gold.
Poison: Founder-side fun is hostage to a healthy, engaged VC-player population; if that side thins or trolls, weeks of pitching become unpaid theater performed for an empty room.
Fix: Let every player simultaneously run a founder and a small angel checkbook, so nobody is ever purely waiting on the other population and everyone feels both sides of the ghosting.

### 4. Runway — FUN: 8/10
Hook: Losing the final partner meeting with three days of runway left because the boss pattern-matched your deck to last month's dead meta is an instantly retellable roguelike death.
Poison: Once the community maps the cycle, it risks becoming just another solved deck-builder with startup art, where the pitch flavor stops mattering and runs feel like grinding the meta tracker.
Fix: Let real investor-players occasionally guest-pilot boss seats in high-stakes runs (with their own scoring incentive), injecting an unwikiable human table-read into the run structure.

### 5. Cap Table — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The post-signing reveal that your opponent's fund was desperate and you left 2x on the table — or that you fleeced them — is a real gasp-or-groan beat.
Poison: Clause-by-clause legalese is homework-flavored, and the audience that finds liquidation preference inherently thrilling is measured in thousands, not the tens of thousands a ladder needs.
Fix: End every match with a fast poker-style showdown replay of both hidden hands and bluffs, so the emotional payoff is automatic, visual, and clippable rather than buried in a term sheet.

### 6. Churnyard — FUN: 4/10
Hook: The day a major guild adopts your tool and your MRR curve goes vertical on a graph other players can see is a genuine screenshot moment.
Poison: Silent churn with no confrontation and no explanation is misery without catharsis — the design takes the single worst feeling in SaaS and makes it the entire emotional diet.
Fix: Add rare overheard "exit interview" moments — churned customers occasionally surface in shared spaces muttering why they left — converting the void into detective breadcrumbs and dark comedy.

### 7. API Wars — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Shipping a breaking change and watching the whole dependent economy convulse — or being the whale-dropped victim the forum spends a week theorizing about — is EVE-grade drama.
Poison: The minute-to-minute is watching usage graphs, and late joiners arrive into an ossified political map where the only move is supplicating incumbents, which is fun for exactly nobody new.
Fix: Run seasonal protocol eras that periodically scramble the dependency graph, keeping platform politics liquid and giving newcomers a recurring fresh land-rush to surf.

### 8. Cold Start — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Watching a stubbornly dead neighborhood finally ignite on the city map after weeks of subsidy seeding is a slow-burn payoff worth the screenshot.
Poison: Most experiments are cash burned into silent streets — a slot machine that usually pays nothing and never explains — and having your supply side poached can feel like an unrecoverable mugging.
Fix: Give poaching a counterplay window (loyalty plays, win-back offers, public raids) so supply wars become visible rivalry arcs with comeback stories instead of quiet theft.

### 9. Standup — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Sharing your "Day 47: died to a payroll miss" grid and discovering your friend's ghost company survived the identical market by one different Tuesday call is perfect argument fuel.
Poison: One menu-pick per day risks feeling like a horoscope — too little authorship for deaths to feel earned, and foggy attribution reads as arbitrary when your agency was a single click.
Fix: Make the daily move a composed play — allocate three chips across fronts plus a one-line rationale that resurfaces verbatim in your postmortem — so the death grid tells a story you wrote.

### 10. Post-Mortem — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Death as a loot drop — the server watching your company die and your verified fact get inscribed in the codex with your name on it — makes losing the best moment in the game.
Poison: The actual run between deaths is an undefined vanilla startup sim, so as pitched this is a brilliant meta-layer wrapped around a hollow core verb.
Fix: Let players stake reputation betting on which codex entries still hold, so each economy drift becomes a server-wide market crash of received wisdom with public winners and losers.

### 11. The Feed — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Your guild's forbidden growth hack printing money for exactly nine days before the server piles in and kills it is a repeatable gold-rush story with built-in dark comedy.
Poison: For the unlucky majority the loop is shipping variants into an indifferent slot machine, and rank-twitch watching trains compulsive checking rather than play.
Fix: Broadcast retraining events as visible earthquakes — a shared land-grab season with leaderboards — so algorithm shifts are communal spectacles instead of private, unexplained dips.

### 12. Voicemail — FUN: 7/10
Hook: The clip of you sweet-talking past a hostile LLM receptionist in your best founder voice is shareable comedy the community will generate endlessly.
Poison: Three real weeks of calls to learn your champion never had budget is a punchline with a fatal setup length, and voice-only play excludes commutes, offices, and anyone with roommates.
Fix: Make every call drop intel fragments for the shared org-chart map regardless of outcome, so even voicemail purgatory pays a small collectible dividend and no session ends at zero.

### 13. Burn — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Split bank authority in co-op — two cofounders screaming over which invoice to pay as the payroll monster's midnight approaches — is Overcooked with money and friendships at stake.
Poison: Explicitly unlearnable revenue RNG means deaths feel dealt rather than earned, and horror with no mastery curve curdles into resentment by hour ten.
Fix: Give each client discoverable payment tells so veterans learn to read the dark — the dread stays, but survival becomes a skill you can brag about instead of a dice roll.

### 14. Term Sheet Poker — FUN: 8/10
Hook: The showdown flip revealing someone's traction cards were pure vapor after you funded them is a betrayal your group will relitigate over drinks for weeks.
Poison: It needs four assembled, startup-literate friends on voice — a brutal population precondition — and with matchmade strangers the trust drama that carries the game evaporates.
Fix: Ship a ten-minute quick-hand mode with persistent cross-hand reputation ladders in public lobbies, so strangers accumulate the betrayal history the fun depends on.

### 15. Fake Door — FUN: 5/10
Hook: Flipping a validated concept on the exchange at peak hype hours before the signal evaporates is a legal pump-and-dump with a straight face — pure trader adrenaline.
Poison: The maker loop is A/B-testing landing pages — a job, not a verb — and the dread mechanic rug-pulls diligent sellers without explanation, punishing exactly the players who engaged deepest.
Fix: Lean all the way into the exchange — public price charts, short-selling rivals' concepts, seller reputation ratings — so market drama and lemon-hunting carry sessions.

### 16. Null Result — FUN: 3/10
Hook: The eureka of a staggered-launch control cleanly proving "wrong audience, not bad product" is real detective catharsis — for the first solver.
Poison: A game whose primary feedback is literally nothing happening is anti-juice, and the pitch celebrates its own death: 90% of players will run a published route and feel neither mystery nor payoff.
Fix: Procedurally reroll every player's market instance so published designs become techniques to adapt rather than answers to copy, keeping the detective work first-hand for everyone.

### 17. Warm Intro — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Becoming a kingmaker node whose intro chains are courted — or burning the founder who flamed out with your name attached — is court-intrigue gossip fuel of the highest grade.
Poison: New players start locked out of the game's only verb, and by month three the fun belongs to a dozen oligarchs while everyone else experiences a velvet rope simulator.
Fix: Seed a constant stream of small deals that only pay when routed through low-connection players, forcing incumbents to compete at recruiting the bottom of the graph.

### 18. Ticket Queue — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The Papers-Please rush of spotting the one real signal ticket in a flood of noise — then learning a rival planted the decoys you fell for — is paranoia done right.
Poison: Reading fake support tickets for a hundred hours is data-entry cosplay unless the writing is consistently brilliant, and a purely reactive player never gets a power-fantasy beat.
Fix: Let deductions become visible moves — publicly accuse a rival of sabotage or ship a mid-crisis fix and watch the queue's tone shift within the same session — so inference closes into action fast.

### 19. Soft Landing — FUN: 4/10
Hook: Walking a doomed company to a fire-sale that somehow nets 2x, then telling the story of the landmine you found in the books, is a genuinely novel triumph fantasy.
Poison: Counterparties going dark for real weeks mid-diligence parks the player in an unplayed waiting room at precisely the moment tension should peak — the pacing is a retention guillotine.
Fix: Run three exit tracks in parallel on staggered rhythms so some table is always warm, and silence on one track becomes readable strategic information instead of dead air.

### 20. Replumb — FUN: 8/10
Hook: The mid-pivot crisis — half your machine dismantled, cash pressure falling, revenue still dripping through old pipes you haven't dared cut — is the best tension screenshot in the batch.
Poison: Fluid demand behind crude gauges frustrates the exact optimizer audience Factorio verbs attract; they came to solve, and the game deliberately withholds the numbers.
Fix: Make measurement itself buildable — better gauges, tracer dyes, A/B valves as machine subsystems — so epistemic uncertainty becomes more factory to engineer rather than fog imposed from outside.

### 21. Cofounder — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Discovering your cofounder took the secret solo acquisition on day 23 produces the single best water-cooler betrayal stories in the entire pool.
Poison: The modal failure is a partner silently quitting, sentencing you to weeks of solo-running a two-person game — abandonment as a game state is simply unplayable dead time.
Fix: Make abandonment a playable phase — vesting clawbacks, emergency recruitment from a pool of fellow abandoned founders, and a "survivor arc" scoreboard — so betrayal opens a chapter instead of ending the book.

### 22. Compliance — FUN: 5/10
Hook: The snitch button — weaponizing a rival's non-compliance and watching the regulator descend on them — is exquisite schadenfreude the forum will never stop litigating.
Poison: Ninety real days of silence on your own filing is retention suicide, and silently patched rules make the community's hard-won archaeology feel like the DM cheating.
Fix: Compress regulatory time to a quarter per real week and leak cryptic case-status breadcrumbs, so waiting reads as a ticking mystery to decode instead of a void to endure.

### 23. Out of Office — FUN: 4/10
Hook: A simulated investor reply landing on your real lock screen at 11pm is the most viscerally real notification any game has ever sent — screenshot guaranteed.
Poison: A game that punishes you for being at dinner is an anxiety subscription; it gets uninstalled the first time it costs the player a real human moment, which is week two at the latest.
Fix: Replace hard reply-windows with a player-scheduled daily "office hours" block where queued crises resolve in one juicy ten-minute triage session — ambient world, bounded obligation.

### 24. Board Deck — FUN: 3/10
Hook: Sweating through your framed metrics in front of real investor-players who might smell the spin is legitimate performance-drama — once a month.
Poison: One gameplay beat per month while the sim runs itself makes players spectators to their own company 29 days out of 30 — a cadence no live game has ever survived.
Fix: Inject weekly micro-fires — a leaked metric, a director's off-cycle question — that force interim spin decisions, so narrative debt is something you feel compounding between meetings.

### 25. Fork Threat — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The fork war — a rival forks your repo, contributors publicly pick sides, and the whole server watches two download graphs race — is a spectacular communal drama.
Poison: Between crises the loop is issue triage and mood divination, which gamifies the exact unpaid maintainer chores the target audience already resents doing in real life.
Fix: Make contributors named, courtable, poachable characters so the between-crisis game is roster-building politics with faces and grudges rather than inbox weeding.

### 26. Dead Corner — FUN: 7/10
Hook: The scratch-card reveal of your first week's till after signing, played out on a street lined with the ghost signage of everyone who died there before you — instant gambling thrill plus gallows-humor screenshot material.
Poison: A bad corner as pitched is a months-long drip of slow losses you can't exit, converting the gamble's rush into sunk-cost misery with no catharsis and no next hand to play.
Fix: Shorten leases and add a break-clause-with-penalty so failure resolves in weeks, and let dying owners author their own ghost-sign epitaph — the graveyard becomes a shared comedy archive and the loop re-deals fast.

### 27. Form 27-B — FUN: 7/10
Hook: The player-clerk you buttered up last week grinning as they stamp your rival's rejection — Papers, Please dark comedy with real humans behind every counter, endlessly retellable.
Poison: The whole game is hostage to clerk population and mood: log in off-peak and you're queueing at empty counters, which is bureaucratic waiting without even the bureaucrat.
Fix: Package each permit push as a 30–45 minute "permit run" dungeon session with NPC understudy clerks (visibly grumpier than humans) filling empty counters, so the crawl always has a session shape and a face to charm.

### 28. Fade — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Overhearing your own customers gossip about you in your waiting-room chat mid-cut — a social frisson no single-player sim can fake, plus a genuinely tactile haircut verb that's fun in minute one.
Poison: The dead Tuesday is literal dead air — you're standing in an empty virtual shop with nothing to do but refresh, and the design's truest lesson doubles as its emptiest hour.
Fix: Give silence a verb — practice cuts on mannequins that mint shareable style cards, or step out and become a paying customer at another player's shop — so slow days build craft and street presence instead of boredom.

### 29. Quarters — FUN: 5/10
Hook: Rent day itself — physically raking mountains of quarters into bags under deadline is a tactile, ASMR-grade payoff that makes cash flow feel like treasure.
Poison: The pitch's proudly unlearnable breakdowns and unexplained demand swings turn the other 90% of play into a maintenance chore treadmill with no story and no mastery curve.
Fix: Give every jam and swing a discoverable forensic cause (gum in the coin slot, the motel upstairs changed linen day) so maintenance becomes small detective fiction rather than RNG janitor work.

### 30. The Landlord's Table — FUN: 5/10
Hook: The landlord's table itself is deliciously wicked — fanning five sealed offers, letting them all expire, and reading the forum meltdown you caused — power-fantasy drama people will absolutely retell.
Poison: Structurally, one player gets the poker game and eleven get the waiting room; the tenant majority's core experience is being ignored with no business underneath to play while they wait.
Fix: Let tenants run pop-up carts and short-term stalls during the courtship, so every unanswered proposal has a playable "meanwhile" — silence becomes a backdrop to hustle instead of the whole screen.

### 31. Footfall Futures — FUN: 4/10
Hook: Unmasking a vanished broker's rebrand and posting the receipts to the forum — con-artist-versus-detective drama that writes its own community soap opera.
Poison: The payoff of every interesting decision (was the report a lie?) lands a real-time year later, and the rational endgame is nobody buying data at all — a dead market simulator with geological feedback.
Fix: Compress leases to two-week terms with broker track records auto-published on expiry, so the con/expose/revenge cycle spins weekly instead of annually and the lemons market stays theatrically alive.

### 32. Ghosted Gym — FUN: 4/10
Hook: The leagues trading cursed churn theories ("it was the new soap, I'm telling you") — communal folklore comedy born from shared statistical suffering.
Poison: The moment-to-moment verb is staring at retention curves that refuse to answer, a gym with no bodies in it — dashboard homework wearing a tank top.
Fix: Render members as visible characters whose visits, routines, and body language you can watch (behavior observable, reasons never), so every experiment produces watchable micro-scenes instead of another line wiggle.

### 33. Bench Fee — FUN: 8/10
Hook: Quoting blind, cracking the case open, and discovering the devious trap a rival player built for you — every box is a gamble, a puzzle, and a war story for both sides of the bench.
Poison: Your best content (the actual repair) is gated behind winning the quote, so an overquote means the customer walks silently AND you never get to touch the toy — double denial.
Fix: Walked-away boxes resurface days later on a rival's public bench for you to spectate — you still lost the job without explanation, but you get schadenfreude, closure, and calibration as consolation prizes.

### 34. Ninety Days — FUN: 8/10
Hook: Death-by-obscure-ordinance screenshots ("fined out of existence over sandwich-board font size") fueling a frantic community race to reverse-engineer this week's hidden city — Spelunky daily meets Outer Wilds.
Poison: By Wednesday the Discord has solved the seed, so late-week runs are following a guide — the community that makes it fun also spoils it on a fixed schedule.
Fix: Salt each player's seed — shared city skeleton, personally re-rolled ordinance details — so community folklore raises your odds but can never fully solve your run.

### 35. Endcap — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Gambling your one endcap on a weird product a player-supplier cold-pitched you, then watching it become a street-wide hit — kingmaker joy with a real human on the other end of the win.
Poison: The register's total silence on why products die makes shelf tinkering feel like feeding a slot machine with hidden odds — experimentation without texture curdles into guessing.
Fix: Show customers physically picking items up and putting them back (behavior visible, motive never), so shelf experiments have watchable, argue-about-able texture while attribution stays honestly ambiguous.

### 36. Hold Music — FUN: 3/10
Hook: The heart-stop of a real callback landing three days later while you're on the bus — a genuinely novel jolt people would tell friends about exactly once.
Poison: The core verb is waiting on hold in real time and being punished for having a life when the callback comes — a novelty about waiting, not a game, uninstalled by week three.
Fix: Convert live callbacks into phone-tag — missed calls leave voicemails you can return within a window, and hold queues carry eavesdroppable crossed lines — making the telephone dance itself the playable game.

### 37. The Strip — FUN: 7/10
Hook: The street's cold war over the vacant unit — whisper campaigns, bloc votes, and passive-aggressive window signage — twelve-player guild drama that generates screenshots and grudges for seasons.
Poison: Your fun is hostage to eleven strangers' login habits; a street of absentee neighbors is a dead ecosystem that kills your shop through no decision you made.
Fix: Dead tenancies get absorbed by a lobbyable NPC "management company" and thin streets merge dynamically, so the political core stays hot at any population level.

### 38. Inspection Day — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The folklore economy — contradictory survival lore about Inspector Grady traded like fishing tips, then the table-slap moment when your pet theory survives an audit.
Poison: A troll inspector citing everyone for nothing turns designed opacity into plain abuse with no recourse — the exact flavor of unfairness that makes players quit loudly.
Fix: Add a theatrical appeals board that overturns citations without ever explaining the rules — inspectors who get overturned bleed standing, so griefers burn out while the fog stays intact.

### 39. Change of Hands — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Monday's ledger handoff with a cryptic margin note — "DO NOT reorder from Sal" — a weekly mystery-box episode with a live studio audience of gossiping past owners.
Poison: Watching your lovingly rebuilt week get strip-mined by your successor teaches emotional detachment fast, and detached players are lapsed players.
Fix: Pay past owners a trailing slice of the shop's future performance, so stewardship compounds, sabotage costs you, and the alumni chat has real money riding on its gossip.

### 40. Till Tape — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Catching a skimming clerk purely from a rhythm anomaly in the receipt stream and firing them by handwritten note — Return of the Obra Dinn for forensic-accounting romantics.
Poison: For everyone who isn't that romantic, it's scrolling numbers with no faces — homework aesthetics that will bore the mainstream out by hour two.
Fix: Let the tape carry human residue — staff scribble sarcastic replies in the margins, item combos imply little customer stories — so the numbers read as epistolary fiction rather than CSV.

### 41. Variance — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Packing Zoning Board Night with your supporters and watching a rival's neon-sign petition die in three minutes of stammering — live courtroom theater that community folklore is made of.
Poison: One scheduled live event excludes whole time zones, and the shop game between hearings is unspecified filler — a great party with nothing between parties.
Fix: Run multiple nightly sittings across time zones and archive proceedings as public case history, so missed hearings become binge-able jurisprudence gossip instead of missed content.

### 42. The Playbook — FUN: 4/10
Hook: A chain's public collapse — royalties imploding, franchisees storming the forum, the franchisor's manual dissected line by line — schadenfreude spectacle of the highest order.
Poison: The daily verb is following someone else's homework for months to learn whether it was garbage — compliance-as-gameplay, with the fireworks arriving twice a year at most.
Fix: Compress seasons so a playbook proves out in two to three weeks, and stage live "franchise summits" where compliance disputes are aired as theater — keep the trust lesson, multiply the blowups.

### 43. Open Up — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The morning the scaffolding you clocked last Tuesday finally chews your till — vindicated paranoia, the told-you-so screenshot, and a perfect five-minute coffee-break ritual around it.
Poison: Noticing is the only verb; once the ritual is muscle memory you're an unarmed spectator to consequences that land a week later, and autopilot rituals quietly stop being opened.
Fix: Let players act on what they notice — drag the sandwich board, chalk a counter-sign, warn the neighbor — small same-morning moves that make observation agentive instead of ornamental.

### 44. Store Hours — FUN: 3/10
Hook: The street collectively discovering Sunday morning is gold and quietly colluding on opening rhythms — an emergent norm players genuinely brag about having set.
Poison: Miss-one-and-gone push notifications turn the game into a guilt-generating second job — the archetypal design that gets uninstalled in self-defense, not churned from boredom.
Fix: Replace live-answer with a grace window where walk-ins browse briefly and leave only a silhouette if you never show — absence stings as mystery and lost revenue, not as a pager going off at dinner.

### 45. Word on the Street — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Discovering the intel you paid three favors for was planted by your rival — and spending the next week engineering the counter-plant — spy-thriller social play that generates legends.
Poison: The month-one experience is drowning with no tooltips in a game about a business the pitch never specifies, and getting burned by poisoned info early teaches distrust of the game itself.
Fix: Make the underlying shop sim tactile and self-evident (only the market layer is undocumented), so newcomers can honestly play shopkeeper on day one while the sharks trade secrets above them.

### 46. As-Is — FUN: 8/10
Hook: The reveal — keys in the door of the business you won on one photo and one probable lie, finding either the loyal-regulars ledger or the mold — Storage Wars with grudges, weekly.
Poison: Winning a true lemon as pitched sentences you to a season of playing your punishment, which turns the auction's rush into a long, resentful cleanup shift.
Fix: Allow fast flips and quick liquidation of lemons within days — you bank the loss, the grudge, and the war story, then get back to the auction floor where the game actually lives.

### 47. The Regulars — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Rescuing a drifting regular because you noticed their order changed two weeks ago — the warm, detective-empathy high of genuinely reading a person, plus righteous fury when a rival poaches them.
Poison: The silent cascade punishes a missed week of play with half your customer base — an avalanche penalty for having a life, which converts affection for your twelve into dread.
Fix: Slow all drift while you're offline and require active rival courtship to trigger cascades, so every loss traces to dramatic events and enemies rather than your calendar.

### 48. Across the Street — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Staging your dumpster with empty champagne crates, then learning your rival copied your fake strategy and starved — poker with storefronts, and the bluff stories write themselves.
Poison: A week-long 1v1 against an outmatched or AFK opponent is seven days of slow-motion nothing — lopsided or abandoned matches are the format's death.
Fix: Cut matches to 48–72 hours in a best-of series with AFK forfeits awarding the street, so bluffs resolve while they're still funny and every rivalry gets a rematch arc.

### 49. Small Fires — FUN: 7/10
Hook: The neighbor calling in their favor at your literal worst moment — a betrayal-comedy beat with a real human author that everyone in the favor web retells for weeks.
Poison: Unrelenting overload with compounding punishment and no wins to savor reads as an anxiety simulator — players don't churn from it, they flee it.
Fix: Give the chaos a rhythm — quiet days between fire days, plus rare "good fires" (a surprise crowd, a windfall pallet) — so triage skill sometimes pays out in joy instead of only lesser ruin.

### 50. Curb Appeal — FUN: 5/10
Hook: Your window display going street-famous as neighboring frontages escalate into a full visual arms race — expressive decoration play with an audience, endlessly screenshotable.
Poison: All that creative effort vanishes into a conversion number against an invisible denominator — no faces, no reasons, no denominator means the artist's reward loop starves.
Fix: Let a converted customer occasionally leave one unprompted line ("stopped for the neon cat") — rare, partial, human feedback that feeds the artist without ever revealing the funnel.

### 51. Dead Letters — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The daily mail-check ritual — Animal Crossing mailbox anticipation where any envelope might be a fortune, an insult, or proof your correspondent was human all along.
Poison: By mid-game you are managing thirty capital-burning threads of dead silence, and refreshing an empty inbox is not a verb.
Fix: Give every player a lively home port — gossiping dockhands, arriving strangers, harbor scenes — so the hours between letters have a playable foreground instead of a refresh button.

### 52. The Incanto — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The seasonal bid-reveal is winner's-curse theater — the room gasps at who overpaid — and spy-in-the-warehouse capers generate retellable capers all season.
Poison: Losing the auction empties your entire season, and losers are most players most seasons.
Fix: Add a spot-charter and subcontracting market where losing bidders lease hold space from winners, so a lost bid reshapes your season instead of deleting it.

### 53. Kontor — FUN: 5/10
Hook: Blackball day as communal drama, followed by the whodunnit of theorizing which veteran you offended and testing the theory with next season's gifts.
Poison: Repeated anonymous rejection by real people is personal humiliation rather than narrative pain, and hazed newcomers uninstall instead of iterating.
Fix: Put several rival kontors in every port so a blackball reroutes your ambition into a rivalry storyline instead of parking you outside the only door.

### 54. The Relay — FUN: 4/10
Hook: The paranoid intimacy of two real trading partners at your oasis — reading crate prices like tea leaves and feeling the chill the week one stops appearing.
Poison: Your fun is hostage to population density and to outcomes three handoffs away you never witness, so most sessions are haggling into fog with no payoff scene.
Fix: Have caravans return carrying stories — letters, news, souvenirs from down-chain — so the far end of the chain becomes visible, gossipable narrative even when the profits are not.

### 55. Ledger of Little Falls — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Settling day — the yearly reveal that grades every gut call at once, cozy-brutal like Stardew crossed with a credit bureau, plus catching a rival's falsified blacklist.
Poison: One fireworks moment a year is a long drought, and the between-time store loop risks becoming going-through-the-motions shopkeeping.
Fix: Add quarterly harvest-festival mini-settlings and traveling strangers whose gossip updates your priors, so judgment gets exercised and tested every session, not annually.

### 56. Underwriters' Row — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The Lloyd's bell moment — the whole coffeehouse goes silent as a ship is posted lost, and everyone checks whose initials are on the slip.
Poison: After you initial, you are a spectator for weeks with zero agency; sweat without verbs is dashboard purgatory.
Fix: Add a live reinsurance market so open risk can be hedged, offloaded, or doubled mid-voyage, turning the wait itself into playable poker.

### 57. The Rumor Bourse — FUN: 6/10
Hook: Breaking the wax seal on a dossier you bid a fortune for — a loot-box thrill where the loot might be gold, a week-old nothing, or a rival's planted lie you later avenge.
Poison: Rational players learn the market is lemons and simply stop buying, killing the game at the exact moment everyone plays it correctly.
Fix: Date-stamp resolution so every rumor eventually settles publicly true or false — reports become bets that pay off instead of goods that only rot.

### 58. Dead Reckoning — FUN: 7/10
Hook: The landfall reveal — fog lifts after a week of star fixes and you learn whether your careful arithmetic found the rich port or a lee shore, screenshot-worthy every run.
Poison: It is a niche sim verb, and dying of thirst off the wrong coast a fifth time reads as failed homework, not adventure.
Fix: Port taverns where crews compare and poison each other's logs over drinks, making navigation knowledge social currency and every wreck a told story.

### 59. The Revenue Men — FUN: 7/10
Hook: Cracking the one hold the smuggler prayed you would skip — a table-slam bluff-call payoff every ten minutes, with regulars developing server-famous tells.
Poison: Disposable rounds with no persistence, and a matchmaking lull means port calls against nobody.
Fix: Persistent careers — smuggler fortunes and officer commendations compounding across shifts — so ten-minute matches accrete into known rivalries and grudge matches.

### 60. Hawala — FUN: 5/10

Poison: A thinning herd is slow starvation you can hear, a death spiral with hooves, and rustler PvP tips from drama into unrecoverable griefing of the already-poor.
Fix: Make trail drives hire-able co-op jobs where broke players ride guard for wages, converting the loss spiral into employment and every drive into a shared multiplayer story.

### 489. Wire Deals — FUN: 5/10
Hook: "I think I've traded with this one before" — recognizing a counterpart's prose style across months of anonymous text is a proud detective moment.
Poison: The fog never resolves — no showdown, no unmasking, no confirmation ever — so it is poker with no river card forever, and bots quietly dissolve the one pleasure (reading humans) into noise.
Fix: Auction one rare "unmask token" per season — a single confirmed identity reveal the whole wire gossips about — creating a jackpot of resolution without breaking baseline anonymity.

### 490. The Lying Manual — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The paranoid 2 a.m. re-read after your first collapse — realizing page 47 killed you — and the annotated-edition holy wars when the estate ships a re-poisoned revision.
Poison: Moment-to-moment you are following a book into a sim the pitch never describes; if that sim is thin, the manual is a quiz answer key and the fun is a book club, not a game.
Fix: Give the guru a live in-world presence — seminars, fresh pamphlets, indignant rebuttals to player accusations — so the fraud is an ongoing antagonist you can heckle, not a static PDF.

### 491. Strata — FUN: 5/10
Hook: Cracking open a deep stratum of rich ghost-demand feels like striking an archaeological motherlode, and learning you are already someone's fossil is a poster-grade chill.
Poison: Your customers are recordings — playing against replays is playing alone — and the live surface market where the actual fun should happen is the hand-waved half of the pitch.
Fix: Make excavation physical and contested — shared dig sites, claim-jumping, strata that collapse if over-mined — so archaeology is a competitive multiplayer verb, not a database query.

### 492. Burn to Speak — FUN: 6/10
Hook: The town-square bonfire is pure theater — a con artist's towering burn on garbage stock, the crowd's gasp, and the season's legendary "he burned his own warehouse" screenshot.
Poison: The poor are mechanically mute — the newcomer experience is watching rich players converse in fire while you can afford to say nothing, misery with zero counterplay.
Fix: Add non-monetary burns for newcomers — publicly torching a week of your own visible labor — so the broke have a costly-signal vocabulary and the theater gains an underdog arc.

### 493. Anecdata — FUN: 7/10

Poison: The disciplined correct play is often to ignore your only mechanic, and never being able to confirm whether yesterday's fury was an outlier makes hour-100 feel like being gaslit by your own game.
Fix: Let players trade, gift, and counterfeit anecdotes in a folk-census marketplace with visible provenance chains, turning the N=1 curse into a social information game.

### 494. Eminent Domain — FUN: 7/10

### 495. Control Group — FUN: 5/10
Hook: "I am currently losing to a frozen copy of myself that does nothing" is the funniest self-own dashboard in the pool, and beating your twin is a clean daily scoreboard.
Poison: The core verb is reading a diff chart — no world, no theater, no people — an A/B-testing seminar whose own pitch admits the chart gets less trustworthy the longer you play.
Fix: Embody the twin as a visible rival storefront across the street run by your ghost, poachable customers and all, so the counterfactual is a place you walk past and resent, not a line on a graph.

### 496. The Seeded Deck — FUN: 7/10

Poison: For the victim, ruin by invisible purchasable disaster is griefing-by-catalog, and rich players can simply out-seed everyone else into permanent misery.

### 497. Honesty Box — FUN: 7/10

### 498. Séance Round — FUN: 5/10
Hook: Haunting the living after your own bankruptcy is the best loser-retention role in the pool — dead founders flexing, sandbagging, and mourning through a ouija board is genuinely funny theater.
Poison: For the living player, advice arriving one letter at a time is friction cosplaying as atmosphere — a chat window on a thirty-second-per-word delay, wholly dependent on strangers' roleplay quality.
Fix: Keep the séance staging but let ghosts speak at conversation speed with era-locked knowledge enforced by tooling (their UI shows the world frozen at their death date), so the comedy is the stale advice, not the typing delay.

### 499. Same Product, Different Day — FUN: 4/10
Hook: The season-end scatter plot is a great communal funeral — everyone's timing manifestos publicly executed by one chart — and the manifesto-writing culture is a legitimately funny meta.
Poison: A whole season of selling an identical widget with nothing to build, brand, or tinker with means the actual play is waiting to pick three dates — a roulette wheel with excellent postgame analytics.
Fix: Add continuous small timing verbs — weekly micro-launches, exits, and regional repositions with fast feedback — so the season holds a hundred timing bets instead of three and skill expression has somewhere to live.

### 500. Shopkeeper in Someone Else's Game — FUN: 9/10
Hook: An adventurer stops mid-haggle and types "wait... are you a PLAYER?" — the unmasking is the single best screenshot moment in all 500 ideas, and patch-day chaos delivers shared war stories on a schedule someone else writes.
Poison: Entirely hostage to a host MMO's population and consent — if adventurer traffic dries up or the host's wiki documents everything, you are doing spreadsheet retail in an empty theme park.
Fix: Build the host on purpose — a free adventurer game whose players are the shopkeepers' content — with unmasking as a formal high-stakes mechanic (suspicion tests, NPC certification audits) so the cat-and-mouse is systematized rather than incidental.
